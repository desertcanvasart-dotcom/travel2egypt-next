# Historical Hotels in Egypt

Egypt's historical hotels are not just old buildings.

They were born when Egyptian tourism still meant "discovery" and "wintering" and "the long Nile journey." A time when archaeologists, writers, diplomats, royalty, colonial officials, newspaper correspondents, and upper-class travellers walked the same terraces and lobbies.

For the traveller staying today, what matters is how much that history remains in the present stay. Does the building remain? Is the view still strong? Is operation at a modern level? Or is the atmosphere real but expectations for comfort need adjusting?

Choosing a historical hotel doesn't mean booking a famous inn. It means deciding whether to let a place carry memory inside the trip itself.

## Mena House

If you choose one historical hotel at [Giza](https://travel2egypt.org/guide/giza), it is Mena House.

It operates today as Marriott Mena House, [Cairo](https://travel2egypt.org/guide/cairo), right beside [the Giza plateau](https://travel2egypt.org/guide/giza/the-giza-plateau). The essence of the hotel is that the pyramids are visible from the garden and some rooms. Different in value from staying in central Cairo for convenience. In the morning, step into the garden and the pyramids are not background — they are landform in front of you.

The building's beginning traces back to a 19th-century hunting lodge of Khedive Ismail. In the 1880s it was arranged as a hotel and became a representative stay for visitors to Giza.

Mena House's history isn't only antiquity. It is tied to 20th-century politics and diplomacy. The 1943 Cairo Conference and 1970s Egypt-Israel relations conferences — the hotel was a stage for modern history too.

For the traveller, though, the strongest pull is location. To receive the pyramids not as the day's sightseeing target but as the environment of an overnight stay. Not an experience other Cairo luxury hotels offer.

Note the constraints. Staying at Giza isn't necessarily convenient for moving around Islamic Cairo, Zamalek, and the Tahrir area. Strong on the day you go deep into the pyramids and the Grand Egyptian Museum. As a base for walking Cairo as a whole, central or Nile-side hotels can be easier.

## Old Cataract

[Aswan](https://travel2egypt.org/guide/aswan)'s Old Cataract is the Egyptian hotel where the stay itself most often becomes the purpose.

It sits on a rise overlooking the Nile's First Cataract, facing [Elephantine Island](https://travel2egypt.org/guide/aswan/elephantine-island) and the western desert. Beyond the room, the terrace, the evening light, and the river's flow are part of the hotel.

Opened in 1899, it is strongly tied to the golden age of Nile travel. Known as a place that shaped the world of Agatha Christie's Death on the Nile, with stays by Winston Churchill and many famous figures. The hotel's value isn't only its guest list. It is that the beauty of Aswan as a place can be received from the most dramatic position.

It operates today as Mandarin Oriental, Old Cataract Aswan. Stays differ between the historic Palace Wing and later-added wings. With historicity weighed, confirm which wing, which view, and which room category before booking.

The Old Cataract works especially well after the density of [Luxor](https://travel2egypt.org/guide/luxor) sightseeing. Not rushing to sites each morning — time spent watching the Nile becomes the memory of the trip.

## Winter Palace

Luxor's Winter Palace is the hotel most strongly tied to the story of Egyptian archaeology.

On the east bank, close to [Luxor Temple](https://travel2egypt.org/guide/luxor/the-luxor-temple), it was a building that symbolised early-20th-century winter travel in Upper Egypt. Tied to the story of Howard Carter and the discovery of Tutankhamun's tomb, it was also a stage in Luxor where archaeologists, writers, and upper-class travellers gathered.

Note the current handling. The Winter Palace is undergoing restoration to reopen as Mandarin Oriental Winter Palace, Luxor, with reopening announced for July 2027. Travellers planning Luxor in 2026 should not plan around staying in the old Winter Palace main building.

After reopening, the hotel will likely retake its position as Luxor's most important historic luxury hotel. What to write now isn't nostalgia but reality. At present, less a hotel to stay in than a hotel waiting for its next chapter.

For a historical stay in Luxor, separate confirming current operating status, alternative hotels, and the decision between Nile-side and west-bank location.

## Le Metropole

[Alexandria](https://travel2egypt.org/guide/alexandria)'s Le Metropole is a little different from the historic luxury hotels of Cairo or Aswan.

Founded in 1902 and known today as Paradise Inn Le Metropole Hotel. Near Saad Zaghloul Square, close to the Corniche and old-town movement, it carries the atmosphere of Belle Époque Alexandria into the present.

What matters here is the character of Alexandria itself. Earlier Alexandria was a Mediterranean city of Greeks, Italians, Jews, Levantine merchants, Egyptians, British, and French overlapping. The literary Alexandria of E. M. Forster, Cavafy, and Lawrence Durrell sits on top of that urban memory.

Le Metropole is a hotel for feeling the trace of that world.

Expectations matter here too. Not a place to expect a large, fully restored palace hotel like Aswan's Old Cataract. A hotel to enjoy together with the atmosphere of a historical building, central location, and the slightly aged air of Alexandria.

For travellers who put comfort first, other options can fit. For travellers who want to touch Alexandria's lost cosmopolitan air, it stays on the list.

## The Windsor Hotel

Cairo's Windsor Hotel isn't a lavish historical hotel.

Rather, it is a place that has preserved a lot of the old colonial-era air of Downtown Cairo in raw form. Not for people seeking modern luxury in room or facility. For people who value the atmosphere of the bar and lobby, the old woodwork, and an air where time seems to have stopped, it offers something hard to find elsewhere.

The Windsor may be better thought of as a stop for feeling Cairo's urban history than as a hotel to stay in. For people interested in Downtown's old buildings, government quarter, and the trace of early-20th-century Cairo, it is an interesting place.

Don't confuse "historical" with luxury here. History exists. Comfort is a separate axis.

## How to choose a historical hotel

When choosing a historical hotel in Egypt, separating three things reduces failure.

First, the history of the building itself. Next, the current operating standard. And the position in the itinerary.

Mena House is a hotel to stay for the pyramids. Strong on the Giza and Grand Egyptian Museum day. A little inconvenient for central Cairo sightseeing.

Old Cataract is a hotel for tasting time in Aswan. Suited to a trip with margin in Aswan more than a trip that rushes [Abu Simbel](https://travel2egypt.org/guide/abu-simbel) and Philae alone.

Winter Palace will carry large meaning in Luxor after reopening. For now, it is something to check operating status for.

Le Metropole is for people who weigh Alexandria's historical atmosphere. See it as an inn to approach the city's memory rather than a perfect luxury stay.

The Windsor is for people interested in Cairo's old hotel culture. For many, going to see the bar and the building suits more than staying.

## In sum

Egypt's historical hotels are not background to sightseeing.

At Mena House the pyramids become the morning scenery. At Old Cataract the Nile itself becomes the centre of the stay. At Winter Palace the memory of Luxor archaeology layers onto the building. At Le Metropole the lost layer of Mediterranean Alexandria appears. At the Windsor the old time of Downtown Cairo remains.

History does not guarantee comfort. The older the hotel, the more current operation, refurbishment, room category, view, and location need to be confirmed.

Chosen rightly, the hotel stops being just a place to sleep.

It becomes a place inside the Egypt trip where the past survives across the night.
