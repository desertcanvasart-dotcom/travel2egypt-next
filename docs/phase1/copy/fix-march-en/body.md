March is one of the most comfortable months of the year to travel in [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt). The days are sunny, dry and mild, the evenings are cool, and the crowds of peak season have thinned. That makes it a good month for almost any kind of trip: temples and tombs, a cruise on the Nile, a few nights in the desert or a week on the Red Sea.

Below is what the weather is like region by region, what to do, what is on the calendar, and where to stay and eat, with the practical points that make a March trip run smoothly. For the wider context, see our guide to [the pyramids of Egypt](/blog/the-pyramids-of-egypt).

## The weather in March

March brings warm, pleasant weather across most of the country. The days are sunny and dry, which suits sightseeing and time outdoors. In [Cairo](/guide/cairo), temperatures range from 14°C to 24°C, comfortable enough for walking through busy markets and around historic sites without overheating.

::: keep 000000000026

[Alexandria](/private-tour-alexandria-day-tour) is cooler and windier, with temperatures between 12°C and 21°C, and makes a pleasant break on the Mediterranean coast. Further south, in [Luxor](/guide/luxor) and [Aswan](/guide/aswan), the heat is noticeable but not oppressive, at 15°C to 30°C. It is good weather for [private day tours in Luxor](/private-day-tours), taking in the temples and tombs at an unhurried pace.

The [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea) resorts, including Sharm el-Sheikh and [Hurghada](/guide/hurghada), see temperatures of 18°C to 27°C: proper beach weather, whether you are snorkelling or simply lying on the sand.

In the Western Desert, including [Siwa Oasis](/guide/siwa-oasis/tours-in-siwa-oasis), the days are warm and the nights cool, with temperatures from 12°C to 25°C. It is a good time for desert trips, before the extreme heat arrives.

With weather like this, March suits both Egypt’s historic sites and its natural landscapes. Our [Egypt weather guide](/travel-tips/egypt-weather-guide) covers the rest of the year.

## What to see and do in March

March offers something for most interests, whether that is ancient ruins, [sailing on the Nile](/7-days-luxury-dahabiya-cruise-on-the-nile) or a trip into the desert.

Start in Cairo. The Great Pyramids of Giza and the Sphinx are the obvious first stop, and with fewer people around than in peak season you can take them in properly on one of our [private day tours in Cairo](/private-day-tours). Leave time for the Egyptian Museum, where Tutankhamun’s treasures and countless other objects tell the story of the ancient civilisation.

::: keep 00000000006e

[Nile cruises](/nile-cruises) are especially pleasant in March. As you sail, the river’s landscapes and villages slide past, and most cruises stop at the key archaeological sites along the way, so the days balance rest with sightseeing. For a longer trip, consider a [Nile cruise combined with a stay in Hurghada](/14-night-nile-cruise-and-hurghada-stay). There is a particular pleasure in waking to a view of ancient temples and quiet riverbanks.

For the more adventurous, March’s cooler weather is ideal for desert safaris. The [White Desert National Park](/guide/farafra-oasis/white-desert-national-park) has its strange chalk formations, and the Black Desert its volcanic peaks. Both are good for camping, stargazing and [off-road adventure](/blog/adventure-travel-guides).

In Luxor and Aswan, the warm but manageable temperatures are right for the big sites: the Valley of the Kings, [Karnak Temple](/blog/129-reasons-to-visit-karnak-temple) and Philae Temple. These are places steeped in history, and the mild weather lets you see them without rushing.

The Red Sea resorts are made for beach time and water sports. The warm, clear water is ideal for snorkelling, diving or simply lying in the sun, and resorts such as [Sharm el-Sheikh](/guide/sharm-el-sheikh) and Hurghada combine comfortable hotels with rich marine life, which makes them popular choices in March.

Between them, these options give a March visit a mix of adventure and rest.

## Cultural events and festivals

March is a lively month in Egypt, and its events offer a view of everyday Egyptian life and a good way into local culture. A [Cairo city break](/cairo-weekend-city-break-2-nights-3-days) is an easy way to catch some of them.

Egypt’s best-known spring festival, Sham El-Nessim, is celebrated with picnics in parks and on riverbanks and plates of fesikh (salted fish). It falls on the Monday after Coptic Easter, so it usually lands in April or May rather than March.

::: keep 0000000000c9

March also falls in the season of [Coptic](/blog/the-copts-of-egypt-guardians-of-an-ancient-faith) Lent, a period of fasting and reflection for Egypt’s Christian community. It involves particular dietary practices and offers an insight into the country’s different religious traditions.

For music and the arts, Cairo and Alexandria host orchestral performances, concerts, exhibitions and theatre productions, a counterpoint to the country’s historic and natural sights.


Events like these do more than entertain: they give a clearer picture of Egypt’s traditions and its modern life.

## Travel tips for March

A trip to Egypt in March is straightforward, but a few practical points will make it smoother.

**What to pack:** The weather is generally mild, but bring layers. Light clothes are comfortable during the day, while mornings and evenings can be cool, especially in the [desert regions](/blog/the-western-desert-in-egypt). A light jacket or jumper covers the cooler hours. Don’t forget sunscreen, sunglasses and a hat for the daytime sun.

**Getting around:** Moving around efficiently makes a real difference. Cairo has a good metro network and plenty of taxis, but hiring a car can be more convenient if you plan to travel to remoter areas such as the Western Desert or the Sinai Peninsula. Between cities, domestic flights or the overnight sleeper trains make the most of your sightseeing time.

[**Health and safety**](/travel-tips/health-and-safety)**:** Stay hydrated. Even in cooler weather the sun can be strong, so carry a bottle of water and reapply sunscreen regularly. It is also sensible to have [travel insurance](/travel-tips/travel-insurance) and to know where the nearest medical facilities are.

**Early starts:** Setting off at sunrise is a good way to avoid the crowds and get the best light for photographs. Early-morning visits to major sites such as the [Pyramids of Giza](/private-tour-pyramids-of-giza-sphinx-memphis-and-saqqara), Karnak Temple or the Valley of the Kings are more pleasant and less busy.

**Local etiquette:** Understand and respect local customs. When visiting religious sites, dress modestly, with shoulders and knees covered. A few words of Arabic, such as “shukran” (thank you) and “marhaba” (hello), go a long way with the people you meet.

**Eating well:** Egyptian food is varied and good. Try local dishes such as koshari, a filling mix of rice, pasta and lentils, and ful medames, a traditional dish of fava beans. Street food is a big part of eating in Egypt, but choose clean, busy vendors with a good reputation to avoid stomach trouble.

With these points in mind, your March trip should be comfortable, safe and full of good memories.

## Where to stay and eat

The right places to stay and eat make a real difference to a March trip, and the pleasant weather means there is a wide choice for every taste and budget.

For a beach holiday with a touch of luxury, the Red Sea resorts in Sharm el-Sheikh offer seafront properties with all-inclusive packages, heated pools and a wide range of water sports. The warm, clear water is ideal for snorkelling, diving or simply relaxing on the shore, and private day tours from Sharm el-Sheikh add more to see. Resorts such as the Four Seasons Resort Sharm El Sheikh and the Oberoi Sahl Hasheesh in Hurghada are known for their high standard of comfort and service.

::: keep 000000000179

In Cairo, you can choose between [historic hotels and modern luxury](/blog/historical-hotels-in-egypt). The [Marriott Mena House](/marriott-mena-house-4-days-stay), with its views of the Pyramids, combines history and comfort, while the Four Seasons Hotel Cairo at Nile Plaza offers contemporary luxury with wide views of the Nile. Cairo’s food scene runs from traditional Egyptian restaurants to international fine dining; make sure to try koshari, ful medames and freshly baked baladi bread.

Luxor and Aswan have [boutique hotels](/blog/boutique-hotels-in-egypt) and distinctive river cruises, and many rooms look out over the Nile or ancient temples. The Sofitel Winter Palace in Luxor and the Old Cataract Hotel in Aswan combine history, elegance and comfort. Dining here often centres on traditional Egyptian food, and many restaurants serve meals on terraces overlooking the river.

::: keep 00000000019e

For a more adventurous stay, the eco-lodges and camps of the Western Desert, such as those around [Siwa Oasis](/guide/siwa-oasis), offer a sustainable way to experience Egypt’s natural landscape. They are built to blend into their surroundings, and meals are often traditional Bedouin cooking over an open fire.

Choosing well where you stay and eat helps make a March trip to Egypt both comfortable and memorable.

## Why March is a good time to visit Egypt

March in Egypt combines pleasant weather, a lively cultural calendar and plenty of opportunity for both adventure and rest. From the pyramids and quiet Nile cruises to local life and good food, it offers something for most kinds of traveller.

The mild climate makes almost everything more comfortable, whether you are visiting ancient temples, heading into the desert or relaxing on the Red Sea.

With a little planning, such as packing layers and organising your transport, the trip should run smoothly, and the range of hotels and restaurants means your stay can be comfortable and full of good meals.
