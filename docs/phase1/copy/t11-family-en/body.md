*The first thing to decide for a family trip in Egypt is not the hotel name. Is it a sea-and-rest trip or a sites trip? Are the children small, or old enough to respond to history? The answer decides the region, and on the coast it decides the beach as well.*

There are three main shapes of family travel in Egypt. One is a Red Sea resort stay: [Hurghada](/guide/hurghada), [El Gouna](/guide/al-gouna), Makadi Bay, [Marsa Alam](/guide/marsa-alam). Another is the Sinai, where [Sharm el-Sheikh](/guide/sharm-el-sheikh) and [Dahab](/guide/dahab) are centred on the sea, snorkelling and wind sports. The third combines [Cairo](/guide/cairo), [Luxor](/guide/luxor) and [Aswan](/guide/aswan) to put children inside actual ancient Egypt. A fourth, smaller shape is the Mediterranean, at [Alexandria](/guide/alexandria) or [Marsa Matruh](/guide/marsa-matruh).

Inside the same "Egypt trip," these are quite different in substance. With small children, where you prize daily meals, naps, the pool and short transfers, an all-inclusive on the Red Sea is a strong option. With children who can swim and respond to the underwater world, Sharm or Marsa Alam works well. With children eight and older who can engage with pyramids, mummies and temples, the value of Cairo and Luxor goes up sharply.

So there is no single best family resort. The answer turns on the family's ages, stamina, interests, and how much itinerary the parents want to build. For the wider picture of what Egypt does with children, see [family adventures in Egypt](/blog/family-adventures-in-egypt) and our notes on [travelling as a family](/journeys/travelling-as-a-family).

## Check the beach before the star rating

For families coming from abroad, the Red Sea is the most workable coast. Clear water, reef close by, family-oriented resorts, comparatively warm even in winter, and some areas combine well with Cairo or Luxor.

Not every Red Sea beach suits toddlers, though. In some places the reef starts right off the shore and you enter the water from a jetty. That is good for swimmers and snorkelling, and inconvenient for small children who want to play in the shallows.

So confirm the beach, not only the stars. Is it sandy? Is there shallow water? Is entry from the shore or from a jetty? Is there coral underfoot? Is there a lifeguard? Is there a children's pool? For a family, these questions matter as much as the hotel's category.

## Hurghada: easy sea for small children

Hurghada is the most legible Red Sea choice for families. It has an airport, a large stock of hotels and easy links to Luxor, and what a family trip needs (all-inclusive, pool, kids' club, beach, evening shows, snorkelling boat trips) generally completes inside the property.

This is not a place to expect strong city appeal. Hurghada is a resort city, not a quiet fishing village; its advantage is the ease of staying inside the hotel. For families with younger children, "not having to move" is large. Breakfast, pool, nap, beach, an early dinner. You can keep that rhythm without breaking it.

[VERIFY: each still operating as a family resort, and its current water-park and kids'-club facilities] Steigenberger ALDAU Beach, Steigenberger Aqua Magic, Rixos Premium Magawish and the Pickalbatros aquapark hotels are familiar candidates here. For upper-end taste, look at service consistency and room size; for waterslide focus, look at aquapark scale; for ease of food, look at the all-inclusive content.

What to watch in Hurghada is that hotels vary a lot. Even within "family-friendly," one property may be built around three-to-seven-year-olds, another around children who want slides all day, another around parents who want service and space. Before booking, check recent reviews for the kids' club, pool safety, food, and ease of getting onto the beach.

## El Gouna: a town feel and shallow lagoons

El Gouna, north of Hurghada, is more planned and calmer. Lagoons, marina, restaurants and hotels are laid out so that walking or cycling is easy. Shallow lagoons suit small children; kitesurfing and similar water sports suit older children and teenagers.

For full-package all-inclusive comfort, Hurghada. For a town feel with room to step out in the evening, El Gouna. That split is practical.

## Makadi Bay: for families that settle into the resort

Makadi Bay is the resort area south of Hurghada. It sits a little away from the busier centre and suits a more inside-the-resort stay. Hotels with large pools, water parks and big grounds make it easier for children to spend an entire day on site without getting bored.

The trade-off is that the airport transfer is a little longer than central Hurghada. The premise is to spend time inside the hotel rather than going out for meals or walks. If that is easier for the family, Makadi Bay is a good choice. For a family that wants a different town walk each evening, it can feel closed.

If you pick Makadi Bay, the inside of the hotel essentially is the trip. Look carefully at room size, pool, slides, children's programmes and food choices.

## Sharm el-Sheikh: strong for swimming children

Sharm el-Sheikh's appeal is the sea itself. Reef, clarity, [Ras Mohammed National Park](/guide/sharm-el-sheikh/ras-mohammed-national-park), boat trips out toward the Strait of Tiran. For families interested in snorkelling and diving, Sharm can leave a stronger impression than Hurghada.

Smaller children need some care. Many Sharm beaches are not gentle walk-in sand; to protect the coral, some entries use jetties into deeper water. Good for fish-watching; not suited to toddlers playing in shallows. A "private beach" can be perfect for small children or better suited to adults snorkelling off a jetty, so confirm hotel by hotel.

[VERIFY: still operating as a family resort; Kids For All Seasons programme and its age range] The Four Seasons Resort Sharm el-Sheikh is an upper-end option for families who prize service quality, rooms and family programmes; its published information has listed the Kids For All Seasons programme for ages 4 to 12. [VERIFY: still operating as a family resort; water park and Rixy Kids Club] Rixos Premium Seagate suits families who prize all-inclusive, water parks and the Rixy Kids Club.

In Sharm, the position of the hotel's bay matters. Near [Naama Bay](/guide/sharm-el-sheikh/naama-bay-sharm-el-sheikh-guide), dining out and boat departures are convenient. The Nabq side is quieter and more resort-like, but further from the centre. Sharks Bay and the areas around it offer a balance for families who want sea and comfort without the liveliest strip. Choose by bay and stay style, not by brand alone.

For older children and teenagers, Sharm is strong: snorkelling, beginner diving, boat trips, desert outings. Sinai can carry travel-advisory or insurance considerations depending on nationality and time. [VERIFY: current home-country travel advice for South Sinai] The Sharm resort area has long operated as a tourist destination; confirm current advice before booking.

## Marsa Alam: when the sea leads

Marsa Alam is quieter than Hurghada or Sharm, with southern Red Sea reef and wildlife as the appeal. Around Abu Dabbab and Marsa Mubarak, turtles and sometimes dugongs may be seen. [VERIFY: whether dugong sightings are still reported at Abu Dabbab and Marsa Mubarak] Wild animals are never guaranteed; for families interested in marine life, the area is very strong.

It suits children who can swim, who enjoy snorkelling, and who like sea creatures. Small children can enjoy it with the right hotel; overall, families with sea-ready children draw out more value.

Outside the resort, options are limited, and the premise is spending time inside the hotel grounds. Consider access too: flight schedules, the real distance from airport to hotel, transfers on the ground. Marsa Alam is the place to pick when the sea leads.

## Dahab: for teenagers

Dahab is a different Sinai sea from Sharm. Less the world of a polished large resort, more a seaside town of dive shops, cafés, wind sports and mountain views. The lagoon makes windsurfing and kitesurfing easy to learn, the nearby reefs allow snorkelling from the shore, and desert and mountain day trips can be built in.

The famous Blue Hole is a strong dive site and known for depth-related risks. With families, treat it as surface snorkelling or as scenery. Deep diving is for experienced divers, not a children's playground.

Dahab may feel too free for families seeking a fully managed resort with young children. For families wanting a slightly independent trip with teenagers, it may stay in memory longer than Sharm. As with Sharm, check current Sinai advice before planning.

## Ain Sokhna: a short sea close to Cairo

Ain Sokhna is the Red Sea closest to the capital, easy to drive to and used by Egyptian families at weekends. It is not where to expect the Red Sea's best reef or full resort experience. The value is closeness: a few days in Cairo, no extra domestic flight, one or two nights of sea and pool added for the children.

For a beach-centred family trip of a week or more, Hurghada, El Gouna, Sharm or Marsa Alam are the candidates.

## The Mediterranean coast

Egypt's Mediterranean is an entirely different trip from the Red Sea. Alexandria is less a beach resort than a sea city: Corniche, the library, catacombs, fortress, cafés. With older children, it is more natural to fit the sea into a city stay than to chase swimming. In summer its beaches crowd with domestic travellers; don't expect the polished quiet of an international resort.

Marsa Matruh has particularly beautiful water, with white sand and pale blue coves such as Agiba Beach. The international family-resort base is less developed than on the Red Sea, so it suits families already used to Egypt's transfers and hotels. The Mediterranean is primarily a summer coast, June to September; in winter it is not a swimming destination.

## Cairo: not a resort, but the core of a family trip

Cairo is not a typical family resort destination. For an Egypt family trip, however, there is no need to skip it. If children have any knowledge of ancient Egypt from school or books, the combination of the [Giza](/guide/giza) pyramids and the Grand Egyptian Museum is a very strong experience.

For lodging in Cairo, decide by purpose. If the centre of the trip is the pyramids and the GEM, a hotel on the Giza side is convenient. [VERIFY: still operating, and pyramid-view rooms as described] The Marriott Mena House, where the view of the pyramid is itself part of the stay, leaves a particularly clear impression on children.

If you want to see Islamic Cairo, [Coptic Cairo](/guide/cairo/coptic-cairo), the Nile and restaurants, hotels in Zamalek, Garden City or on the Corniche become candidates. Cairo is heavy with traffic, however. For family travel, a plan that does not push long transfers every day is important.

Cairo is a place for building strong days, not for easy resort rest. Don't overload the pyramids and the museum into one day; build in hotel rest time, and the trip works.

## Luxor: where older children gain the most

Luxor deserves more family attention than it usually gets. The [Valley of the Kings](/guide/luxor/valley-of-the-kings), Karnak, [Luxor Temple](/guide/luxor/the-luxor-temple), the Hatshepsut mortuary temple, [Medinet Habu](/guide/luxor/madinat-habu-temple). These are not textbook illustrations; they are places where you feel size in the body.

It is not for very young children. Heat, distances, the size of the sites, the volume of explanation: these suit children of eight or older, ideally those who can engage with history a little.

For families in Luxor, a Nile-side hotel with a pool is easy to use. [VERIFY: still operating; pool and Nile-view facilities] A property like the Hilton Luxor Resort & Spa sits close to Karnak, with pool and Nile views, and works as the place to rest after sites. The point is to use the hotel as the rest device for sightseeing.

In Luxor, see sites early in the morning, rest at midday, and go out again in the late afternoon. Pushing through the midday tires adults and children both.

## Combining sites and sea

In family travel, combining sites and sea is what makes Egypt work. Two or three nights in Cairo and Giza. Three or four on the Nile or in Luxor. Three on the Red Sea. With ten to twelve days, this shape is very stable.

Put the sea at the end. Pyramids, museum, temples and tombs first, then rest on the Red Sea. The children's stamina returns and the adults close the trip calmly.

Hurghada and El Gouna combine easily with Luxor, by road or by domestic flight. Sharm suits trips that really want the Sinai-side sea; combined with Cairo or Luxor, confirm flight connections carefully. Marsa Alam is excellent as a sea-led trip, but harder to join to Nile sightseeing.

Don't add the beach for a single night. Transfer, check-in, luggage and settling in eat the time. Two nights is the reasonable minimum; three lets the body actually change pace.

## All-inclusive or breakfast-only

On the Red Sea, all-inclusive often suits families. Children want drinks and snacks repeatedly, and going out for meals adds transfer load; all-inclusive lets you read both cost and effort.

In Cairo and Luxor, all-inclusive is not necessarily right. Breakfast-only is usually enough. You go out for sites in the day and fit lunch and dinner to the itinerary.

Don't conflate the two cases. At a sea resort, the hotel is the centre of the trip. In Cairo or Luxor, the hotel is the base that supports the trip.

## By age

Under six, prioritise short transfers, sandy beaches, shallow water, shallow pools, kids' menus, medical access and easy naps. Large resorts in Hurghada, Makadi Bay, El Gouna or Sahl Hasheesh fit.

Seven to eleven, combine Red Sea time with a light sites experience. Snorkelling, glass-bottom boats, short boat trips and simple water sports fit in; Sharm for snorkelling, or a short Cairo segment added to a Hurghada stay. With good swimmers, Marsa Alam appears too.

Twelve and up, the value of Cairo and Luxor rises sharply. Ancient Egypt, mummies, royal tombs, temples and the geography of the Nile become understandable as a concrete world, not just "hot ruins." The coast widens too: Discover Scuba, kitesurfing, windsurfing, Dahab's independent air. Rather than parking teenagers at the hotel, choose experiences together.

With mixed ages, large resorts are easier: pool and kids' club for the young, snorkelling and sports for the older ones, rest time for the adults. Don't try to give everyone the same experience. For health, documents and packing with children, see [travelling with kids](/travel-tips/traveling-with-kids).

## Season

October through April is the easiest for family travel. The Red Sea is comfortable, and Cairo and Luxor are less severe than midsummer. December to February brings cool days and wind on the coast, though resort stays remain comfortable. December and January are popular and prices climb. Spring break and European school holidays also crowd.

Summer prices drop, but the heat of Luxor and Cairo is hard on family travel. A Red Sea–only trip works: with pool and sea at the centre, it is much easier than summer Cairo or Luxor. July and August crowd in some areas with domestic and international holidays. A sites-heavy trip in summer does not work.

For diving or snorkelling, factor in wind, currents, water temperature and the children's swimming ability. Warm water doesn't mean every site suits beginners.

## Safety in the water

For children in the water, the hotel's beach is part of the hotel choice. Is it shallow? Rocks or coral? Jetty entry? Lifeguard? Days with current from tide or wind? Are reef shoes needed?

Don't step on coral, don't touch it, don't feed fish. Use reef-safe sunscreen. Teach children that the Red Sea is a living sea, not a theme park.

Snorkelling, even with flotation, needs supervision; strong swimmers can drift from shore, and a child who swims well in a pool can tire quickly in open water. On boat trips, check life jackets, the guide's briefing and the meeting point. For Discover Scuba, use a certified centre and confirm age limits, health, insurance and instructor ratios. Don't choose on price alone.

## Before you book

In family travel, the small details matter most. Before booking a resort, check:

- the real ages accepted by the kids' club, and whether it is free or charged;
- the programme's hours and season;
- family or connecting rooms, and the policy on cots and extra beds;
- whether the water park is inside the hotel or in a sister property;
- whether the beach allows easy entry or the reef requires a jetty;
- whether there is a heated pool in winter;
- distance from the airport;
- recent reviews of food and cleanliness;
- medical care on site or nearby.

Official descriptions help, but recent reviews from families usually reveal more. A resort can be excellent on paper and exhausting in practice if internal distances are huge, children's food is limited, or the kids' club runs only a few hours in high season.

## Which to pick

Convenient, low-failure family beach: Hurghada. A town feel with shallow lagoons: El Gouna. Staying inside a big resort: Makadi Bay. International resort with strong reef: Sharm el-Sheikh. Marine life and quiet: Marsa Alam. Active time with teenagers: Dahab. A short sea close to Cairo: Ain Sokhna. City and Mediterranean: Alexandria. Beautiful summer water for families who already know Egypt: Marsa Matruh.

## Finally

An Egypt family trip can end at the resort, and the warm sea, the pool and the all-inclusive comfort can make a perfectly enjoyable trip.

What Egypt actually offers families, however, is the chance to put "moments where the world widens" inside a holiday. Standing in front of a pyramid. Looking at Tutankhamun's funerary equipment. Walking between the columns at Karnak. Swimming over real reef on the Red Sea.

A good family hotel is the place that supports those experiences. It rests the children, doesn't exhaust the parents, and brings everyone back to a state where they can actually enjoy the next day of Egypt. That is the most important criterion when choosing family lodging here.
