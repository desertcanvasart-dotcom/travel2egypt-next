*Egypt doesn't really have a "bad time." It has times that don't suit the trip you have in mind. The same month means something different at the pyramids, in *[*Luxor*](/guide/luxor)*, on the Red Sea, and in the desert.*

The most common answer to the question of when to travel in Egypt is "October to April." That is broadly right, especially for a first trip that goes through [Cairo](/guide/cairo), [Giza](/guide/giza), Luxor, [Aswan](/guide/aswan), and [Abu Simbel](/guide/abu-simbel).

But Egypt is large. The Mediterranean coast at [Alexandria](/guide/alexandria), Cairo, the upper Egypt of Luxor and Aswan, the Red Sea at [Hurghada](/guide/hurghada) and Sharm, and the oases of the Western Desert do not share the same season. A summer in Luxor is severe; a summer beach holiday on the Red Sea works. A winter desert is beautiful; the nights are cold.

So the better way to choose is to decide first what the trip is centred on.

## For a first trip to Egypt

For a first trip built around Cairo, Giza, Luxor, and Aswan, November, February, and March are particularly easy.

November settles the heat and is less crowded than the winter peak. The pyramids and Luxor both walk well and the Nile cruise season is open.

February sits after the Christmas–New Year crush, with good temperatures and strong upper-Egypt site work. If the Abu Simbel sun alignment matters to you, planning around 22 February is an option.

March warms a little but still travels easily. Spring carries the khamsin wind, which on some days throws dust and can disrupt outdoor sightseeing.

## The most comfortable stretch: October to April

October to April is the base travel season for Egypt's main destinations. To run Cairo, Giza, Luxor, and Aswan without strain, choose inside this range.

October still carries summer heat early in the month and travels better the closer you get to the end. November is one of the most reliable months. December and January are strong on weather and the most crowded; prices climb. February balances well. March and April mix spring warmth and a little instability.

Late December into early January carries heavy international travel; popular hotels and Nile cruises fill early. If you go then, book early and prioritise early-morning sites.

## Luxor and Aswan

Luxor and Aswan are the most weather-sensitive places to choose for. Summer is genuinely hot, and walking the [Valley of the Kings](/guide/luxor/valley-of-the-kings) and Karnak takes a real toll.

The best window is November through March. Morning sites are pleasant and afternoons read well from the boat or the hotel. April is workable, with heat building in the second half. Late October is also a good choice.

Upper Egypt from May to September is cheaper and quieter, but the heat is the cost. To go in summer, rework the day: sites only in the early morning, midday rest, shade and water as non-negotiables.

## Cairo and Giza

Cairo and Giza are not as extreme as Luxor, but the summer is hot and spring can be hit by khamsin.

To walk the pyramids comfortably, November through March is the easy range. Winter mornings are cool, and evenings can be colder than you expect — bring a light layer in winter.

Spring on clear days is beautiful; on a dust day, visibility drops and outdoor sightseeing wears you down. Built-in margin in the schedule reduces the impact of those days.

## The Red Sea

Hurghada, Sharm el-Sheikh, and [Marsa Alam](/guide/marsa-alam) run on a different calendar from the sites.

For beach and snorkelling, October to May is the most comfortable. Not too hot, the water is enjoyable, and resort stays work.

Summer on the Red Sea is also possible. There is heat, but the trip works if it is built around the water. If you spend the day in the pool or the sea and don't add much land sightseeing, summer is a real option.

Combining the Red Sea and Luxor in the same summer trip needs care. The sea may be easy; the inland sites are a different heat entirely.

## The Western Desert and the oases

Siwa, Bahariya, the White Desert, Dakhla, Kharga — autumn through spring is the time. October to March is the season.

Winter desert days walk well; the nights are cold. For camping, bring warmth. Places like the White Desert read most beautifully in winter, when the low-angle light brings the rock forms forward.

A summer Western Desert trip is not recommended. Heat is severe and the risk of vehicle trouble or health problems goes up. If the desert is the centre of the trip, pick a cool season.

## Alexandria and the north coast

Alexandria and the Mediterranean coast carry a slightly different seasonal sense. Summer fills with domestic travellers escaping Cairo's heat, and the town's coastal character comes forward.

For quiet sightseeing, spring or autumn is better. Summer has life, but it crowds. Winter brings rain and wind days, with weather closer to the Mediterranean than the rest of Egypt.

## When to take a Nile cruise

A Nile cruise is possible year-round. The Luxor-to-Aswan stretch is regulated by the [High Dam](/guide/aswan/aswan-high-dam), so the river no longer answers to the great flood rhythm of antiquity. What changes by season is less the river than the feel of the trip: the heat as you walk the temples, the hours you can spend on the upper deck, the size of the crowds, the price, and how much stamina you have left at the end of each day.

### November to February: the easiest window

For most travellers, November through February is the easiest stretch. Days at Luxor and Aswan are comfortable; morning sites and afternoon time on the boat both work without strain. Evenings turn cool, so a light layer is useful on the upper deck.

December and January, on climate alone, are excellent. They are also crowded. Christmas and New Year compress demand: the popular boats, dahabiyas and upper-end hotels fill early and prices climb, and the Valley of the Kings, Karnak, [Edfu](/guide/edfu) and [Kom Ombo](/guide/kom-ombo) all get busy in the morning. November and February are the balanced months. Small boats and dahabiyas have few cabins, so in this window book early.

### March and April

March and April are very good months for the Nile. Temperatures climb gradually, mornings and evenings still feel easy, and many days are lighter than the winter peak. Easter and school holidays do compress: weeks that overlap European holidays push prices up and put crowds back into the main sites, so check the calendar before booking.

Spring also suits the [dahabiya](/blog/dahabiya-nile-cruises-wind-powered-journey), a small sailing vessel that moves more slowly than a standard cruise ship and calls at small villages and banks between the temples. Late April starts to warm up, but it is not yet summer; with an early morning and a real midday rest, it still travels comfortably.

### October

October is the shoulder. The first half can still feel hot; the second half travels significantly more easily, and the river and the western cliffs read beautifully in the late-afternoon light. As in February, the Abu Simbel sun alignment is known around 22 October. Travellers who don't handle heat well, families with small children and older travellers will be more comfortable waiting for November.

### May to September

A summer Nile cruise is not for everyone. In Luxor and Aswan, day temperatures often go above 40°C, and walking the temples and tombs becomes hard. The advantages are real: crowds are thin, prices drop, and the Valley of the Kings and Karnak at first light can be surprisingly quiet.

A summer trip has to be designed differently. Concentrate sites in the early morning and finish before late morning, rest on board through the middle of the day, and move again late afternoon. A large ship, with air conditioning and an interior to retreat to, is the easiest boat in the hot months. Dahabiyas are at their best in spring and autumn, and long [felucca](/blog/felucca-rides-in-egypt) trips are not recommended in summer.

### Crowds and the hour of the day

Beyond the season, the time of day matters. At the Valley of the Kings, Karnak, Edfu and Philae especially, getting in close to opening time changes the visit. To avoid the worst crowds, skip late December into early January, and skip Easter. November, February, early March, late April and late October tend to balance conditions and crowd.

## On Ramadan

Ramadan moves date each year. It affects travel, but it isn't a season to rule out.

During the day, some shops and restaurants change hours, and in more local areas, eating and drinking openly calls for care. After sunset, the city comes strongly alive — iftar, family gatherings, the energy of the night — and carries a particular air.

The main sites generally remain open. Transfers, meals, and opening hours simply need a more flexible handling. If you travel during Ramadan, don't pack the days; leave room for the night side of Egypt.

## Finally

For a first trip to ancient Egypt: November, February, March. For a Nile cruise: November to February, or March and April on a dahabiya. For a Red Sea rest: October to May, or a dedicated summer beach trip. For the desert: October to March. For going deep in Luxor and Aswan, skip high summer.

What matters in choosing the season is not average temperature alone. Where you walk, what hour you move, how crowded it will be, what the city looks like at night. Choosing with all of that in view makes Egypt much easier to travel.
