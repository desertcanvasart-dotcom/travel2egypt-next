Mount Sinai Sunrise Trek & St Catherine's Monastery

An overnight ascent of Mount Sinai for sunrise from the summit at 2,285 metres, followed by a guided visit to the 6th-century St Catherine's Monastery — the Burning Bush, the icons, the manuscripts.

The midnight climb up the mountain Moses is said to have climbed, sunrise at the summit, and the oldest continuously inhabited Christian monastery in the world at the bottom. Mount Sinai rises 2,285 metres above the southern Sinai peninsula; the ascent is a working pilgrimage that pre-dates tourism by centuries, and the summit reach is a sunrise that lights the surrounding granite in colours no daylight repeats.

At the base is St Catherine's Monastery — founded in the 6th century under Justinian I, one of the oldest continuously inhabited Christian monasteries on earth, and the site Coptic and Orthodox tradition places the Burning Bush. Its library holds one of the most significant collections of early Christian manuscripts in the world.

The Mount Sinai sunrise is one of the very few tourist experiences that survives the encounter with its own reputation.

The day, hour by hour

  • Late afternoonPickup from your hotel. Air-conditioned vehicle through the Sinai interior — roughly two and a half hours from Sharm, less from Dahab.
  • EveningBrief rest at the base. The licensed guide gives the safety and trail briefing and the historical and religious context. The standard route is the Camel Trail, gentle for most of its length; the final ascent is the 750 Steps of Repentance, on foot.
  • MidnightThe ascent begins. Roughly three hours at a steady pace. Small Bedouin huts along the trail sell hot tea and rest stops. Camel hire is available for the lower section.
  • Before dawnSummit. The sunrise from the eastern ridge takes roughly twenty minutes — pinks to oranges to gold over the granite. Cold at the top regardless of season.
  • Early morningDescent & monastery. Around two hours down, then a guided visit to the sections of St Catherine's open the day of your visit — typically the chapel exterior, the courtyards, the Burning Bush. Roughly 60–90 minutes on site.
  • MiddayReturn. Light breakfast nearby, then the drive back to your hotel — arrival by midday to early afternoon.
Concierge note

The camel covers the lower trail but not the final 750 steps — those are on foot for everyone. If the steps are the worry, that's the part to train for, not the climb.

A note on 2026 access

St Catherine's has been operating under shifting restrictions linked to surrounding development works and the monastery's own conservation programme. The library, parts of the icon collection, and sections of the basilica have been intermittently closed; the mountain trail and the outer courtyards have remained accessible. We confirm current access the day of your visit.

Who it's for — and who should skip it

This is a genuinely fitness-demanding pilgrimage trek, not a sightseeing transfer. It suits walkers comfortable with a three-hour night climb on uneven granite who want both the sunrise and the monastery's accessible chambers in a single trip. If a midnight start and a cold summit don't appeal, the daytime St Catherine's visit reaches the same monastery without the climb — say the word and we'll switch you.
