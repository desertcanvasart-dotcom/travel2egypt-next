### Planning and entry

#### When is the best time to visit Egypt?

October through April is the comfortable window. Between November and February, daytime highs in
Cairo and Luxor typically sit around 19–25°C (66–77°F), which makes long days at open, unshaded
sites manageable rather than punishing. That window is also peak season, so expect fuller sites
and higher rates. October and April are the useful compromise — warm but bearable, with
noticeably thinner crowds. Summer works if the Red Sea is your focus, since the sea moderates the
heat, but Upper Egypt in July regularly clears 40°C (104°F).

**Link:** `/best-time-to-visit-egypt/` · **Status:** Rewritten — legacy version had all temperature figures missing

#### Do I need a visa to travel to Egypt?

Most visitors do. Egypt operates an e-Visa portal, and visa on arrival is available at major
airports for a large number of nationalities. Your passport needs at least six months' validity
from your date of entry. Eligibility, fees, and processing times vary by nationality and change
without much notice, so confirm against the official Egyptian e-Visa portal or your nearest
Egyptian consulate rather than a travel blog.

**Link:** `/passport-and-visa/` · **Status:** VERIFY — eligibility lists and fees change; consider a "last checked" date stamp on this one

#### What is the weather like across the year?

Hot and dry from May to October; mild and pleasant from November to March. Cairo and the Delta get
occasional light winter rain. Upper Egypt and the Western Desert are effectively rainless. Desert
nights drop sharply year-round — a 35°C afternoon in Siwa can be followed by a 15°C night. Khamsin
winds carrying sand and dust can blow through between March and May.

**Link:** `/egypt-weather-guide/` · **Status:** Merged from two thin legacy entries

#### What should I pack for Egypt?

Light, loose, breathable clothing that covers shoulders and knees. Closed walking shoes with grip —
temple and tomb floors are uneven and often polished smooth. A brimmed hat, sunglasses, and
high-factor sunscreen. A scarf, which does triple duty as sun cover, dust filter, and mosque
headcovering. A refillable bottle. A European two-pin adapter (Type C/F, 220V). And one warm layer,
because desert evenings and over-air-conditioned interiors both catch people out.

### Money, connectivity and practicalities

#### What currency is used in Egypt?

The Egyptian Pound (EGP), divided into 100 piastres. Cash still matters more than visitors expect —
in markets, small restaurants, taxis, tipping, and anywhere rural. ATMs are common in cities and
resort towns. Change money at banks, licensed exchange bureaux, or your hotel; not with anyone who
approaches you on the street.

**Link:** `/currency-in-egypt/` · **Status:** Rewritten — add a note that the rate has moved substantially since the March 2024 float

#### Can I use credit cards throughout Egypt?

In hotels, larger restaurants, and established shops in cities and resort areas, yes. In bazaars,
local eateries, taxis, and rural areas, no. Carry small denomination notes — getting change for a
large note is a routine friction point, and it affects tipping more than anything else.

**Link:** `/currency-in-egypt/`

#### How does tipping work in Egypt?

Tipping (*baksheesh*) is woven into daily service culture and expected in more situations than most
visitors anticipate: hotel staff, drivers, guides, restaurant service, site attendants, and anyone
who assists you. Small notes, given directly, are the practical approach.

**Status:** NEW — the legacy page had no tipping question at all, which is a significant gap for a booking site

#### Can I get a local SIM card easily?

Yes. SIM cards are sold at airport arrivals halls and at operator shops and kiosks throughout
cities. You need your passport for registration. Data is inexpensive by international standards.
eSIM is increasingly available if your handset supports it.

#### What time zone is Egypt in?

Egypt is on Eastern European Time, UTC+2. Since 2023 it also observes daylight saving, moving to
UTC+3 from the last Friday of April to the last Friday of October.

### Safety and health

#### Is it safe to travel to Egypt right now?

The main tourist corridor — Cairo, Giza, Luxor, Aswan, the Nile valley, and the Red Sea resorts —
is heavily policed and continuously visited. Ordinary precautions against petty theft and
overcharging apply, as in any major destination. Government travel advisories consistently draw a
distinction between these areas and parts of North Sinai and certain border regions, which carry
restrictions. Because advisories change, check your own government's current guidance before
booking rather than relying on a static page.

**Link:** `/health-and-safety/` · **Status:** VERIFY — consider making this one dynamic, or dated. A hardcoded safety claim is the single riskiest sentence on a travel site.

#### Are there health concerns before travelling to Egypt?

Speak to a travel health clinic or your GP well before departure so routine vaccinations can be
reviewed. Stomach upset is the most common traveller complaint — stick to bottled or filtered
water, be cautious with raw salads and unpeeled fruit outside established restaurants, and pack a
basic remedy. Heat and dehydration cause more problems than anything exotic; the sun at midday on
an open site is stronger than most visitors expect.

**Link:** `/health-and-safety/`

#### What vaccinations do I need for Egypt?

There are generally no mandatory vaccination requirements for entry from most countries. Hepatitis
A and B and typhoid are commonly recommended because they're food- and water-borne, and routine
cover (tetanus, diphtheria, MMR, polio) should be current. This is a conversation for a travel
health professional, not a website.

**Link:** `/health-and-safety/` · **Status:** Merged — the legacy page asked this twice with slightly different answers

#### Can I drink the tap water in Egypt?

Egyptian tap water is treated and chlorinated, but it isn't recommended for drinking by visitors —
the chlorination is heavy and older pipework can reintroduce sediment. Use sealed bottled or
filtered water for drinking, and for brushing teeth if you have a sensitive stomach. Check the seal
is intact when you buy. Ice served at established hotels and restaurants is normally made from
filtered water; ice from street vendors is not.

**Link:** `/water-safety-in-egypt-advice-for-travelers/` · **Status:** Rewritten — legacy answer opened by saying the water "should be safe to drink" and then told readers not to drink it

#### How do I avoid tourist scams in Egypt?

The common patterns are consistent and easy to recognise once you know them: unsolicited "help"
that turns into a fee, a site being described as closed so you're diverted to a shop, a camel or
carriage ride quoted at one price and settled at another, and taxi fares that are never mentioned
until you arrive. Agree every price before the service starts, not after. Book guides and
excursions through licensed operators rather than someone who approaches you at the gate.

**Link:** `/health-and-safety/`

#### Are there restrictions on photography?

Yes, and they're taken seriously. Photography of military sites and personnel is prohibited, and
that can extend to bridges, checkpoints, and some transport infrastructure that looks innocuous.
Many museums and tombs charge a separate photography ticket or prohibit it entirely — the Valley of
the Kings has its own rules. Always ask before photographing people.

#### What are the rules on flying a drone in Egypt?

Drones are tightly regulated. Bringing one into the country without prior authorisation risks
confiscation at customs, and flying without a permit — particularly near archaeological sites,
military areas, or infrastructure — carries serious legal consequences. Permits are difficult for
visitors to obtain.

### Culture and etiquette

#### How should I dress when visiting Egypt?

Modest dress is the norm and reads as respect. For both men and women, covering shoulders and knees
works everywhere and avoids unwanted attention. Loose, lightweight fabric is more comfortable in
the heat than close-fitting clothing. Beach and pool areas at resorts are relaxed; the rest of the
resort, and everywhere outside it, is not.

**Link:** `/cultural-etiquette-in-egypt/`

#### What's the dress code for mosques and religious sites?

Conservative. Arms and legs covered, nothing tight or sheer. Women should carry a scarf for hair
covering in mosques. Men should wear long trousers rather than shorts. Shoes come off before
entering prayer areas. These aren't suggestions — they're often conditions of entry.

**Link:** `/visiting-a-religious-site/` · **Status:** Merged from two near-identical legacy entries

#### What customs and traditions should I be aware of?

Egypt is predominantly Muslim, with a significant Coptic Christian minority, and religious
observance is part of ordinary public life. Greet people before getting to business — the abruptness
that reads as efficient elsewhere reads as rude here. The right hand is used for eating and giving.
Hospitality is offered generously and refusing outright can cause offence; accepting a small amount
is the graceful move. Religion and domestic politics are handled carefully in conversation.

**Link:** `/cultural-etiquette-in-egypt/`

#### What should I know about travelling during Ramadan?

Muslims fast from dawn to sunset, so eating, drinking, and smoking in public during daylight is
avoided out of respect. Many restaurants outside hotels close or shorten hours during the day and
come alive after sunset. Sites and offices often run reduced hours. Against that, the atmosphere
after iftar is genuinely one of the best things about visiting Egypt — cities stay awake and social
deep into the night.

**Link:** `/ramadan-in-egypt/`

#### Is English widely spoken in Egypt?

In Cairo, the resort towns, and anywhere serving tourism — hotels, guides, drivers, larger shops —
yes, commonly. Away from those, less so, and in the countryside barely at all. A handful of Arabic
phrases goes a disproportionately long way.

**Link:** `/language-in-egypt/`

#### What Arabic phrases are worth knowing?

*As-salamu alaykum* (peace be upon you) as a universal greeting, answered with *wa alaykum
as-salam*. *Shukran* for thank you. *Min fadlak* (to a man) or *min fadlik* (to a woman) for please.
*La shukran* — no, thank you — is the single most useful phrase in a bazaar. *Yalla* means let's go
and you will hear it constantly.

**Link:** `/language-in-egypt/`

#### What are the rules around alcohol in Egypt?

The legal drinking age is 21. Alcohol is served in licensed hotels, bars, restaurants, and resorts,
and sold at licensed shops. It is not part of ordinary public life, and drinking in public spaces
or being visibly drunk outside a venue is poorly received. During Ramadan, availability narrows
considerably.

**Link:** `/alcohol-in-egypt/` · **Status:** CORRECTION — legacy answer had the age missing entirely

#### What festivals might I encounter?

Ramadan and the two Eids (al-Fitr and al-Adha) reshape the calendar and shift each year against the
Gregorian date. Moulid al-Nabi marks the Prophet's birthday. Coptic Christmas falls on 7 January.
Sham El-Nessim, a spring festival with pharaonic roots, is celebrated by Egyptians of all faiths on
the Monday after Coptic Easter. The Abu Simbel Sun Festival, when sunlight reaches the temple's
inner sanctuary, draws crowds on 22 February and 22 October.

### Getting around

#### What transport options are available for tourists?

Domestic flights connect Cairo with Luxor, Aswan, Abu Simbel, and the Red Sea, and save a lot of
time. Sleeper trains run Cairo–Luxor–Aswan. Long-distance coaches cover most intercity routes.
Within cities, ride-hailing apps are the simplest option for visitors. Nile cruise boats and
feluccas cover the Luxor–Aswan stretch as an experience rather than pure transport.

**Link:** `/transportation-in-egypt/`

#### How do I get around Cairo?

The Metro is fast, cheap, and genuinely the best way to cross the city during the day — it bypasses
the traffic entirely. Ride-hailing apps are widely used and remove the fare negotiation problem
that comes with street taxis. Street taxis work if you agree the fare before setting off. City
buses are difficult to navigate without Arabic.

**Link:** `/transportation-in-egypt/`

#### Can I take a Nile cruise?

Yes, and it remains one of the best ways to see Upper Egypt. Standard itineraries run between Luxor
and Aswan over three to seven nights, stopping at Edfu, Kom Ombo, and Esna. Options range from
large cruise vessels to small dahabiyas under sail to Lake Nasser cruises reaching Abu Simbel by
water.

**Link:** `/the-definitive-guide-to-egypt-nile-cruise/`

#### What are the best day trips from Cairo?

Giza and Saqqara together, which shows you the whole arc of pyramid development in a single day.
Dahshur, for the Bent and Red Pyramids without the crowds. Alexandria, reachable by train, for the
catacombs, the Mediterranean corniche, and a completely different urban character. Fayoum, for
Wadi El Rayan and the whale fossils at Wadi El Hitan.

### Museums

#### Should I visit the Grand Egyptian Museum?

The Grand Egyptian Museum opened fully on 1 November 2025 and to the public on 4 November, standing
beside the Giza plateau. It holds the complete Tutankhamun collection displayed together for the
first time since Howard Carter's 1922 discovery — more than 5,000 objects, including the gold
funerary mask — across twelve permanent galleries covering prehistory to the Graeco-Roman period.
Allow considerably more time than you think.

#### What's at the Egyptian Museum in Tahrir Square?

The original 1902 museum in Tahrir Square still holds an enormous collection, but its two headline
draws have moved: the Tutankhamun treasures went to the Grand Egyptian Museum, and the royal
mummies went to the National Museum of Egyptian Civilization in Fustat. What remains is a
dense, old-fashioned, and rewarding collection for anyone who wants depth rather than highlights.

At the National Museum of Egyptian Civilization in Fustat, since the 2021 Pharaohs' Golden Parade
moved them from Tahrir Square. They're displayed in a purpose-built, low-lit hall.

#### Are there good collections outside Cairo?

The Luxor Museum is smaller, better lit, and better curated than its size suggests — many people
prefer it to Cairo's larger institutions. The Nubian Museum in Aswan covers a culture and a history
that the Cairo museums barely touch.

### Ancient sites

#### Can I go inside the Pyramids of Giza?

Yes, within limits. The exteriors and the plateau are freely accessible with a site ticket. Interior
access to the Great Pyramid of Khufu requires a separate ticket, issued in limited daily numbers,
so arrive early or book ahead. The interior is a steep, narrow, unventilated climb, which is worth
knowing before you commit.

**Link:** `/the-pyramids-of-egypt/`

#### Can I see the Sphinx up close?

You can get close enough for detail and photography from the viewing terrace and the enclosure
approach, though direct access to the Sphinx itself is restricted. It sits within the Giza complex,
so it's covered by the same ticket.

**Link:** `/exploring-the-great-sphinx-of-giza/`

#### What are the must-see historical sites in Egypt?

Giza for the pyramids and the Sphinx. Saqqara for the Step Pyramid. Karnak and Luxor Temple.
The Valley of the Kings. Abu Simbel. Philae. Edfu and Kom Ombo. That list covers roughly two and a
half thousand years of building and is the spine of most itineraries.

**Link:** `/historical-egypt-travel/`

#### What should I know about the Valley of the Kings?

The standard ticket admits you to three tombs from a rotating open list. Tutankhamun, Seti I, and
Ramesses V/VI each require separate tickets. Photography rules vary by tomb. The site is hot and
almost entirely unshaded, so early morning is the only comfortable option.

**Link:** `/must-see-tombs-in-the-valley-of-the-kings/`

#### What is Karnak Temple?

A vast temple complex on the east bank at Luxor, built and rebuilt over roughly two thousand years.
The Great Hypostyle Hall — 134 columns, some over twenty metres — is the reason most people
remember it. Allow at least two hours.

**Link:** `/half-day-tour-of-luxor-karnak-temples/` · **Status:** Legacy version linked this to an Aswan page. Karnak is in Luxor.

#### How do I get to the Temple of Philae?

Philae sits on Agilkia Island near Aswan and is reached by motorboat from the Marina jetty. The
temple, dedicated to Isis, was relocated stone by stone when Lake Nasser rose behind the High Dam.
The setting on the water is a large part of the appeal.

**Link:** `/places-to-visit-in-aswan/`

#### What about the Temple of Edfu?

Dedicated to Horus and among the best-preserved temples in Egypt, largely because it was buried in
sand for centuries. It's a standard Nile cruise stop between Luxor and Aswan, traditionally
approached by horse-drawn carriage from the mooring.

**Link:** `/historical-sites-visited-by-a-nile-cruise/` · **Status:** Legacy version linked to an Aswan page

#### What is the Dendera Temple Complex?

A remarkably intact temple to Hathor, north of Luxor, known for its painted ceiling and the
astronomical reliefs. It sees a fraction of Karnak's visitors, which is much of its appeal. Reached
by road from Luxor, often paired with Abydos in a long day.

**Link:** `/historical-sites-visited-by-a-nile-cruise/`

#### Why visit Abydos?

The Temple of Seti I holds some of the finest surviving relief carving in Egypt, and the Abydos
King List — a carved sequence of pharaohs — is one of the most cited objects in Egyptology. Usually
combined with Dendera as a full-day trip from Luxor.

**Link:** `/historical-sites-visited-by-a-nile-cruise/`

#### What is Medinet Habu?

The mortuary temple of Ramesses III on the Luxor west bank. The reliefs retain more original colour
than almost anywhere else, and the battle scenes on the outer walls are extraordinary. Quiet
compared to the Valley of the Kings, which is a strong argument for going.

**Link:** `/places-to-visit-in-luxor/` · **Status:** Legacy version linked to an Aswan page

#### Why visit the Step Pyramid of Djoser?

It's the oldest large stone structure of its kind in Egypt, at Saqqara, and marks the architectural
step between mastaba tombs and true pyramids. The reopened interior and the surrounding funerary
complex reward more time than the standard coach stop allows.

**Link:** `/the-pyramids-of-egypt/` · **Status:** Legacy version linked to a museums page

#### What are the lesser-known ancient sites?

Dahshur, for the Bent Pyramid and the Red Pyramid — Sneferu's experiments, and far quieter than
Giza. Memphis, the Old Kingdom capital, now an open-air site. Beni Hasan and Tuna el-Gebel in
Middle Egypt. Kom Ombo's double temple, which most cruise passengers see but few plan for.

**Link:** `/historical-egypt-travel/`

#### Can I take a hot air balloon ride in Luxor?

Yes, over the west bank at sunrise. Flights lift before dawn and give you the Valley of the Kings,
the mortuary temples, and the green strip of the Nile against the desert edge. Flights are
weather-dependent and cancellations are common in winter.

**Link:** `/hot-air-balloons-ride-in-luxor-egypt/`

#### Where can I learn about hieroglyphs?

The Grand Egyptian Museum and the Egyptian Museum both run programming. In practice, the best
learning happens on site with a good Egyptologist guide at Karnak, Medinet Habu, or the Valley of
the Kings, where the carving is at reading distance.

**Link:** `/ancient-egyptian-kings/`

### Red Sea and outdoors

#### Where are the best beaches in Egypt?

Sharm El Sheikh for reef access straight off the shore. Hurghada for a long sandy stretch and the
widest range of resorts. Dahab for a slower, low-rise atmosphere. Marsa Alam for quieter water and
better marine encounters. The north coast and Marsa Matrouh have Mediterranean beaches with a
different character and a short summer season.

**Link:** `/top-adventures-things-to-do-in-sharm-el-sheikh/`

#### Is Egypt good for diving and snorkelling?

The Red Sea is one of the world's serious diving destinations — high visibility, healthy hard coral,
and reef systems reachable from shore. Ras Muhammad National Park near Sharm El Sheikh, the Straits
of Tiran, and the offshore reefs from Hurghada and Marsa Alam are the headline sites. Courses run
year-round at every level.

**Link:** `/top-adventures-things-to-do-in-sharm-el-sheikh/`

#### Is there wreck diving in the Red Sea?

Yes — the SS Thistlegorm, a British merchant vessel sunk in 1941 with its cargo still in the holds,
is among the most dived wrecks in the world. Abu Nuhas reef holds several more.

**Link:** `/safety-tips-while-diving-in-the-red-sea/` · **Status:** CORRECTION — the legacy page renders this as "This telegram wreck," which appears twice. It is the SS Thistlegorm.

#### Where can I windsurf or kitesurf?

Dahab and Hurghada both have reliable wind. Dahab's lagoon is shallow and forgiving, which makes it
a standard beginner location. Hurghada, Soma Bay, and El Gouna suit progression and higher wind.

**Link:** `/top-adventures-things-to-do-in-sharm-el-sheikh/`

#### Where can I hike in Egypt?

Sinai is the main hiking region — Mount Sinai itself, and the wider Bedouin-led trail networks
through the interior. The Red Sea Mountains offer harder routes. Hiking in Sinai is done with local
Bedouin guides, which is both a permit matter and the reason the walking is good.

**Link:** `/the-western-desert-in-egypt/`

#### How do I get to Mount Sinai?

From Dahab, Sharm El Sheikh, or St Catherine village. The standard approach is a night ascent
timed for sunrise, either by the camel path or the steeper Steps of Repentance. It's cold at the
summit before dawn regardless of season.

**Link:** `/sinai-historical-sites/`

#### Can I visit St Catherine's Monastery?

Yes. It sits at the foot of Mount Sinai and is among the oldest continuously operating Christian
monasteries in the world, a UNESCO World Heritage Site holding an exceptional icon and manuscript
collection. Opening hours are short and it closes to visitors on Fridays, Sundays, and feast days.

**Link:** `/sinai-historical-sites/` · **Status:** VERIFY opening days before publishing

#### Can I do a camel safari?

Short rides operate on the Giza plateau, which give you the pyramids from the desert side rather
than the road. Longer multi-day treks run in Sinai with Bedouin guides, and in the Western Desert
around the oases.

**Link:** `/sinai-desert-safaris/`

#### How do I get to the Bahariya Oasis and the White Desert?

Bahariya is roughly a four-hour drive from Cairo into the Western Desert and is the usual base for
the Black and White Deserts. Overnight camping among the chalk formations of the White Desert is
the reason most people go.

#### What is there at Siwa Oasis?

Siwa sits near the Libyan border, about eight to ten hours from Cairo, and has a distinct Berber
culture and language. The Oracle Temple of Amun, the mud-brick Shali fortress, salt lakes, hot
springs, and the Great Sand Sea are the draws. It is a commitment to reach and that keeps it quiet.

**Link:** `/siwa-oasis-a-timeless-epic-adventure/`

#### What national parks and reserves are worth visiting?

Ras Muhammad National Park at the southern tip of Sinai, for reef and mangrove. Wadi El Rayan and
Wadi El Hitan in Fayoum — the latter a UNESCO site holding fossil skeletons of early whales. Wadi
El Gemal on the southern Red Sea coast.

**Link:** `/best-ecolodges-in-fayoum/`

#### Where can I go birdwatching in Egypt?

Egypt sits on a major migration flyway. Lake Nasser and the Aswan stretch of the Nile, the Delta
lakes, Wadi El Rayan in Fayoum, and the Red Sea coast during spring and autumn passage are the
productive areas.

**Link:** `/best-ecolodges-in-fayoum/`

#### Are there eco-lodges and sustainable options?

Yes — off-grid lodges in Siwa, Fayoum, and along the southern Red Sea coast, several of which are
built from local materials and staffed locally. Some operators run itineraries built around low
impact and community benefit.

### Food, markets and living culture

#### What Egyptian dishes should I try?

Koshari — rice, lentils, macaroni, chickpeas, tomato sauce, fried onions — is the national dish and
the best cheap meal in the country. Ful medames, slow-cooked fava beans, is breakfast. Ta'ameya is
Egypt's falafel, made with fava rather than chickpeas and greener inside than you expect. Molokhia,
a jute-leaf soup with a distinctive texture, served over rice with chicken or rabbit. Hawawshi,
spiced minced meat baked into bread. Om Ali for dessert.

**Link:** `/the-evolution-of-food-in-egypt/`

#### Can I do a food tour or cooking class?

Yes, in Cairo, Alexandria, Luxor, and Aswan. Cooking classes typically cover koshari, ta'ameya, and
stuffed vegetables. Food walks through Downtown Cairo or Khan El Khalili cover street food you
would otherwise walk past.

**Link:** `/culinary-journey-in-egypt/`

#### Which markets are worth visiting?

Khan El Khalili in Cairo, running since the fourteenth century, for metalwork, glass, spices, and
the coffee houses in its side lanes. Aswan's souk for Nubian crafts, spices, and hibiscus. Luxor's
souk for alabaster. Haggling is expected in all three and is a social exchange rather than a
confrontation.

**Link:** `/a-visit-to-cairos-khan-el-khalili-bazaar/`

#### What are the best souvenirs from Egypt?

Hand-blown Muski glass, inlaid mother-of-pearl boxes, cotton and linen textiles, papyrus from a
workshop that can show you the process, alabaster from Luxor, spices and dried hibiscus, Siwan
silver, and Nubian basketry. Avoid anything presented as a genuine antiquity — exporting antiquities
is illegal and the items on offer are not what they're described as.

#### Can I visit a Nubian village?

Yes. Gharb Soheil and the villages on Elephantine Island near Aswan receive visitors, with
brightly painted houses, distinct architecture, and a culture and language separate from the rest
of Egypt. Going with a Nubian-run operator makes the difference between a visit and a photo stop.

**Link:** `/things-to-do-in-aswan/`

#### Where can I see Coptic Christian heritage?

Old Cairo, principally — the Hanging Church, the Church of St Sergius and Bacchus, the Coptic
Museum, and the Ben Ezra Synagogue in the same compound. Together they cover a religious history
most visitors to Egypt never encounter.

**Link:** `/exploring-coptic-cairo/`

#### Where can I see Islamic architecture?

Historic Cairo is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in its own right. The Mosque of Ibn Tulun, the
Sultan Hassan and Al-Rifa'i mosques facing each other beneath the Citadel, Al-Azhar, and the Mosque
of Amr ibn al-As — the first mosque built in Egypt and in Africa. Al-Muizz Street runs through the
densest concentration of medieval Islamic architecture anywhere.

**Link:** `/must-visit-islamic-places-in-cairo/` · **Status:** Merged from two near-identical legacy entries

#### Where is the nightlife in Egypt?

Cairo's Zamalek and Downtown have the bar and restaurant density, with rooftop venues along the
Nile. Sharm El Sheikh and Hurghada have resort-driven nightlife. Outside these, evenings are more
about coffee houses, shisha, and staying out very late than about clubs.

**Link:** `/places-to-visit-in-cairo-at-night/`

#### What cultural performances can I see?

The Sound and Light shows at Giza, Karnak, and Philae. Tanoura, the Egyptian Sufi whirling
performance, at Wekalet El Ghouri in Cairo. The Cairo Opera House programmes classical Arabic and
Western music. Traditional music venues in Downtown Cairo run late.

**Link:** `/cultural-travel-guides-in-egypt/`

#### Should I hire a local guide?

For the major sites, a licensed Egyptologist guide changes the experience substantially — the
carving on a temple wall is a text, and without someone to read it you're looking at decoration.
Book through a licensed operator rather than at the gate.

**Link:** `/cultural-travel-guides-in-egypt/`

#### Is Egypt good for a family trip?

It works well. Cairo and Giza carry their own appeal for children who arrive already interested in
pyramids. The Red Sea resorts handle the beach and downtime half of a trip. Nile cruises suit
families because the travel happens while everyone is asleep or eating. Pacing matters more than
anywhere — heat and long site days wear children down fast.

**Link:** `/historical-egypt-trips-for-families/`
