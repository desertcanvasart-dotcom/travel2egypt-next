The Giftun Islands are not a secret, and nothing about them is exclusive. They are Hurghada’s standard day at sea: two islands inside a protected marine park about 12 km offshore, roughly an hour out by day boat, and the destination most snorkelling trips from the city have used for decades. What makes the day worth taking is the quality of the reef and the shallow, clear water around the beaches, not any promise of having them to yourself.

Set expectations there and Giftun delivers. Set them at “deserted island” and the boat count at the mooring will disappoint you before lunch.

## Two islands, one park

Giftun Kebir, Big Giftun, is the larger of the two and the closer to Hurghada. Giftun Soraya, Little Giftun, lies further east. Both sit inside the Giftun Islands protected area, where the Egyptian Environmental Affairs Agency limits visitor numbers and prohibits fishing. The islands themselves are low desert: pale sand, scrub and dunes, with the Red Sea Mountains visible on the mainland horizon on a clear morning. The interest is in the water.

## The reefs

The reef walls and shallow coral gardens around the islands are the reason the boats come. Sites vary by season and sea state, and a good skipper chooses them on the morning for coral cover and visibility. Expect hard and soft coral, large shoals of reef fish, parrotfish, angelfish and wrasse close in, the occasional barracuda at a distance, and sea turtles on the quieter mornings. Named sites around the islands include Hamda, Banana Reef and Sha’ab Torfa. This is not a dolphin site; the dolphin trips run to a different reef, and a Giftun day should be chosen for calm reef-and-beach snorkelling rather than for a chance encounter.

## Mahmya and Orange Bay

Two landings account for most beach afternoons. Mahmya is a managed beach on the south side of Big Giftun, reached by boat only, with shade, facilities and sand that is kept clean because someone is paid to keep it that way. Orange Bay is the sand bar between the two islands, the most recognisable shallow-water site on this stretch of coast: knee-deep turquoise water over white sand, a timber jetty, and beach clubs that fill from late morning. Both look like their photographs. Both are shared. By midday, neither is quiet.

## The day itself

The standard shape is fixed and it works: a morning pickup from your hotel, the marina, a crossing of 30 to 45 minutes to the first reef, two or three snorkelling stops with lunch on board, and an afternoon on one of the beaches before the return. Shared yachts run daily and are the sensible choice for most travellers; a private boat buys pace and the right to leave a crowded mooring, not a different sea. The park charges a fee per person, usually collected by the boat operator, and it is worth confirming whether your price includes it.

## Before you book

Giftun is the reliable day, not the remarkable one. Divers who want steel rather than coral should look at the wrecks of Abu Nuhas instead, and anyone hoping for empty sand should know that the park’s protection is what keeps the reef good and the beaches managed, and that both come with company.
