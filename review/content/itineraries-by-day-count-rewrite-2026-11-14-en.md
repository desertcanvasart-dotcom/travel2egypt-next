`000000000001` When you plan a trip to Egypt, the first question isn't where to go but how many days you have. The pyramids, the [[Valley of the Kings]], the Nile, [[Abu Simbel]], the Red Sea, Sinai, the Western Desert: the wish list grows fast, and an itinerary that doesn't match the days leaves you more tired than amazed.

`000000000003` On a short trip, staying with [[Cairo]] and [[Giza]] is more satisfying. A week can join Cairo, [[Luxor]] and [[Aswan]]. With ten days or more, you can add Abu Simbel, a Nile cruise, the Red Sea or Sinai, depending on what the trip is for.

`000000000005` Below are realistic ways to build an Egypt trip for three to fifteen days. The point isn't to add sights but to choose the backbone that fits the days you have.

`000000000007` **[h2]** Before you build the itinerary

`000000000009` Getting around Egypt takes longer than the map suggests. Cairo traffic, airports, waits for domestic flights, the walking at each site and breaks from the heat all limit what one day can hold.

`00000000000b` For a first trip, Cairo, Luxor and Aswan make the steadiest backbone: Cairo for the Giza pyramids and the museum, Luxor for Karnak and the Valley of the Kings, Aswan for Philae and Abu Simbel.

`00000000000d` The Red Sea, Sinai, the Western Desert and [[Alexandria]] all have their appeal, but squeezed into a short trip they mostly add travel. Add a region because you want it in the trip, not because it seems to fit.

`00000000000f` Details for the Grand Egyptian Museum, the sites, domestic flights, visas, tickets and security can change. Check prices, opening hours and entry rules with official sources or a trusted operator when you book and again before you leave.

`000000000011` **[h2]** 3 days: stay with Cairo and Giza

`000000000013` With three days, the realistic plan is Cairo and Giza. Adding Luxor or Aswan looks possible, but travel would take most of the trip.

`000000000015` Day one: arrive and settle into Cairo. If there's time, a gentle walk in Islamic Cairo, along al-Muizz Street or around [[Khan el-Khalili]] shows the layers of the city beyond its ancient sites.

`000000000017` Day two: the Giza pyramids and the Sphinx. Go early in the morning, then spend the afternoon at the museum or resting at the hotel, depending on your energy. Giza, Saqqara and the museum on the same day is too much for a first visit.

`000000000019` Day three: Saqqara and Dahshur, or more of the city. Saqqara traces pyramid-building from the Step Pyramid to Giza. For the city, combine [[Coptic Cairo]], Islamic architecture and the museums.

`00000000001b` Three days is short, but Cairo alone has plenty. Seeing the city and the pyramids properly makes for a more complete trip than forcing in a flight south.

`00000000001d` **[h2]** 4 days: add one major thing to Cairo

`00000000001f` With [[4 days]], add one major thing to Cairo and Giza. The most natural choice is a day at Saqqara, Dahshur and Memphis.

`000000000021` If you only see Giza, the pyramids seem to appear from nowhere. At Saqqara and Dahshur, the Step Pyramid, the Bent Pyramid and the Red Pyramid show how royal tomb-building developed in the Old Kingdom.

`000000000023` The other choice is a day trip or a night in Alexandria. The Mediterranean city feels completely different from Cairo, and the Kom el-Shoqafa catacombs, the Citadel of Qaitbay and the modern [[Bibliotheca Alexandrina]] layer Ptolemaic, Roman and modern history.

`000000000025` Adding Alexandria to four days means less time in Cairo. For a first, site-focused trip, choose Saqqara; for the atmosphere of a Mediterranean city, choose Alexandria.

`000000000027` **[h2]** 5 days: Cairo-based or Aswan-based

`000000000029` With five days, the thinking changes a little. Many people want to add Luxor to Cairo, but with the travel involved, it gets crowded.

`00000000002b` For a first trip, a northern plan of Cairo, Giza, Saqqara and Alexandria works well: the city, the pyramids, the ancient capital and the Mediterranean, without strain.

`00000000002d` If the south is what draws you, five days based in Aswan also works. Philae, the Nubian Museum, [[Elephantine Island]] and Abu Simbel focus on a landscape and a history quite different from Cairo's.

`00000000002f` Five days covering Cairo, Luxor, Aswan and Abu Simbel packs in too much travel. On a short trip, a clear focus matters more than the number of places.

`000000000031` **[h2]** 7 days: connect Cairo and the upper Nile

`000000000033` [[7 days]] is the shortest trip that connects Cairo and Upper Egypt properly. The usual split is two to three days in Cairo and Giza and four to five in Luxor and Aswan.

`000000000035` In Cairo, choose between the Giza pyramids, the museum and Saqqara. It's better to pick what connects to the second half of the trip than to try to see everything in full.

`000000000037` In Luxor, the core is Karnak and [[Luxor Temple]] on the east bank, and the Valley of the Kings, Hatshepsut's temple and the Colossi of Memnon on the west bank. Seeing the whole west bank in one day is tiring, so decide what to see in depth.

`000000000039` In Aswan, the focus is Philae and time by the Nile. Abu Simbel usually means a very early start and needs slack in the plan; fitting it into seven days means lightening Cairo or Luxor.

`00000000003b` **[h2]** 8 days: Abu Simbel fits more easily

`00000000003d` With eight days, Abu Simbel fits more easily alongside Cairo, Luxor and Aswan. Allow more than half a day for the journey.

`00000000003f` Ramesses II's great temple and Nefertari's small temple represent the south of Egypt. They matter both as an expression of ancient royal power and as a symbol of the modern rescue of the sites, and they're worth the time.

`000000000041` One baseline for eight days: two days in Cairo, two in Luxor, two in Aswan, one for Abu Simbel and one for travel and adjustment. With a Nile cruise, the ship replaces the Luxor–Aswan journey.

`000000000043` Even at this length, adding the Red Sea or Sinai is crowded. On a first trip, see the Nile valley properly first.

`000000000045` **[h2]** 10 days: time to go deeper

`000000000047` [[10 days]] makes an Egypt trip much steadier. Cairo, Luxor, Aswan and Abu Simbel connect without strain, with time to go a little deeper at each.

`000000000049` In Cairo, adding Saqqara or Dahshur to Giza makes the history of the pyramids three-dimensional, and time at the museum and in Islamic Cairo shows the depth of the city beyond antiquity.

`00000000004b` In Luxor, instead of packing the west bank into one day, split the Valley of the Kings, Hatshepsut's temple, [[Medinet Habu]] and [[Deir el-Medina]] by interest. Deir el-Medina, the village of the tomb-builders, shows ancient society beyond kings and temples.

`00000000004d` In Aswan, with Philae, the Nubian Museum, Elephantine and Abu Simbel, the days are no longer just travel. Ten days is better spent understanding the main regions more deeply than covering more ground.

`00000000004f` **[h2]** 11 to 12 days: consider the Red Sea or Sinai

`000000000051` With eleven or twelve days, there's room to add the Red Sea or Sinai to the Nile valley. Including both scatters the trip.

`000000000053` For the Red Sea, Hurghada or Marsa Alam are the options. They suit travellers for whom diving, snorkelling or a resort rest is part of the trip, and a few days by the sea after a long run of sites helps your energy too.

`000000000055` For Sinai, it's the southern mountains around St. Catherine, or Sharm el-Sheikh. Conditions and safety vary by area, so always check the latest travel advice and the local situation before planning.

`000000000057` An eleven- or twelve-day trip is easiest built on Cairo, Luxor, Aswan and Abu Simbel, followed by two nights on the Red Sea. Including Sinai needs more careful checks on the route and local conditions.

`000000000059` **[h2]** 15 days: see Egypt by region

`00000000005b` With [[15 days]], it becomes easier to travel Egypt as a country of regions rather than a chain of sights. Cairo, Giza, Luxor, Aswan and Abu Simbel can be combined with the Red Sea, Sinai, Alexandria or the Western Desert without strain.

`00000000005d` One way to build it: three days in Cairo and Giza, three in Luxor, three in Aswan and Abu Simbel, three on the Red Sea, and three for travel and adjustment. That gives time to the sites, the Nile, the sea and the city.

`00000000005f` For the Western Desert (Bahariya Oasis, the White Desert and the Black Desert), trips need a private car, permits, local arrangements and safety checks, so plan them separately from ordinary travel between cities.

`000000000061` With fifteen days and more places within reach, a theme helps: tracing ancient Egypt in chronological order, feeling the Nile and the desert, or combining sites and the sea. With a theme, the choices in the itinerary stop wavering.

`000000000063` **[h2]** If you include a Nile cruise

`000000000065` A Nile cruise runs between Luxor and Aswan, stopping at Edfu, Kom Ombo and Esna. It combines travel and accommodation, and not having to move your luggage repeatedly is a big advantage.

`000000000067` Large cruise ships have full facilities and are straightforward for a first trip, though how long they stop at each site depends on the ship. Dahabiyas are quieter and slower, and conditions and prices vary widely from boat to boat.

`000000000069` When choosing a cruise, look beyond the ship's name: check which sites you actually visit, how the guiding works, and whether the departure and arrival fit your itinerary. A cruise is convenient, but it gives up some flexibility.

`00000000006b` **[h2]** Thinking by day count

`00000000006d` For short trips of three to five days, focusing on one region works best: Cairo and Giza, or Aswan and Abu Simbel. Make the backbone clear.

`00000000006f` For seven to ten days, combine Cairo and Upper Egypt. How you handle Luxor and Aswan decides the quality of the trip, so decide early: a cruise, or a private car and domestic flights, and where you'll rest.

`000000000071` For eleven to fifteen days, widen the regions. There's room for the Red Sea, Sinai, Alexandria and the Western Desert, but no need to include them all. Egypt rewards a return visit, and deciding what to see in depth this time makes for a more satisfying trip than packing everything in.

`000000000073` **[h2]** What makes a good itinerary

`000000000075` A good Egypt itinerary isn't measured by the number of sights. Light travel days, no pushing through the hottest hours, slack in both the cities and the sites, and safe connections, such as returning to Cairo the day before you fly home, are what matter.

`000000000077` The pyramids, the Valley of the Kings, Karnak, Philae and Abu Simbel are each a powerful experience. Rushing through them one after another blurs the impressions.

`000000000079` With fewer days, narrow the focus; with more, feel the differences between regions. Moving between those two ways of thinking is what gives an Egypt trip its shape.
