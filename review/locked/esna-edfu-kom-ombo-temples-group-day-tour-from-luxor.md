## Opening

Between Luxor and Aswan, the Ptolemies and the Romans after them built temples that still have their roofs, their painted ceilings and, in places, their original reds and blues. This shared day connects three of them, Esna, Edfu and Kom Ombo, and ends in Aswan rather than looping back. It moves you as well as shows you: a one-way drive south with three serious stops, in a small group of four to eight.

---

## Why the day runs this way

You leave Luxor at 07:00 and head south, stopping in the order the road gives you: Esna after about 55 kilometres, Edfu 60 kilometres on, then Kom Ombo, 45 kilometres short of Aswan. The driving adds up to about four hours. Edfu gets the longest visit, about an hour and three quarters, because it shows most completely of the three how an Egyptian temple worked; Esna and Kom Ombo get about an hour each.

## Your day

### Morning · Esna and Edfu

**07:00 · Pickup from your hotel**
Your guide meets the group in Luxor, and you set off south along the Nile in an air-conditioned vehicle. Esna is about an hour away.

**The Temple of Khnum, Esna**
Sunk some nine metres below the modern town, which has simply grown up around it. The hall is Roman, from the 1st and 2nd centuries AD, built for Khnum, the ram-headed god who shaped people on a potter’s wheel. Its 24 columns carry some of the latest hieroglyphs ever carved, as late as the 3rd century AD, under a ceiling whose colour has recently been restored. About an hour here.
*Sites:* Temple of Khnum, Esna

**The Temple of Horus, Edfu**
About an hour further south. Begun under Ptolemy III in 237 BC and finished in 57 BC, then buried under sand and houses until Auguste Mariette uncovered it nearly intact in the 1860s. Pass the 36-metre pylon and its granite falcon into ever-darker halls: no site shows more clearly how an Egyptian temple worked. The walls tell the myth of Horus and his battles with Set. This is the stop that justifies the day.
*Sites:* Temple of Horus, Edfu

### Midday · Kom Ombo and lunch

**The Temple of Kom Ombo**
A temple built double from the start: two sets of halls and sanctuaries side by side, for Horus the Elder and for Sobek the crocodile god, at a bend of the river where crocodiles once gathered. Next door, a small museum of mummified crocodiles makes the gods startlingly physical.
*Sites:* Temple of Kom Ombo · Crocodile Museum

**Lunch at a local restaurant (included)**
On the road, between the temples and Aswan.

### Afternoon · Aswan

**Afternoon · Arrival in Aswan**
The last stretch to Aswan takes about 45 minutes. The day ends there: you stay on in Aswan.
