## Opening

Aswan’s identity is shaped as much by Nubia as by Egypt, and this private half day is built around that difference. Two close, quietly rewarding stops: the Nubian Museum, which tells one of the ancient world’s least-known stories, from prehistoric rock art to the kingdom of Kush and the villages drowned by Lake Nasser, and the working market where Aswan does its shopping, full of loose spices, cotton and Nubian silver.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The tour runs in the afternoon and into the evening, after the midday heat; in summer, Aswan passes 40°C. The museum stays open into the evening, so there’s no rush. It comes first, to give the market its context; the market is at its liveliest as the day cools, and dinner closes the visit. The two are close together, so there’s little time in the car. The pace is gentler than Luxor, and that’s the point.

## Your day

### Afternoon and evening · The museum, the market and dinner

**Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you in a private air-conditioned car.

**The Nubian Museum**
Opened in 1997 with UNESCO, in a building by Mahmoud El-Hakim that borrows from Nubian construction: thick walls, deep windows, ochre render. In the garden, a scale model of the valley down to Sudan sets the scene. Inside, the story runs from rock art of around 6000 BC through Pharaonic Nubia, Kush and Meroë, Christian and Islamic times to today, with gold, ceramics and objects saved from sites now under the lake. Allow 90 minutes to two hours.
*Sites:* Nubian Museum

**Aswan’s local market**
Parallel to the Corniche, the market Aswanis actually use: spices by weight (cumin, coriander, dried mango powder, hibiscus for karkade), cloth, household goods and, near the Nile end, Nubian jewellery, painted leather and soapstone carvings. Bargain politely and be ready to walk away. The karkade here is far better than the airport packets.
*Sites:* Aswan souk

Drinks aren’t included.
