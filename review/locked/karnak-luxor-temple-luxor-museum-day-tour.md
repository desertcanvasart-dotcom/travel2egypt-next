## Opening

Karnak, Luxor Temple and the Luxor Museum are the three strongest reasons to spend a day on Luxor’s East Bank, and this private day takes in all three with an Egyptologist. Two open-air temple complexes in the morning, then a museum that shows you the statues and objects those temples were built around, and a walk through Luxor’s local market to finish. It’s a full day, for people who want the reasons behind what they’re looking at, not just the photograph.

---

## Why the day runs this way

Karnak comes first, before the heat builds in its open courts. In summer, Luxor is brutally hot by mid-morning, and the early start is part of the deal. From Karnak you follow the Avenue of Sphinxes south to Luxor Temple, the route the ancient processions took between the two. The museum comes in the afternoon, out of the sun, and it makes more sense after the temples than before them; the local market closes the day. Because the tour is private, the time at each stop is yours: more temple, less museum, or the reverse.

## Your day

### Morning · Karnak and Luxor Temple

**Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you early.

**Karnak**
Less a temple than a religious city, built over roughly 1,500 years. You enter the Precinct of Amun-Ra through pylons that grow older as you move in, to the Great Hypostyle Hall of Seti I and Ramses II: 134 sandstone columns in 16 rows, the central ones nearly 21 metres high. Beyond it lie the obelisks of Thutmose I and Hatshepsut, the sacred lake and the Festival Temple of Thutmose III. An hour here feels preliminary; two begins to feel right.
*Sites:* Karnak · Great Hypostyle Hall · Sacred Lake · Festival Temple of Thutmose III

**The Avenue of Sphinxes**
The 2.7-kilometre processional road between Karnak and Luxor Temple, lined with ram-headed sphinxes at one end and human-headed ones at the other, reopened in 2021 after excavation and restoration. Walking a section of it shows how the two temples belonged together.
*Sites:* Avenue of Sphinxes

**Luxor Temple**
Built mainly under Amenhotep III and extended by Ramses II, whose entrance pylon carries the Battle of Kadesh. Of the two obelisks that stood in front of it, one remains; the other has been in the Place de la Concorde in Paris since 1836. The mosque of Abu Haggag sits in the north-east corner, built when the precinct had silted up to roof level. The temple never entirely went out of use.
*Sites:* Luxor Temple · Mosque of Abu Haggag

### Afternoon · The Luxor Museum and the market

**The Luxor Museum**
Small, and carefully edited: where the Cairo museum overwhelms, this one lets each object register. Look for the reconstructed Akhenaten wall from a dismantled Aten temple at Karnak; the statues found beneath Luxor Temple’s courtyard in 1989, among them a polished quartzite Amenhotep III in near-perfect condition; and the chariots and weapons from the reigns of Ahmose I and Thutmose III.
*Sites:* Luxor Museum

**Luxor’s local market (complimentary)**
To finish, a walk through the town’s market, with its spices, fabrics and everyday trade: the living city around the temples. The visit is our gift; anything you buy is your own.
*Sites:* Luxor market
