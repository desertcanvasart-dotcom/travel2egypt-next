## Opening

Cairo is several cities stacked on one another, and this private day moves through them in order: the royal mummies at the National Museum of Egyptian Civilisation, the churches and synagogue of Coptic Cairo built over a Roman fortress, then the Khan El Khalili bazaar. It’s one museum read deeply rather than three skimmed, with an Egyptologist to connect each era to the next.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The museum comes first, while you’re fresh, because the mummies deserve your full attention. Old Cairo is ten minutes away, and Islamic Cairo a short drive beyond, so the driving between sites is short and the day flows from the oldest layer to the most recent. Cairo’s traffic still stretches timings; your guide will manage it, but bring some patience.

## Your day

### Morning · The National Museum of Egyptian Civilisation

**09:00 · Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you in a private air-conditioned car for the twenty- to thirty-minute drive to Fustat.

**The Royal Mummies Hall**
The mummies of New Kingdom kings and queens, moved here from Tahrir in 2021 in a procession that shut central Cairo, lie in a hushed hall designed to feel like a tomb. Seqenenre Taa II still shows the wounds of a battlefield death in the Hyksos wars; Ramses II lived to about ninety; Hatshepsut ruled as pharaoh for more than two decades. Your guide explains the forensic work behind the identifications.

**The main galleries**
Egyptian civilisation from the prehistoric Nazlet Khater skeleton through Pharaonic, Coptic and Islamic times, and a textile gallery of some six hundred pieces. About two hours in the museum altogether.
*Sites:* National Museum of Egyptian Civilisation

### Midday · Coptic Cairo and lunch

**The Hanging Church**
Built over the gatehouse of the Roman fortress of Babylon, so its nave seems suspended, under a wooden ceiling shaped like an upturned ship’s hull, with more than a hundred icons.

**Ben Ezra and Abu Serga**
The Ben Ezra Synagogue, where tradition places the finding of the infant Moses, and where the Cairo Geniza came to light in the 19th century, changing how scholars read medieval Jewish and Arabic life. Abu Serga, one of Cairo’s oldest churches, keeps the crypt where Coptic tradition says the Holy Family sheltered.
*Sites:* Hanging Church · Ben Ezra Synagogue · Abu Serga

**Lunch at a local restaurant (included)**
Drinks aren’t included.

### Afternoon · Khan El Khalili

**Khan El Khalili**
A working market since the 1380s, selling spices, gold, alabaster, textiles, perfume oils and souvenirs of every quality. Your guide helps you navigate honestly. If your energy holds, sit for a few minutes at El Fishawy coffeehouse.
*Sites:* Khan El Khalili · El Fishawy

**Back to your hotel**
Early evening.
