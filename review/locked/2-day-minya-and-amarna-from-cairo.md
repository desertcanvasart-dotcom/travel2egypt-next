## Opening

Most visitors never leave the Cairo–Luxor–Aswan triangle, so Middle Egypt goes almost unseen. This private two-day trip drives south to Minya for four sites few people outside Egyptology know, and all of them reward real attention: Beni Hassan, Hermopolis, Tuna el-Gebel and Tell el-Amarna.

---

## Why this route works

Beni Hassan’s painted Middle Kingdom tombs, and Amarna, the city Akhenaten built for a single god and that was abandoned after his death, are places you’ll likely have to yourselves. Two days let you see all four sites on the first, then go back to Tuna el-Gebel and Amarna on the second for whatever deserved a longer look.

## Your two days

### Day 1 · Cairo → Minya
**Beni Hassan, Hermopolis, Tuna el-Gebel and Amarna**

An early pickup from your Cairo hotel and the drive south, three to four hours depending on traffic.

**Morning.** Beni Hassan, a clifftop cemetery on the east bank with thirty-nine tombs of Middle Kingdom governors, twelve of them open, painted with wrestlers, hunters and daily life.

**Afternoon.** Hermopolis, ancient Khmun, city of Thoth, with the columns of a basilica built from temple blocks. Then Tuna el-Gebel, its necropolis, with streets of tombs, catacombs of sacred ibises and baboons, and the tomb of the priest Petosiris, from around 300 BC, which blends Egyptian and Greek art. Lunch at a local restaurant along the way.

**Late afternoon.** Tell el-Amarna, Akhenaten’s "Horizon of the Aten", built around 1346 BC. The courtiers’ cliff tombs show the royal family under the rays of the Aten, each ray ending in a small hand. Then to your hotel in Minya for dinner.

**The day:** Beni Hassan · Hermopolis (Ashmunein) · Tuna el-Gebel · Tomb of Petosiris · Tell el-Amarna
**Overnight:** Omar El Khayam Al Minya Hotel
**Meals:** Lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Pickup from your Cairo hotel · private air-conditioned car
**Pace:** A long day, with uneven ground at Beni Hassan

### Day 2 · Minya → Cairo
**A second look at Tuna el-Gebel and Amarna**

Not repetition, a second pass. Both sites are big enough that the first visit shows you where to go back. Breakfast at your hotel, then the deeper catacombs at Tuna el-Gebel in the morning, and the northern or southern tombs at Amarna at midday, with the boundary stelae Akhenaten set around his city. Lunch at a local restaurant, then the drive back, arriving at your Cairo hotel by early evening.
