## Opening

Four days on the Nile from Aswan to Luxor, privately guided. You begin at Aswan, ancient Egypt’s southern frontier, stop at the river temples of Kom Ombo and Edfu, and finish at Luxor, ancient Thebes, the religious capital of the New Kingdom. It’s a short cruise with a full programme, and the days below show exactly how it runs.

---

## Why this route works

Aswan and Luxor bracket the main run of temples in Upper Egypt, and the ship sails north with the current, linking them in order. Aswan comes first, with the dam that reshaped everything downstream. Then the temples of Kom Ombo and Edfu mid-river, and Luxor’s two banks to close. Four days is a tight fit, and the itinerary doesn’t pretend otherwise. The ship is your hotel throughout, and your Egyptologist guides you at every site.

## Your four days

> **Your ship sets the exact order.** Cruise ships between Aswan and Luxor follow slightly different sailing schedules. Some visit Kom Ombo and Edfu together on Day 2, as shown below. Others stop at Kom Ombo on the evening of Day 1 and at Edfu on the morning of Day 2. The sites are the same either way, and your final itinerary confirms the order for your ship.

### Day 1 · Aswan
**Board the cruise: Philae, the High Dam, the Unfinished Obelisk and a felucca**

**Morning.** You’re met at Aswan airport or the railway station, or collected from your hotel in Aswan. The sightseeing starts early, while it’s cooler. First Philae, the temple complex of Isis, reached by motorboat. It was moved to its present island in the 1970s, in the same UNESCO campaign that saved Abu Simbel, and it was among the last temples where the old religion was practised, into the sixth century AD. Then the Aswan High Dam, completed in 1970, which created Lake Nasser, one of the largest artificial reservoirs on earth.

**Midday.** You board the ship for lunch.

**Afternoon.** The Unfinished Obelisk, still lying in its granite quarry. It was abandoned when it cracked; finished, it would have stood about 42 metres tall and weighed an estimated 1,200 tonnes. The day ends with a felucca sail around Kitchener’s Island.

### Day 2 · On the Nile
**Kom Ombo and Edfu**

The ship reaches Kom Ombo first: a Ptolemaic temple built for two gods at once, its whole plan doubled (two entrances, two hypostyle halls, two sanctuaries), one half for Sobek the crocodile god and one for Horus the Elder. A small museum beside it holds mummified crocodiles found in the precinct.

Edfu follows in the afternoon. The Temple of Horus is the most completely preserved temple in Egypt, built between 237 and 57 BC, with its 36-metre pylon, walls, roof and inner sanctuary largely intact. Few places give a clearer picture of how a working temple was organised. The ship then sails north towards Luxor through the evening and night.

**The day:** Temple of Kom Ombo · Crocodile Museum · Temple of Horus at Edfu · sailing to Luxor
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, sailing to Luxor
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By cruise
**Pace:** Two temple stops, then the river

### Day 3 · Luxor
**The East Bank: Karnak and Luxor Temple** ⚑

**Morning.** Karnak, less a temple than a precinct, built and extended by successive pharaohs over roughly 1,500 years. Its centrepiece is the Great Hypostyle Hall: 134 sandstone columns, the tallest 21 metres high, covering about 5,000 square metres. It rewards slow movement rather than a march through.

**Afternoon.** Luxor Temple, about three kilometres south along the Avenue of Sphinxes, the processional road reopened in November 2021. It’s smaller and more unified than Karnak, built mainly under Amenhotep III and Ramses II.

**Evening.** Aboard, moored at Luxor.

**The day:** Karnak · Avenue of Sphinxes · Luxor Temple
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, moored at Luxor
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner

### Day 4 · Luxor
**The West Bank, lunch, then departure** ⚑

**Early morning.** The Valley of the Kings holds more than sixty tombs, many of them royal, cut into the limestone from around 1539 BC. Nearly all were robbed in antiquity. Tutankhamun’s survived because it was missed, its entrance buried under later debris until Howard Carter found it in 1922. Your ticket includes three of the standard tombs. Then the Mortuary Temple of Hatshepsut at Deir el-Bahari, three colonnaded terraces against the cliff, and the Colossi of Memnon, two 18-metre seated figures of Amenhotep III standing alone where his mortuary temple once stood.

**Midday.** Lunch at a local restaurant.

**Afternoon.** Transfer to Luxor airport or railway station, depending on your onward plans, with meet and assist.

## Your stay and travel

**On the Nile:** three nights aboard a five-star Nile cruise ship, your hotel throughout, full board from lunch on Day 1 to breakfast on Day 4. Every cabin faces outward, and the ship has a pool and sun deck. Deluxe and Luxury ships are available as upgrades.

**Arriving and leaving:** meet and assist on arrival in Aswan, by flight or train, or pickup from your Aswan hotel. Meet and assist again for your departure from Luxor, by air or rail.

**Every day:** a private Egyptologist with your party, private air-conditioned transfers, and a bottle of water per person on sightseeing days. The trip is private, operated for your party alone.

**On the water:** a motorboat to Philae and a felucca sail around Kitchener’s Island.
