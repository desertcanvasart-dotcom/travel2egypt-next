## Opening

A first look at Egypt for families, in eight days: Cairo's museum and old city, a night train south, both banks of Luxor, three nights on the Red Sea, and the pyramids of Giza saved for the last full day. The beach sits in the middle, where a family needs it.

---

## Why this route works

The demanding days sit either side of the Red Sea. Cairo and Luxor come first, while everyone is fresh; the sleeper train turns the journey south into a night rather than a day. Hurghada is three hours from Luxor by road, so the temples hand straight over to the sea. The flight back to Cairo leaves Giza for last, so the pyramids are the last thing you see before you fly home.

### Day 1 · Cairo
**Arrival**

You're met at Cairo airport and taken to your hotel. The evening is kept light on purpose: check in, rest after the flight, and leave the city outside until the family is ready for it. The sightseeing starts tomorrow, with breakfast at the hotel.

**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Travel:** Meet and greet at the airport, private transfer to the hotel

### Day 2 · Cairo → the night train
**The Grand Egyptian Museum, Old Cairo, the Citadel and Khan El Khalili**

**Morning.** The Grand Egyptian Museum, beside the Giza plateau, where the complete Tutankhamun collection is shown together: a first look at the objects before you see the tombs and temples they came from later in the week.

**Midday.** Lunch at a local restaurant.

**Afternoon.** The Hanging Church in Old Cairo, its nave built over the gatehouse of a Roman fortress. Then the Citadel of Saladin and the Mosque of Muhammad Ali, completed in the 1840s, and Khan El Khalili, trading since the 14th century. In the evening you board the sleeper train for Luxor, with dinner in your cabin.

**The day:** Grand Egyptian Museum · Hanging Church · Citadel of Saladin · Mosque of Muhammad Ali · Khan El Khalili
**Overnight:** Sleeper train
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner (on the train)
**Travel:** Private transfers · overnight train, Cairo → Luxor
**Pace:** A full day, then the train

### Day 3 · Luxor · the West Bank
**The Colossi, Hatshepsut and the Valley of the Kings**

The train reaches Luxor early in the morning. The West Bank first: the Colossi of Memnon, two seated figures of Amenhotep III about 18 metres tall. Hatshepsut's three terraces at Deir el-Bahari, built for the woman who ruled as pharaoh for some twenty years. Then the Valley of the Kings, more than sixty royal tombs cut into the cliffs, their walls painted with the journey of the dead king through the underworld. Check in at your hotel in the afternoon.

**The day:** Colossi of Memnon · Temple of Hatshepsut · Valley of the Kings
**Overnight:** Luxor hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast (on the train) · lunch
**Travel:** Private transfers

**Optional on this day:**
- The tombs of Tutankhamun, Seti I and Ramses VI: extra tickets

### Day 4 · Luxor → Hurghada
**Karnak, then east to the Red Sea**

Karnak, a temple city built and extended over about 1,500 years, with the Great Hypostyle Hall of 134 columns up to 21 metres high. Walk it without hurrying. Then you leave the Nile Valley and cross the Eastern Desert to Hurghada, about three hours, the green fields giving way to open desert and then the coast. Check in to your resort by the evening.

**The day:** Karnak · Great Hypostyle Hall
**Overnight:** Red Sea resort, Hurghada, all-inclusive
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers · private car, Luxor → Hurghada

### Day 5 · Hurghada
**The reef**

A day to meet the Red Sea. Snorkelling over the coral gardens, windsurfing on the steady breeze, or a glass-bottom boat for younger children who would rather stay dry. For certified divers, diving. Activities with their own age or certification rules are flagged when you book. Or the pool and the beach.

**The day:** your choice
**Overnight:** Red Sea resort, Hurghada, all-inclusive
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Pace:** As much or as little as you like

### Day 6 · Hurghada
**A day left open**

A day with nothing planned, on purpose. Go back to the water, use the resort, walk Hurghada's old town, El Dahar, or do very little. A trip of this length needs one unstructured day, and this is it.

**Overnight:** Red Sea resort, Hurghada, all-inclusive
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Pace:** As much or as little as you like

### Day 7 · Hurghada → Giza
**The pyramids, and a Nile dinner cruise**

A morning flight back to Cairo, and on to the Giza plateau: the pyramids of Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure, built around 2500 BC. You go inside the pyramid of Khafre or Menkaure, subject to site rules and availability on the day. Then the panorama viewpoint, where the three pyramids line up in one frame, and the Great Sphinx and the Valley Temple below. In the evening, a farewell dinner cruise on the Nile, the lit city passing on both banks.

**The day:** Giza pyramids · inside Khafre or Menkaure · panorama viewpoint · Great Sphinx · Valley Temple · Nile dinner cruise
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast (at the resort) · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Domestic flight Hurghada → Cairo (not included) · private transfers

**Optional on this day:**
- Inside the Great Pyramid of Khufu: extra ticket, subject to availability and site rules on the day
- A camel ride on the plateau: extra cost. We work with handlers we know, whose animals are in good condition, and the ride is entirely your choice.

## Is this journey for you?

**A good fit if you:**
- are a family visiting Egypt for the first time, with school-age children or older
- want the headline monuments and a real Red Sea break inside eight days

**Worth knowing:**
- Day 2 is long: a full day in Cairo, then the night train. Sleeper cabins are compact; pack an overnight bag you can reach.
- Days 5 and 6 have no guide; your resort is the base.
- The pyramid interior means crouching in a narrow passage.
- Diving and some Red Sea activities have their own age and fitness rules.
- Dress modestly for the church and the mosque: shoulders and knees covered, and shoes come off in the mosque.
