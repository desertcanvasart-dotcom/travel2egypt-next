## Opening

Egypt’s classic itinerary in eight days, with Cairo at both ends: the pyramids, a flight south, a Nile cruise from Luxor to Aswan, and a flight back for the Grand Egyptian Museum, the Citadel and Khan El Khalili. Your own Egyptologist throughout, and a private cabin on a five-star cruise ship.

---

## Why this route works

The Nile runs from south to north, and Egyptian civilisation ran along it. You start at the monuments the whole world recognises, follow the river that made them possible to Aswan, where it narrows and slows, and finish at the museum that now holds what was carried out of the sites you’ve just walked through. Seeing the objects after the places makes both more legible.

### Day 2 · Cairo
**Giza, Memphis and Saqqara**

The opening chapter of Egyptian history in one day. Giza, where the pyramids of Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure have stood since around 2500 BC, with the Great Sphinx below them facing east. Then south to Memphis, first capital of a united Egypt, with the fallen colossus of Ramses II and the great alabaster sphinx. Last, Saqqara, where the Step Pyramid of Djoser, built around 2650 BC, is where the pyramid was invented. Seeing the experiment after the finished thing makes both easier to read.

**The day:** Giza pyramids · Great Sphinx · Memphis · Saqqara · Step Pyramid of Djoser
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch
**Travel:** Private transfers

**Optional on this day:**
- Inside one of the pyramids: extra ticket

### Day 3 · Cairo → Luxor
**Karnak, and Luxor Temple after dark**

A short flight south to Luxor, ancient Thebes. You board your cruise ship for lunch, then go straight to Karnak, less a temple than a temple city, built and extended over about 1,500 years; its Great Hypostyle Hall has 134 columns up to 21 metres high. As evening comes, Luxor Temple, at the end of the Avenue of Sphinxes, seen after dark with the columns lit from below and the crowds thinned.

**The day:** Karnak · Great Hypostyle Hall · Avenue of Sphinxes · Luxor Temple by night
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Luxor
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Domestic flight Cairo → Luxor · private transfers

### Day 4 · Luxor · the West Bank
**The Valley of the Kings, Hatshepsut and the Colossi**

The land of the dead. The Valley of the Kings, more than sixty royal tombs, their corridors still bright with the journey through the afterlife; Tutankhamun’s is the most famous, but the tombs of Ramses III, Seti I and Thutmose III show royal burial on its proper scale. Then Hatshepsut’s three terraces at Deir el-Bahari, built for the woman who ruled as pharaoh for some twenty years, and the Colossi of Memnon, two seated figures of Amenhotep III.

**The day:** Valley of the Kings · Temple of Hatshepsut · Colossi of Memnon
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers

**Optional on this day:**
- The tombs of Tutankhamun, Seti I and Ramses VI: extra tickets

### Day 5 · On the Nile → Aswan
**Edfu, Kom Ombo and a felucca at Aswan**

A travelling day in the best sense. Edfu and the Temple of Horus, built between 237 and 57 BC, with its walls, roof and inner sanctuary still intact. Then Kom Ombo, built for Sobek the crocodile and Horus the Elder in exact mirror image. The day ends on the water at Aswan, in a felucca around Kitchener’s Island as the light drops.

**The day:** Temple of Horus at Edfu · Temple of Kom Ombo · felucca around Kitchener’s Island
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Aswan
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By cruise · felucca

### Day 6 · Aswan
**Philae, the High Dam and the Unfinished Obelisk**

Philae, on Agilkia Island, where the Temple of Isis was moved block by block in the 1970s to save it from Lake Nasser. Then the High Dam, completed in 1970, which tamed the annual flood, generates power and created the lake, with consequences still being reckoned with. Last, the Unfinished Obelisk, about 42 metres long, abandoned in its granite quarry.

**The day:** Temple of Philae · Aswan High Dam · Unfinished Obelisk
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers · boat to Philae

### Day 7 · Aswan → Cairo
**The Grand Egyptian Museum, the Citadel and Khan El Khalili**

You fly back to Cairo. The Grand Egyptian Museum holds the complete contents of Tutankhamun’s tomb, and after the sites you’ve walked through, seeing what they once held is a more intimate kind of encounter. Then up to the Citadel of Saladin and the Mosque of Muhammad Ali, its Ottoman silhouette dominating the skyline, and finally Khan El Khalili, trading since the 14th century.

**The day:** Grand Egyptian Museum · Citadel of Saladin · Mosque of Muhammad Ali · Khan El Khalili
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast (on the cruise) · lunch
**Travel:** Domestic flight Aswan → Cairo · private transfers
