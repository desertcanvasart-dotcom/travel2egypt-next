## Opening

The Giza Plateau, the Egyptian Museum on Tahrir Square and Khan El Khalili are three different worlds: one monumental, one curated, one all senses and trade. In sequence, with your own Egyptologist, they make a fair portrait of Cairo across five thousand years. It’s the classic three-act Cairo day, and it works as a one-day stopover on the way to Luxor or Aswan too.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The plateau comes first, before the heat: from late May to September, Cairo passes 38°C by noon, and the pyramids have no shade. Lunch follows, then the museum fills the afternoon indoors, and the bazaar comes last, when you can take it at your own speed. Lunch is the one long pause, so the day is full, though not punishing.

## Your day

### Morning · The Giza Plateau

**Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you in a private air-conditioned car.

**The three pyramids**
Khufu’s Great Pyramid, begun around 2560 BC: 146 metres high when finished, an estimated 2.3 million blocks averaging about 2.5 tonnes each. Khafre’s looks taller because it stands on higher ground, and near its top its original Tura limestone casing still shows how all three once shone. Menkaure’s is much smaller, and the contrast is its own history lesson.
*Sites:* Great Pyramid of Khufu · Pyramid of Khafre · Pyramid of Menkaure

**Inside a pyramid (included).** You go inside the pyramid of Khafre or Menkaure, subject to site rules and availability on the day.

**The Great Sphinx**
Cut from the bedrock, 73 metres long and 20 high, most probably under Khafre. Who built it, and when, is still debated, and your guide’s answer is more interesting than a confident one would be.
*Sites:* Great Sphinx

**Optional extras, if you’ve chosen them**
- Inside the Great Pyramid of Khufu: extra ticket, subject to availability and site rules on the day
- A camel ride: extra cost. We work with handlers we know, whose animals are in good condition, and the ride is entirely your choice.

### Afternoon · The Egyptian Museum and Khan El Khalili

**The Egyptian Museum, Tahrir Square**
The 1902 building, not the Grand Egyptian Museum. Tutankhamun’s treasure is now at the GEM and the royal mummies at the NMEC, but more than 100,000 objects remain, from the Narmer Palette and Old Kingdom statuary to Middle Kingdom models and Roman-era portraits, in galleries less polished and more archival than the new museum. Your guide focuses the visit, about two hours, around what you most want to see.
*Sites:* Egyptian Museum

**Khan El Khalili**
Founded in 1382 and trading ever since: spices, textiles, jewellery, copper, papyrus, alabaster and silver cartouches, of very mixed quality. Your guide gives you a short primer on bargaining before you go in, and can point out the Mamluk façades of Al-Muizz Street if you have time.
*Sites:* Khan El Khalili · Al-Muizz Street
