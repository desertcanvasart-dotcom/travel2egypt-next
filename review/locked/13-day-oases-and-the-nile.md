## Opening

Most journeys treat Egypt as a corridor along the Nile. This one goes sideways first: west from Cairo into the Western Desert, through the oases of Bahariya, Dakhla and Kharga and a night camped in the White Desert, and only then east to Luxor, where a four-night cruise carries you up the river to Aswan, with Abu Simbel at the end.

---

## Why this route works

The desert comes first, while you're fresh for the long drives, and the river second, when four nights on board let someone else do the travelling. The oases run in a chain from Bahariya south to Kharga, so the road never doubles back, and from Kharga it's a single drive east to Luxor, where you board the cruise the same day.

### Day 2 · Giza, Memphis and Saqqara
**Where the pyramid was perfected, and where it was invented**

Giza first: the pyramids of Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure, built around 2500 BC, and the Sphinx below them. Then south to Memphis, first capital of a united Egypt around 3100 BC, with the fallen colossus of Ramses II and an alabaster sphinx, and Saqqara, where the Step Pyramid of Djoser, built around 2650 BC, shows the experiment that made Giza possible.

**The day:** Giza pyramids · Great Sphinx · Memphis · Saqqara · Step Pyramid of Djoser
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers

**Optional on this day:**
- Inside one of the pyramids: extra ticket

### Day 3 · Cairo → Bahariya
**The first oasis**

About four hours southwest across the desert to Bahariya, green with palms and olives in its depression, with hot springs and the small town of Bawiti at its heart. One collective ticket opens the oasis sites: a few of the gilded mummies found in 1996 in what is now called the Valley of the Golden Mummies, the painted Late Period tombs of Bannentiu and Djed-Amun-ef-ankh, and the Temple of Alexander the Great.

**The day:** Golden Mummies · Tombs of Bannentiu and Djed-Amun-ef-ankh · Temple of Alexander
**Overnight:** Bahariya hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private car, Cairo → Bahariya

### Day 4 · The Black Desert → the White Desert
**Volcanic hills, Crystal Mountain, and a night under the formations**

By 4×4 south through the Black Desert, a field of dark, basalt-capped hills; Crystal Mountain, a small outcrop of crystals that catches the light; and the Agabat valley, its pale spires best walked rather than photographed from the road. In the afternoon, the White Desert National Park: chalk carved by the wind into Mushroom Rock, towers and columns, long shadows at dusk. Camp among them, after everyone else has gone.

**The day:** Black Desert · Crystal Mountain · Agabat · White Desert National Park
**Overnight:** Camping in the White Desert
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private 4×4

### Day 5 · White Desert → Dakhla
**Farafra, the long road south, Deir el-Hagar and Qasr al-Dakhla**

Breakfast in the desert, then the long drive south through Farafra, the smallest of the oases, to Dakhla, with its rice fields, palm groves and springs below a pink escarpment. The road comes in from the west, past Deir el-Hagar, a Roman-period sandstone temple to Amun, Mut and Khonsu with the names of emperors from Nero to Hadrian on its walls, and Qasr al-Dakhla, a medieval mud-brick town of covered lanes, carved wooden lintels and an old minaret, still partly lived in.

**The day:** Farafra · Deir el-Hagar · Qasr al-Dakhla
**Overnight:** Dakhla hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private car, White Desert → Dakhla
**Pace:** A long drive

### Day 6 · Dakhla → Kharga
**The Temple of Hibis and the chapels of Bagawat**

East to Kharga, the largest of the New Valley oases, at the northern end of the Forty Days Road, the old caravan route from Sudan. The Temple of Hibis, a Late Period temple to Amun, its walls carved largely under the Persian king Darius I. Then Bagawat, a hillside of hundreds of mud-brick Christian funerary chapels from the 4th century AD on, some with early painted domes.

**The day:** Temple of Hibis · Bagawat Necropolis
**Overnight:** Kharga hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private car, Dakhla → Kharga

### Day 7 · Kharga → Luxor
**East to the Nile, Karnak and Luxor Temple, and board the cruise**

An early start for the drive east out of the desert to the Nile at Luxor, and on board your cruise for lunch. In the afternoon, Karnak, a temple city added to over fifteen centuries, with the Great Hypostyle Hall of 134 columns up to 21 metres, and Luxor Temple at the end of the Avenue of Sphinxes.

**The day:** Karnak · Great Hypostyle Hall · Luxor Temple · Avenue of Sphinxes
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Luxor

### Day 8 · Luxor · the West Bank
**The Valley of the Kings, Hatshepsut and the Colossi**

The Valley of the Kings, more than sixty royal tombs cut into the cliffs, their corridors painted with scenes from the Book of the Dead. Hatshepsut's colonnaded temple at Deir el-Bahari, and the Colossi of Memnon, two seated figures of Amenhotep III about 18 metres tall. Then the cruise moves south.

**The day:** Valley of the Kings · Temple of Hatshepsut · Colossi of Memnon
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers · by cruise

### Day 9 · Edfu and Kom Ombo
**Two river temples, and a galabiya party**

Edfu's Temple of Horus, its enclosure wall, roof, halls and sanctuary standing as completed in the 1st century BC. In the afternoon, Kom Ombo, its plan doubled for Sobek and Horus the Elder, with mummified crocodiles in the museum. A galabiya party on board in the evening.

**The day:** Temple of Horus at Edfu · Temple of Kom Ombo · Crocodile Museum · galabiya party
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner

### Day 10 · Aswan
**The High Dam, Philae and a felucca**

The Aswan High Dam, completed in 1970, and the Unfinished Obelisk, cracked in its quarry. By motorboat to Philae, the Temple of Isis, moved to Agilkia Island. A late-afternoon felucca around Elephantine Island.

**The day:** Aswan High Dam · Unfinished Obelisk · Temple of Philae · felucca
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Aswan

### Day 11 · Aswan → Abu Simbel → Cairo
**The temples of Ramses II, then the flight north**

Off the cruise after breakfast and a short flight south to Abu Simbel: Ramses II's two cliff temples of the 13th century BC, their four seated figures twenty metres high and the sanctuary reached by the sun twice a year. Between 1964 and 1968 they were cut into blocks and rebuilt 65 metres higher, out of reach of Lake Nasser. Then the flight to Cairo.

**The day:** Abu Simbel
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast (on the cruise) · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Domestic flights Aswan → Abu Simbel → Cairo (not included) · private transfers
**Pace:** A day of airports, worth it
