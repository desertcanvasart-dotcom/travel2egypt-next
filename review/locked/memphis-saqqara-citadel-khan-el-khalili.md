## Opening

Giza is on every postcard, but Cairo’s character was built by others: the Old Kingdom Egyptians whose first capital lay just south of the city, and the medieval dynasties who built its fortress, mosques and markets. This private day skips Giza and crosses that ground: Memphis, where Egypt began as one state; Saqqara, where the pyramid was invented; the Citadel of Saladin; and the Khan El Khalili bazaar. About four thousand years, in two halves.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The morning is the Old Kingdom, in the desert south of the city, while it’s still cool. The drive back takes about an hour, with lunch on the way or in the city depending on your pace. The afternoon is medieval Cairo: the Citadel on its ridge, then down into the bazaar. If your party feels the heat, the morning archaeology and the afternoon city can be rebalanced.

## Your day

### Morning · Memphis and Saqqara

**08:00–09:00 · Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you in a private air-conditioned car. The city gives way to farmland, then desert.

**Memphis**
Founded, by tradition, by Menes around 3000 BC as the capital of a united Egypt. Most of the ancient city lies under villages and fields; the open-air museum holds a colossal limestone Ramses II, lying down because its feet are missing, and an alabaster sphinx of the New Kingdom, in better condition than its famous cousin.
*Sites:* Memphis open-air museum

**Saqqara and the Step Pyramid**
A short drive west across the desert. Imhotep’s Step Pyramid for Djoser, around 2650 BC, is where Egyptian architects learned to build in stone, and the pyramid as an idea was born. Around it lie Old Kingdom tombs painted with hunting, farming, music and banquets. Which ones are open rotates with conservation work; your guide will know.
*Sites:* Saqqara · Step Pyramid of Djoser

### Midday · Lunch

**Lunch at a local restaurant (included)**
On the way back into the city, or in Cairo, depending on your pace. Drinks aren’t included.

### Afternoon · The Citadel and Khan El Khalili

**The Citadel of Saladin and the Mosque of Muhammad Ali**
Saladin began the fortress in 1176 to defend Cairo and Fustat against the Crusaders, and Egypt was ruled from it for seven centuries. The Alabaster Mosque of Muhammad Ali, built 1830–48, is the silhouette in most photographs of Cairo’s skyline. From the western walls on a clear day, the pyramids show on the horizon. It’s a working mosque: modest dress, shoes off.
*Sites:* Citadel of Saladin · Mosque of Muhammad Ali

**Khan El Khalili**
Trading in the same lanes since 1382. Most of what’s sold is made for visitors, and we won’t pretend otherwise, but the bones of the medieval market are there: narrow streets, old caravanserais, mosques, and El Fishawy coffeehouse. Wander for half an hour without an agenda, then let your guide steer you to copper, perfume or textiles.
*Sites:* Khan El Khalili · El Fishawy
