## Opening

Lake Nasser is not a natural lake, and the temples along its shore are not where they were built. When the Aswan High Dam was completed in 1970, the water covered Lower Nubia, and its temples were cut into blocks and rebuilt on higher ground. Four nights on the lake take you south from Aswan, temple by temple, to Abu Simbel at the far end.

---

## Why this route works

Sailing south builds towards the end: the smaller rescued temples first, then Wadi es-Sebua and Amada, and Ramses II’s cliff temples last. Four nights leave room for what the lake is good at: long hours on deck, the evening light, and an afternoon at Abu Simbel with the night moored nearby, rather than a few hours on a day trip.

## Your five days

### Day 1 · Aswan
**Board the ship**

You’re met at Aswan airport or the railway station, or collected from your hotel, and taken to the ship, moored on the lake just south of the High Dam. Lunch on board. There are no visits today: the afternoon is for settling in, the deck, and the first evening on open water, with the dam behind you and the desert on both sides. Dinner on board.

**Overnight:** Lake Nasser cruise ship, near the High Dam

### Day 2 · Kalabsha
**Three rescued temples, then south**

In the morning, the temples rebuilt on New Kalabsha island, close to the dam. The Temple of Kalabsha, built in the Roman period for the Nubian god Mandulis, with its pylon, hall and sanctuary standing. Beit el-Wali, a rock-cut temple of Ramses II, its coloured reliefs showing the king’s campaigns. And the Kiosk of Kertassi, a small Roman structure with two Hathor-headed columns above the water. In the afternoon the ship sails south to moor at Wadi es-Sebua.

**The day:** Temple of Kalabsha · Beit el-Wali · Kiosk of Kertassi
**Overnight:** Lake Nasser cruise ship, at Wadi es-Sebua
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By cruise
**Pace:** A morning of visits, an afternoon on deck

### Day 3 · Wadi es-Sebua and Amada
**The Valley of the Lions, and the painted temples**

Wadi es-Sebua, the Valley of the Lions, named for the avenue of sphinxes before the temple of Ramses II. It was later used as a church, and in the sanctuary a painted Saint Peter stands where the king’s cult image was. Rebuilt beside it, Dakka, a Ptolemaic temple to Thoth, and the small Roman kiosk of Maharraqa. Later, Amada, among the oldest temples in Nubia, begun in the 18th Dynasty, its colours still bright; Derr, a rock-cut temple of Ramses II; and the painted tomb of Pennut.

**The day:** Temple of Wadi es-Sebua · Temple of Dakka · Maharraqa · Temple of Amada · Temple of Derr · Tomb of Pennut
**Overnight:** Lake Nasser cruise ship
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By cruise
**Pace:** The fullest day of the cruise

### Day 4 · Qasr Ibrim → Abu Simbel
**A fortress from the deck, and the temples of Ramses II**

Early on deck to pass Qasr Ibrim, the one site on the lake that was never moved: its hill stood above the water and now looks like an island. Landing is not allowed. In the afternoon, Abu Simbel: the Great Temple, its four seated figures of Ramses II about twenty metres high, and the temple of Nefertari. Around 22 February and 22 October the sun reaches the sanctuary. Between 1964 and 1968 both were cut into blocks and raised 65 metres. The ship moors nearby for the night.

**The day:** Qasr Ibrim (from the deck) · Great Temple of Abu Simbel · Temple of Nefertari
**Overnight:** Lake Nasser cruise ship, at Abu Simbel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By cruise
**Pace:** A morning on deck, the afternoon at Abu Simbel

## Is this journey for you?

**A good fit if you:**
- have seen the classic Nile and want the rescued temples of Nubia
- want Abu Simbel as the end of the story, with a night beside it
- like quiet days on deck between visits

**Worth knowing:**
- There are no towns on the lake, no shops and no evening market. The ship is where you spend your free time.
- Day 1 has no visits, by design.
- Qasr Ibrim is seen from the deck only.
- Flights from Abu Simbel are few. Plan the onward journey before you book the cruise.
- Tipping the cruise crew is customary and expected. Budget for it separately.
