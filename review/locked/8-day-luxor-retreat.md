## Opening

Eight days in one hotel in Luxor, ancient Thebes, with three guided days for the West Bank, Karnak and the Luxor Museum, and the rest left open for the pool, the Nile and long dinners.

---

## Why this route works

Luxor is usually a two-day stop between Cairo and the Red Sea, and the New Kingdom pharaohs built here on a scale that two days cannot read. Staying in one place changes that. The monument days are spread through the week, with rest days between them, so each site gets your full attention and none of the packing. You unpack once, and the pace is your own.

## Your eight days

### Day 1 · Luxor
**Arrival**

You’re met at Luxor airport and taken to your hotel. Luxor is small, and the drive gives you your first orientation: the Nile, the green strip of the valley, and beyond it the pale limestone cliffs of the Theban hills where the tombs are cut. Dinner is at the hotel. The programme starts when you choose.

**Overnight:** Luxor hotel
**Meals:** Dinner
**Travel:** Meet and greet at the airport · private transfer
**Pace:** Easy

A day with nothing fixed. Breakfast by the pool, the hotel gardens, the Nile at the edge of the grounds, and, if the pull of the monuments is already stronger than the pull of rest, a walk along the Corniche. Your guide is on call if you decide to see something today.

**Overnight:** Luxor hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · dinner

### Day 3 · Luxor
**The West Bank**

The Valley of the Kings, cut into the limestone of the Theban hills, where the royal tombs of the New Kingdom lay from about 1550 to 1070 BC. More than sixty are known, their corridors and chambers painted with scenes from the funerary texts, and Tutankhamun’s tomb is among them. The three colonnaded terraces of the temple of Hatshepsut at Deir el-Bahari, built for a woman who ruled as pharaoh for about twenty years. The Colossi of Memnon, two seated quartzite figures of Amenhotep III, on the plain at the edge of the West Bank. We go at the hour the heat allows.

**The day:** Valley of the Kings · Temple of Hatshepsut · Colossi of Memnon
**Overnight:** Luxor hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers

**Optional on this day:**
- The tombs of Tutankhamun, Seti I and Ramesses VI: extra tickets

**Overnight:** Luxor hotel

### Day 5 · Luxor
**The Luxor Museum**

A compact, well-curated museum on the East Bank Corniche, with New Kingdom statuary from the Theban temples, shown with room and light to spare. It takes a morning, and it makes the temples you have seen, and the one you are about to, easier to read. The afternoon is free.

**The day:** Luxor Museum
**Overnight:** Luxor hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers
**Pace:** Easy

---

### Day 6 · Luxor
**Karnak and Luxor Temple**

Karnak, expanded by pharaoh after pharaoh over roughly fifteen centuries, with the Great Hypostyle Hall of 134 columns up to 21 metres tall, the Sacred Lake and a sequence of pylons, each built to outdo the last. It takes a morning to do properly. Luxor Temple, joined to Karnak by the Avenue of Sphinxes, which reopened in November 2021, is best at dusk, when the columns are lit from below.

**The day:** Karnak · Great Hypostyle Hall · Luxor Temple · Avenue of Sphinxes
**Overnight:** Luxor hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers
