## Opening

From Alexandria to the cradle of the pyramids and back in one long day: Memphis, the capital that commissioned everything; Saqqara, where monumental stone building was invented; and Dahshur, where the builders finally got the geometry right. Few days in Egypt tell such a clean story. It’s quieter than Giza, and it makes Giza make sense.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The road is the honest cost: about 220 kilometres each way before Egypt’s oldest stones give anything back. So you leave early, reach Memphis after about two and a half to three hours on the desert road, and follow the story in order: the capital, then the necropolis, then the pyramid field. If your trip later passes through Cairo, these sites cost less from there; if Alexandria is your only base, the early start is what makes it work.

## Your day

### Morning · Memphis and Saqqara

**Pickup from your hotel or the port**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you early from your hotel, or meet you at the port entrance, in a private air-conditioned car for the desert road south.

**Memphis**
Capital of a united Egypt from around 3100 BC, founded, by tradition, by Menes. The city has almost vanished; a small, honest open-air museum keeps the reclining limestone colossus of Ramses II and an alabaster sphinx, probably of the 18th Dynasty. It’s the context: from here, everything else today was ordered.
*Sites:* Memphis open-air museum

**The Step Pyramid of Djoser**
Under thirty minutes away. Built around 2650 BC by Imhotep: six steps, about 60 metres high, and one of the earliest large buildings in dressed stone. Around it, his enclosure copies the palace’s reed and mud-brick architecture in stone: false doors, engaged columns, the court of the royal jubilee. Going inside the pyramid is an extra ticket.
*Sites:* Saqqara · Step Pyramid of Djoser

**The Imhotep Museum and a nobles’ tomb**
The site museum at the entrance to Saqqara, named after Djoser’s architect, and one of the Old Kingdom nobles’ tombs, chosen with your guide from those open on the day, with painted scenes of hunting, farming and offerings. Both are included.
*Sites:* Imhotep Museum · Saqqara nobles’ tombs

### Afternoon · Dahshur

**The Bent Pyramid**
About 10 kilometres south. Built by Snefru, Khufu’s father, in the 26th century BC. It rises at about 54 degrees, then changes to 43 partway up, almost certainly a correction made mid-build, and it keeps most of its original smooth casing.
*Sites:* Bent Pyramid

**The Red Pyramid**
Snefru’s next attempt, built at the gentler angle from the start: the first true smooth-sided pyramid, about 105 metres high. Going inside, down the long, low passage to its chambers, is an extra ticket.
*Sites:* Red Pyramid

**Optional extras, if you’ve chosen them**

- Inside the Red Pyramid: extra ticket
- Further tombs at Saqqara: extra tickets
