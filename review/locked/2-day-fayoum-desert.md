## Opening

About a hundred kilometres south-west of Cairo, the Fayoum depression holds a forty-million-year fossil record and a living oasis within an hour's drive of each other. It has its own water, its own farmland, its own great lake and a UNESCO World Heritage site where whales with legs lie in the rock. A day trip gets you one of these things. Two days lets you see them in the right order.

---

## Why this route works

A single day forces a choice: Wadi al-Hitan or Wadi al-Rayan, but not both well. Wadi al-Hitan alone is six hours of driving for two on the ground, the wrong proportion for a World Heritage site. Splitting the circuit with a night in the depression puts the right ratio back: Day 1 belongs to the whales, and Day 2 to the lakes, the waterfalls and the petrified wood of Gebel Qatrani. You read the geology in daylight without rushing, and no single day overwhelms.

## Your 2 days

### Day 1 · Cairo → Wadi al-Hitan → Tzila Lodge
**The Valley of the Whales, and a night in the Fayoum**

The drive takes about three hours, the last forty-five minutes on desert track. Wadi al-Hitan was inscribed by UNESCO in 2005 for what it records: skeletons of whale ancestors from the late Eocene, about forty million years old, when the Tethys Sea covered this land, still with the legs they kept as they moved from land to water. They lie where they were found, with walking paths between them and a visitor centre that sets out the geology. Your Egyptologist and desert guide read the site with you; lunch comes after the visit. In the late afternoon, the drive continues to Tzila Lodge for dinner and the night, as the desert cools sharply after sunset.

**The day:** Wadi al-Hitan
**Overnight:** Tzila Lodge, Fayoum
**Meals:** lunch · dinner
**Travel:** private air-conditioned car from Cairo, about 3 hours

### Day 2 · Wadi al-Rayan → Lake Qarun → Gebel Qatrani → Cairo
**Waterfalls, dunes, the great lake and a fossil forest**

Wadi al-Rayan is a protected area of two lakes, Upper and Lower, joined by a short run of waterfalls, modest but famous in Egypt, and important for migrating birds. The morning is for walking the lakeshore and the falls, with sandboarding on the dunes nearby, included if you want it. After lunch comes Lake Qarun, the Fayoum's largest lake, for birdwatching and a slower pace, and then Gebel Qatrani, north of the lake, where petrified wood lies in the same ancient formations as the whales of Day 1. From there, the two-hour drive back to Cairo.
