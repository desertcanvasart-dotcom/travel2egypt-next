## Opening

Most visitors never quite manage to put Cairo together on their own: from Pharaonic antiquity, through medieval Islamic power, to the living, trading city of today. This private day does it in order: the Egyptian Museum on Tahrir Square, the Citadel of Saladin, Al-Muizz Street and Khan El Khalili. Done well, it works like a key; everything you see later in your trip makes more sense because of it.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The museum comes first, while your attention is freshest, then the Citadel on its hill, with the city laid out below. After lunch, the afternoon moves down into the medieval city, ending in the bazaar in the late afternoon, when the light shifts and the call to prayer rises over the stalls. The distances look small on a map; in Cairo’s traffic they fill the day, which is why the driving is left to someone who knows the city.

## Your day

### Morning · The Egyptian Museum and the Citadel

**Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you in a private air-conditioned car.

**The Egyptian Museum, Tahrir Square**
The red-domed building, home of the state collection since 1902. Tutankhamun’s treasure is now at the GEM and the royal mummies at the NMEC, but more than 100,000 objects remain. Your guide focuses the visit: the colossal royal statues, Rahotep and Nofret, the treasure of Yuya and Thuya and the Narmer Palette of around 3100 BC. About two hours.
*Sites:* Egyptian Museum

**The Citadel of Saladin and the Mosque of Muhammad Ali**
Saladin began the Citadel in 1176 on a spur of the Mokattam Hills, and Egypt was governed from here for some seven centuries. The Alabaster Mosque of Muhammad Ali, completed in 1848, borrows from the Ottoman mosques of Istanbul. From the courtyard on a clear morning, you can see the Giza pyramids. The Military Museum, in Muhammad Ali’s former Harem Palace, is there if modern history interests you.
*Sites:* Citadel of Saladin · Mosque of Muhammad Ali · Military Museum

### Afternoon · Al-Muizz Street and Khan El Khalili

**Al-Muizz Street**
The spine of the Fatimid city, now an open-air museum of medieval Islamic architecture: the Qalawun Complex of 1284–85, mausoleum, madrasa and hospital in one, and the al-Ghuri complex from the very end of Mamluk rule, with public fountains, Qur’an schools and stretches of the Fatimid wall between them.
*Sites:* Al-Muizz Street · Qalawun Complex · Al-Ghuri Complex

**Khan El Khalili**
The bazaar grew around a Mamluk caravanserai in the late 14th century and is loosely divided by trade: gold and silver, spices, cotton, leather, papyrus. Bargain in the small stalls, or use the fixed-price shops. Take a tea at al-Fishawi.
*Sites:* Khan El Khalili · Al-Fishawi
