## Opening

Three terrains most short trips have to choose between, in one private journey: the Nile and its temples on a cruise from Luxor to Aswan, with Abu Simbel; the granite mountains and monastery of Sinai, with a pre-dawn climb of Mount Sinai; and the coral coast at Sharm El Sheikh.

---

## Why this route works

It doesn’t loop; it moves in one direction. South by air to Luxor and up the river to Aswan, back to Cairo for the pyramids, then east into Sinai, finishing at the sea, with a last Cairo day for the Grand Egyptian Museum before you fly home.

### Day 2 · Cairo → Luxor
**Karnak, Luxor Temple and the Luxor Museum, and board the cruise**

An early flight to Luxor. Karnak, a temple city added to over fifteen centuries, with the Great Hypostyle Hall of 134 columns up to 21 metres. Luxor Temple, more unified, at the end of the Avenue of Sphinxes, reopened in November 2021. The Luxor Museum’s precisely displayed New Kingdom collection, then on board your Nile cruise.

**The day:** Karnak · Great Hypostyle Hall · Luxor Temple · Avenue of Sphinxes · Luxor Museum
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Luxor
**Meals:** Dinner
**Travel:** Domestic flight Cairo → Luxor · private transfers

### Day 3 · Luxor · the West Bank
**The Valley of the Kings, Hatshepsut, the Colossi and Medinet Habu**

The Valley of the Kings, more than sixty painted royal tombs: Tutankhamun’s, made famous by its intact contents in 1922, and the grander tombs of Ramses III and the Thutmosid kings. Hatshepsut’s colonnaded temple at Deir el-Bahari; the Colossi of Memnon; and Medinet Habu, Ramses III’s temple, among the most intact on the West Bank. Then the cruise moves south.

**The day:** Valley of the Kings · Temple of Hatshepsut · Colossi of Memnon · Medinet Habu
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers · by cruise

**Optional on this day:**
- The tombs of Tutankhamun, Seti I and Ramses VI: extra tickets

### Day 4 · Edfu and Kom Ombo
**Two river temples, and a galabiya party**

By horse-drawn carriage from the dock to Edfu’s Temple of Horus, its enclosure wall, roof, halls and sanctuary standing as completed in the 1st century BC. In the afternoon, Kom Ombo, its plan doubled for Sobek and Horus the Elder, with mummified crocodiles in the museum. A galabiya party on board in the evening.

**The day:** Temple of Horus at Edfu · Temple of Kom Ombo · Crocodile Museum · galabiya party
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By cruise · horse-drawn carriage

### Day 5 · Abu Simbel → Aswan
**The temples of Ramses II, the High Dam and a felucca**

The longest day. Before dawn, south to Abu Simbel and Ramses II’s two cliff temples of the 13th century BC, their four seated figures twenty metres high and the sanctuary reached by the sun twice a year; between 1964 and 1968 they were rebuilt 65 metres higher. Back in Aswan, the High Dam and the Unfinished Obelisk, cracked in its quarry, and a late-afternoon felucca around Elephantine Island.

**The day:** Abu Simbel · Aswan High Dam · Unfinished Obelisk · felucca
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Aswan
**Meals:** Breakfast · dinner
**Travel:** Excursion to Abu Simbel · private transfers · felucca
**Pace:** The longest day

### Day 6 · Aswan → Cairo
**Philae, then the flight north**

The last morning on the river: by motorboat to Philae, the Temple of Isis, moved to Agilkia Island, one of the last temples built in the old style, from Nectanebo I in the 4th century BC into Roman times. Then a flight back to Cairo.

**The day:** Temple of Philae
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast (on the cruise)
**Travel:** Motorboat to Philae · domestic flight Aswan → Cairo

### Day 7 · Memphis, Saqqara and Giza
**Where the pyramid was invented, and where it was perfected**

Memphis, first capital of a united Egypt around 3100 BC, with the fallen colossus of Ramses II and an alabaster sphinx. Saqqara and the Step Pyramid of Djoser, built by Imhotep around 2650 BC. Then Giza: the pyramids of Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure, around 2500 BC, and the Sphinx below them, with a camel ride on the plateau worth taking.

**The day:** Memphis · Saqqara · Step Pyramid of Djoser · Giza pyramids · Great Sphinx · camel ride
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Travel:** Private transfers

**Optional on this day:**
- Inside one of the pyramids: extra ticket

**Overnight:** Saint Catherine hotel

### Day 9 · Mount Sinai and Saint Catherine’s Monastery
**Sunrise on the summit**

The climb starts in darkness, two to three hours up the camel path to the summit at 2,285 metres for sunrise over the Sinai ranges, and the Gulf of Aqaba when the air is clear. After the descent, Saint Catherine’s Monastery, in use since the 6th century, with its early icons and ancient manuscripts; access inside changes, and your guide confirms what’s open.

**The day:** Mount Sinai · Saint Catherine’s Monastery
**Overnight:** Saint Catherine hotel
**Travel:** On foot · camel optional
**Pace:** A pre-dawn climb

**The Grand Egyptian Museum, the Citadel and Khan El Khalili**

Back to Cairo for the Grand Egyptian Museum, with the complete Tutankhamun collection and Khufu’s cedar boat. Then the Citadel on the Mokattam ridge, which commanded Cairo for seven centuries, and the alabaster Mosque of Muhammad Ali. Khan El Khalili and a final dinner in the old city.
