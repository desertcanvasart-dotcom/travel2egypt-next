## Opening

Two ends of Egyptian history in one day, and the quarry that made them possible. At Edfu, a Ptolemaic temple stands with its roof still on; at El Kab, an officer's tomb tells, in his own words, the war that founded the New Kingdom; and at Gebel Silsila, the cliffs show where the stone for Karnak and Luxor was cut. For most of the day, you won't be sharing the ground with coach tours.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The day starts at a civilised nine and heads north along the river. Edfu comes first, about 105 kilometres from Aswan, on the west bank. El Kab is some 20 kilometres beyond it, on the east bank, so the two fit together before lunch. On the way back south, a short stop at Gebel Silsila explains where much of the sandstone for the great Theban temples came from. El Kab and Silsila are quiet in a way Edfu's courtyard isn't; if the quiet places are why you're coming, tell us and we'll give them more time.

## Your day

### Morning · Edfu

**09:00 · Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you in a private air-conditioned car, north through sugar cane, banana groves and villages, as the desert cliffs close in.

**The Temple of Horus, Edfu**
Built between 237 and 57 BC, and standing essentially whole: pylon, courtyard, columned halls, sanctuary and the corridor around it. It survived because it was buried under silt and a village for a thousand years; Auguste Mariette dug it out in the 1860s. Inside, the granite shrine of Nectanebo II still stands in the sanctuary, and the walls record the Horus myth and how a late temple worked, day to day.
*Sites:* Temple of Horus, Edfu

### Midday · El Kab and lunch

**The tombs of El Kab**
A short drive on, across to the east bank: ancient Nekheb, city of the vulture goddess Nekhbet. In the cliff above the plain, Ahmose, son of Ibana, who served three kings, describes his own part in driving out the Hyksos around 1550 BC: a first-person war memoir with little like it in the record. Nearby, the tomb of his grandson Paheri keeps farming and banquet scenes with their colour.
*Sites:* El Kab · Tomb of Ahmose son of Ibana · Tomb of Paheri

**Lunch at a local restaurant (included)**
Drinks aren't included.

### Afternoon · Gebel Silsila and the road home

**Gebel Silsila**
On the way back south, where the Nile squeezes between sandstone cliffs and successive dynasties quarried stone for Karnak, Luxor and the great Theban monuments. A small rock-cut chapel of Horemheb and stelae line the cliff. A short, quiet stop.
*Sites:* Gebel Silsila · Speos of Horemheb

**Back to Aswan**
To your hotel, at the end of the day.
