## Opening

Egypt is usually read as a river country. This journey gives the river its due, with Giza and Saqqara at the start and Luxor near the end, but spends its middle in the Western Desert: Bahariya, a night camped in the White Desert, then south through Farafra to Dakhla and east to Kharga, before the road brings you back to the Nile at Luxor.

---

## Why this route works

The route is a loop, so no road is driven twice. West from Cairo to Bahariya, south through the White Desert and Farafra to Dakhla, east to Kharga, and from Kharga a single drive east to Luxor. Two nights in Luxor give the East and West Banks a day each, and a short flight takes you back to Cairo for the Grand Egyptian Museum and the bazaar. The desert comes early, while you’re fresh for the long drives.

### Day 2 · Giza, Memphis and Saqqara
**Where the pyramid was perfected, and where it was invented**

Giza first: the pyramids of Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure, built around 2500 BC. You go inside the pyramid of Khafre or Menkaure, subject to site rules on the day, then the panorama viewpoint, the Great Sphinx and the Valley Temple. Then south to Memphis, first capital of a united Egypt around 3100 BC, with the fallen colossus of Ramses II and an alabaster sphinx, and Saqqara, where the Step Pyramid of Djoser, built around 2650 BC, shows the experiment that made Giza possible, with the Imhotep Museum and a nobles’ tomb.

**The day:** Giza pyramids · inside Khafre or Menkaure · panorama viewpoint · Great Sphinx · Valley Temple · Memphis · Saqqara · Step Pyramid of Djoser · Imhotep Museum · a nobles’ tomb
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch
**Travel:** Private transfers
**Pace:** The fullest sightseeing day

**Optional on this day:**
- Inside the Great Pyramid of Khufu: extra ticket, subject to availability and site rules on the day
- Inside the Step Pyramid: extra ticket

### Day 3 · Cairo → Bahariya
**The first oasis**

About four hours southwest across the desert to Bahariya, green with palms and olives in its depression, with hot springs and the small town of Bawiti at its heart. One collective ticket opens the oasis sites: a few of the gilded mummies found in 1996 in what is now called the Valley of the Golden Mummies, the painted Late Period tombs of Bannentiu and Djed-Amun-ef-ankh, and the Temple of Alexander the Great.

**The day:** Golden Mummies · Tombs of Bannentiu and Djed-Amun-ef-ankh · Temple of Alexander
**Overnight:** Bahariya hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private car, Cairo → Bahariya

---

### Day 4 · The Black Desert → the White Desert
**Volcanic hills, Crystal Mountain, and a night under the formations**

By 4×4 south through the Black Desert, a field of dark, basalt-capped hills; Crystal Mountain, a small outcrop of crystals that catches the light; and the Agabat valley, its pale spires best walked rather than photographed from the road. In the afternoon, the White Desert National Park: chalk carved by the wind into Mushroom Rock, towers and columns, long shadows at dusk. Camp among them, after everyone else has gone.

**The day:** Black Desert · Crystal Mountain · Agabat · White Desert National Park
**Overnight:** Camping in the White Desert
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private 4×4

---

### Day 5 · White Desert → Dakhla
**Farafra, the long road south, Deir el-Hagar and Qasr al-Dakhla**

Breakfast in the desert, then the long drive south through Farafra, the smallest of the oases, to Dakhla, with its rice fields, palm groves and springs below a pink escarpment. The road comes in from the west, past Deir el-Hagar, a Roman-period sandstone temple to Amun, Mut and Khonsu with the names of emperors from Nero to Hadrian on its walls, and Qasr al-Dakhla, a medieval mud-brick town of covered lanes, carved wooden lintels and an old minaret, still partly lived in.

**The day:** Farafra · Deir el-Hagar · Qasr al-Dakhla
**Overnight:** Dakhla hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private car, White Desert → Dakhla
**Pace:** A long drive

---

### Day 6 · Dakhla → Kharga
**The Temple of Hibis and the chapels of Bagawat**

East to Kharga, the largest of the New Valley oases, at the northern end of the Forty Days Road, the old caravan route from Sudan. The Temple of Hibis, a Late Period temple to Amun, its walls carved largely under the Persian king Darius I. Then Bagawat, a hillside of hundreds of mud-brick Christian funerary chapels from the 4th century AD on, some with early painted domes.

**The day:** Temple of Hibis · Bagawat Necropolis
**Overnight:** Kharga hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private car, Dakhla → Kharga

---

### Day 7 · Kharga → Luxor
**East to the Nile, Karnak and Luxor Temple**

An early start for the drive east out of the desert to the Nile at Luxor, and lunch on arrival. In the afternoon, Karnak, a temple city added to over fifteen centuries, with the Great Hypostyle Hall of 134 columns up to 21 metres high, and Luxor Temple at the end of the Avenue of Sphinxes. Then your hotel, and the first night by the river after four in the desert.

**The day:** Karnak · Great Hypostyle Hall · Luxor Temple · Avenue of Sphinxes
**Overnight:** Luxor hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch
**Travel:** Private car, Kharga → Luxor
**Pace:** A long drive, then the temples

---

### Day 8 · Luxor · the West Bank
**The Valley of the Kings, Hatshepsut and the Colossi**

Across the river to the Valley of the Kings, more than sixty royal tombs cut into the cliffs, their corridors painted with scenes from the Book of the Dead. Hatshepsut’s colonnaded temple at Deir el-Bahari, set against the cliff, and the Colossi of Memnon, two seated figures of Amenhotep III about 18 metres tall, alone in the fields. The afternoon is yours, in a town that rewards a walk along the Corniche.

**The day:** Valley of the Kings · Temple of Hatshepsut · Colossi of Memnon
**Overnight:** Luxor hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch
**Travel:** Private transfers

**Optional on this day:**
- The tombs of Tutankhamun, Seti I and Ramses VI: extra tickets

---

### Day 9 · Luxor → Cairo
**The Grand Egyptian Museum and Khan El Khalili**

A morning flight to Cairo, and straight to the Grand Egyptian Museum, beside the Giza plateau, where the complete Tutankhamun collection is shown together. Give it the morning and the early afternoon. Then across the city to Khan El Khalili, trading since the 14th century, for a last walk through the old lanes.

**The day:** Grand Egyptian Museum · Khan El Khalili
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch
**Travel:** Domestic flight Luxor → Cairo (not included) · private transfers
