## Opening

Egypt’s desert interior is a different country. This private safari leaves the Nile behind and goes southwest from Cairo into the Sahara, to two limestone caves whose walls carry the marks of people who crossed this desert long before the pharaohs: Djara, with its stalactites and prehistoric drawings, and Obeiyed, near Farafra, with its rock inscriptions. Between them, the black volcanic hills, the chalk formations of the White Desert and the springs that explain why anyone ever lived out here.

---

## Why this route works

Most desert trips reach one cave, if any. Six days reach both, and give each one its own day rather than a stop on the way to something else. The route moves outward in stages: Bahariya is the threshold, the White Desert the visual centrepiece, the two caves the destination, with the New White Desert and the springs on the way back. Two guides travel with you: an Egyptologist for the oasis archaeology and the rock art, and a Bedouin guide who knows the desert. It asks for long drives on desert tracks, three nights under open sky, and the discomforts of the deep desert: heat in the day, cold at night, nothing between stops. It repays all of that.

## Your six days

### Day 1 · Cairo → Bahariya
**The threshold oasis**

**Morning.** About four hours, some 370 kilometres southwest through the desert plateau, to Bahariya Oasis: a working agricultural town fed by natural springs, with date palms and olive groves, a staging post on the old caravan routes. Lunch in Bahariya.

**Afternoon.** Pyramid Mountain and its wide view across the oasis basin; English Mountain, with the ruins of a British post from the First World War; the old village of El Bawiti and the Ethnic Museum, with objects from the oasis’s Roman, Islamic and Bedouin past. Dinner at your hotel.

**The day:** Pyramid Mountain · English Mountain · old village of El Bawiti · Ethnic Museum
**Overnight:** Bahariya hotel
**Meals:** Lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private car, Cairo → Bahariya

### Day 2 · Bahariya → the White Desert
**The Golden Mummies, the Black Desert and a night among the chalk**

**Morning.** Breakfast at the hotel. The Golden Mummies, from the Graeco-Roman cemetery found in 1996, when a donkey stumbled into a shaft: more than 250 mummies, some in gilded cartonnage masks. Then into 4×4 country with the desert drivers.

**Afternoon.** Lunch on the way. The Black Desert, a plateau darkened by volcanic stone and basalt cones; Crystal Mountain, an outcrop of quartz crystals exposed by erosion; the Agabat valley, a maze of sculpted chalk; and the Old White Desert, where the wind has shaped the chalk into mushrooms, arches and animals, including the formations of Tent Valley.

**Evening.** Camp among the formations, with dinner and the chalk shapes visible by moonlight.

**The day:** Golden Mummies · Black Desert · Crystal Mountain · Agabat · Old White Desert · Tent Valley
**Overnight:** Camping in the White Desert
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private 4×4

### Day 3 · The White Desert → Djara Cave
**Into the deep desert**

Breakfast at camp, then a genuinely remote stretch, with very little between the stopping points. The Acacia Tree, a lone acacia that has become a landmark because there is nothing else near it for a very long way. Watermelon Valley, named for its rounded dark stones. Lunch in the desert. Then camp beside Djara Cave, some 150 kilometres from the White Desert camp: the remotest night of the journey.

**The day:** the Acacia Tree · Watermelon Valley · Djara Cave camp
**Overnight:** Camping beside Djara Cave
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private 4×4

---

### Day 4 · Djara Cave → Obeiyed
**The first cave**

**Morning.** Breakfast at camp, then Djara, a limestone cavern in the desert plateau, with stalactites and stalagmites inside and prehistoric drawings on its walls: animals, human figures and symbols, made by people moving through the desert when the Sahara was wetter than it is now. It’s not a crowded site, and the time allows for real exploration rather than a glance.

**Afternoon.** Lunch, then west across the desert towards Farafra, to camp near Obeiyed.

**The day:** Djara Cave · the crossing towards Farafra
**Overnight:** Camping near Obeiyed
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private 4×4

### Day 5 · Obeiyed Cave → the New White Desert → Bahariya
**The second cave, the springs and the way back**

**Morning.** Breakfast at camp, then the Obeiyed Cave, a limestone cavern near Farafra, with prehistoric rock inscriptions and paintings on its walls: animal figures, human silhouettes and geometric patterns, from the same broad horizon as the rock art at Djara. The walk in is uneven and not trivial; sturdy shoes and sun protection are essential. Then Magic Spring, a pocket of water and green in the middle of the plateau.

**Afternoon.** Lunch, then the New White Desert, its chalk worn into larger, more sculptural shapes than the Old. Hawaas Springs and Bir Serw, then the drive back to Bahariya, with dinner and a hotel bed after three desert nights.

**The day:** Obeiyed Cave · Magic Spring · New White Desert · Hawaas Springs · Bir Serw
**Overnight:** Bahariya hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner

## Is this journey for you?

**A good fit if you:**
- are drawn to prehistoric rock art and remote desert
- have already seen the Nile programme and want the other Egypt

**Worth knowing:**
- Three nights are camping, and the drives are long, on desert tracks with no infrastructure between stops.
- The walk to Obeiyed Cave is uneven. Wear sturdy shoes.
- Desert days can be hot and the nights cold, especially in winter. Bring warm layers.
