## Opening

Siwa lies in Egypt’s far west, about fifty kilometres from the Libyan border, and for most of its history it was reached by caravan. Alexander the Great crossed the desert in 332 BC to consult its oracle of Amun. This private route goes much as he did, west along the Mediterranean and then south through the desert, with the war cemeteries of El Alamein on the way out.

---

## Why this route works

Alexandria to Siwa is a long day by road, about eight to ten hours with the stops, and the route uses it: El Alamein breaks the coastal stretch, and Marsa Matruh is the turn south. Day 2 is the whole of the oasis, the Oracle and the springs in the morning and the dunes of the Great Sand Sea in the afternoon, with the night in a desert camp. Day 3 takes Siwa’s old town and its tombs before the road home. Two long drives in three days is the honest cost.

## Your three days

### Day 1 · Alexandria → El Alamein → Siwa
**The desert war, then the road south**

An early start west along the coast, about 130 kilometres, to El Alamein, where in 1942 the Allied and Axis armies fought the battles that turned the war in North Africa. The war museum, with uniforms, weapons and photographs from both sides; the Commonwealth war cemetery; and the German and Italian memorials, the Italian one in white marble above the sea. Then on to Marsa Matruh for lunch, and south across the desert, about four to four and a half hours, to Siwa, arriving in the evening for dinner.

**The day:** El Alamein war museum · Commonwealth war cemetery · German memorial · Italian memorial
**Overnight:** Siwa hotel
**Meals:** Lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Pickup from your hotel in Alexandria · private car, Alexandria → El Alamein → Marsa Matruh → Siwa
**Pace:** A very long drive

---

### Day 2 · Siwa and the Great Sand Sea
**The Oracle, the springs and a night in the dunes**

**Morning.** Aghurmi and the Temple of the Oracle of Amun, built in the 26th Dynasty, where Alexander was hailed as the son of Amun; then the single carved wall of the temple at Umm Ubayda. Cleopatra’s Spring, a stone-lined spring pool, and a float in one of Siwa’s salt lakes, the water so dense with salt that you can’t sink. Lunch in town.

**Afternoon.** By 4×4 into the Great Sand Sea, its dune chains running in parallel for hundreds of kilometres. Bedouin tea at the dune edge as the sun goes down, a barbecue dinner, and the night in camp.

**The day:** Temple of the Oracle · Umm Ubayda · Cleopatra’s Spring · salt lake · Great Sand Sea
**Overnight:** Desert camp at the edge of the Great Sand Sea
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private car · private 4×4

---

### Day 3 · Siwa → Alexandria
**Shali, the Mountain of the Dead, and the coast road home**

Back into town after breakfast. The Shali Fortress, Siwa’s old town of salt-mud karshif on its rock, mostly dissolved by three days of rain in 1926 but still walkable. Then the Mountain of the Dead, with tombs cut in the 26th Dynasty and the Ptolemaic period, Si-Amun’s among them, its Greek-Egyptian paintings still visible. Lunch in Siwa, then north to Marsa Matruh, about four to four and a half hours, and east along the coast to Alexandria, arriving in the evening.

**The day:** Shali Fortress · Mountain of the Dead · Tomb of Si-Amun
**Overnight:** —
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch
**Travel:** Private car, Siwa → Marsa Matruh → Alexandria, drop-off at your hotel
**Pace:** A very long drive
