## Opening

Luxor isn't a city that happens to have temples; it is the temples. This private half day follows the processional way the pharaohs used between the vast precinct of Karnak and Luxor Temple, along the Avenue of Sphinxes, in a few focused hours. With an Egyptologist of your own, the commentary becomes a conversation, and the morning becomes a lesson in how power, faith and architecture were once the same thing.

---

## Why the day runs this way

Karnak comes first. An early start isn't a brochure habit here: it's the sensible way to see Karnak's open courts before the sun is overhead, and in summer Luxor is genuinely hot. Then a stretch of the restored Avenue of Sphinxes shows you the ritual geography that tied the two temples together, and Luxor Temple closes the visit before lunch. Because the tour is private, you can weight the hours towards either temple.

## Your day

### Morning · Karnak

**Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you early in a private air-conditioned car.

**Karnak**
Where Luxor Temple is coherent, Karnak is vast and deliberately overwhelming: built, extended, torn down and rebuilt over some 1,500 years. Ten pylons form its spine. Beyond the first stands the Great Hypostyle Hall of Seti I and Ramses II: 134 columns up to 21 metres high, meant to evoke a papyrus marsh at the moment of creation. Stand in the middle, and the columns become a forest.
*Sites:* Karnak · Great Hypostyle Hall

**Hatshepsut's obelisk and the Sacred Lake**
Hatshepsut's obelisk, nearly 30 metres tall and raised around 1457 BC, was later walled in by her successor; it still stands. The Sacred Lake, the basin of ritual purification, is a quieter place to pause and take stock.
*Sites:* Obelisk of Hatshepsut · Sacred Lake

**The Avenue of Sphinxes**
The processional road between the two temples, lined with ram-headed sphinxes near Karnak and human-headed ones at the Luxor end, now excavated and restored along much of its length. Walking a stretch of it makes the link between the temples physical.
*Sites:* Avenue of Sphinxes

### Late morning · Luxor Temple

**Luxor Temple**
Built mainly by Amenhotep III and Ramses II, at the height of the New Kingdom. Ramses's seated colossi guard the pylon; behind them, Amenhotep's colonnade leads through courts that grow more intimate towards the sanctuary. A Roman chapel and the still-used Mosque of Abu Haggag share the site: three thousand years of worship in one building. The wall reliefs show the Opet Festival, when Amun, Mut and Khonsu were carried here from Karnak.
*Sites:* Luxor Temple · Mosque of Abu Haggag
