## Opening

A private fortnight that earns its second week: a seven-night Nile cruise from Luxor to Aswan and back, with your own Egyptologist, then seven all-inclusive nights at Hurghada for the Giftun reefs, the wrecks of the Strait of Gubal, and slow days by the sea.

---

## Why this route works

Most trips start in Cairo and add a beach. This one begins in Luxor and puts the coast second, once enough history has built up that a week of open water feels earned. The first week is intellectually demanding; the second isn’t, and shouldn’t be.

## Your fifteen days

### Day 1 · Luxor
**Arrival, Karnak and Luxor Temple**

You’re met at Luxor and taken to your cruise ship, then out to Karnak, built up over some 1,500 years, its Great Hypostyle Hall of 134 sandstone columns up to 21 metres, one of the few ancient interiors that truly overwhelms at human scale. Luxor Temple, joined to Karnak by the Avenue of Sphinxes, reopened in November 2021, at its best after dark.

**The day:** Karnak · Great Hypostyle Hall · Avenue of Sphinxes · Luxor Temple
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Luxor
**Meals:** Lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Meet and greet at Luxor airport · private transfers

### Day 2 · Luxor → Esna
**The West Bank, then the ship sails**

The Valley of the Kings, more than sixty royal tombs painted with the journey into the afterlife, Tutankhamun’s among them. The Tombs of the Nobles nearby, less visited and in places more vivid than the royal ones. Hatshepsut’s three terraces at Deir el-Bahari, built for the pharaoh her successors tried to erase, and the Colossi of Memnon, about 720 tonnes each. In the afternoon the ship sails south to Esna.

**The day:** Valley of the Kings · Tombs of the Nobles · Temple of Hatshepsut · Colossi of Memnon
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Esna
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers · by cruise

**Optional on this day:**
- The tombs of Tutankhamun, Seti I and Ramses VI: extra tickets

### Day 3 · Esna → Edfu → Kom Ombo
**The Temple of Horus**

Edfu’s Temple of Horus, built between 237 and 57 BC: not the oldest temple, but the most complete, roof, walls, sanctuary and pylons intact, with black granite falcons of Horus throughout. The ship then sails on to Kom Ombo.

**The day:** Temple of Horus at Edfu
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Kom Ombo
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By cruise

### Day 4 · Kom Ombo → Aswan
**The double temple, and Aswan**

Kom Ombo, two of every corridor and sanctuary for Sobek and Horus the Elder, with its mummified crocodiles. The ship reaches Aswan, where the light on the river is at its best late in the day. In Aswan, the High Dam, completed in 1970, which reshaped Egypt and submerged much of Nubia; the Unfinished Obelisk, 42 metres, never moved, showing exactly how monoliths were cut; and a felucca past Kitchener’s Island, planted as a garden in the 1890s.

**The day:** Temple of Kom Ombo · Crocodile Museum · Aswan High Dam · Unfinished Obelisk · felucca
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Aswan
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By cruise · private transfers · felucca

### Day 9 · Hurghada
**The reef week begins**

Six days to use the coast properly, with several left empty by design. Choose from the Giftun Islands, a marine-protected archipelago about an hour offshore, for drift snorkels over the coral gardens; for certified divers, the SS Thistlegorm off the Strait of Gubal, sunk in October 1941 with its cargo of motorcycles and trucks, and the wrecks of Abu Nuhas; a glass-bottom boat, windsurfing or sailing; a morning in El Dahar, the old town, with its Friday souq, small mosques and coffee houses; or the Eastern Desert, with quad biking, a Bedouin evening and the granite hills behind the coast.

**Overnight:** Hurghada resort, all-inclusive
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers
**Pace:** As much or as little as you like
