## Opening

In Cairo you can stand at the foot of the Great Pyramid before lunch and be bargaining for hand-beaten copper in a medieval bazaar by late afternoon. This private day is built on that contrast: the three pyramids and the Sphinx in the quiet of the morning, then the Fatimid and Mamluk city of Al-Muizz Street and Khan El Khalili in the afternoon. It’s the Cairo classic, at your own pace.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The early start matters most. The plateau is exposed limestone desert with no shade, and from May to September midday temperatures regularly pass 35°C; its first hour belongs to the light and the thinner crowds. Then you cross the city, about 15 kilometres, though traffic can add 30 to 45 minutes, to the old city for the afternoon. Everything after the start bends to your party: a slower midday for children, or the medieval lanes at full walking length.

## Your day

### Morning · The Giza Plateau

**09:00 · Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you in a private air-conditioned car.

**The Great Pyramid of Khufu**
Built around 2560 BC: 146 metres high when finished, about 2.3 million blocks. The figures don’t prepare you for the scale up close, where a single course of stone reaches your shoulder. Its base is aligned to tolerances a modern surveyor would accept.
*Sites:* Great Pyramid of Khufu

**Khafre and Menkaure**
Khafre’s pyramid looks taller than his father’s because it stands on higher ground; near its top, original white Tura limestone casing still clings, showing how all three once looked. Menkaure’s, at about 65 metres, is the smallest, but not a lesser monument: look closely at the stonework.
*Sites:* Pyramid of Khafre · Pyramid of Menkaure

**Inside a pyramid (included).** You go inside the pyramid of Khafre or Menkaure, subject to site rules and availability on the day.

**The Great Sphinx**
Cut from a spur of bedrock, most probably under Khafre: 73 metres long and 20 high, facing due east to the equinox sunrise. Your guide can take you to a quieter angle than the one in most photographs.
*Sites:* Great Sphinx

**Optional extras, if you’ve chosen them**

- A camel ride: extra cost. We work with handlers we know, whose animals are in good condition, and the ride is entirely your choice.

### Afternoon · Al-Muizz Street and Khan El Khalili

**Al-Muizz Street**
The spine of the Fatimid capital, laid out in the 10th century and pedestrianised along its finest stretch: about a kilometre holding a thousand years of Islamic architecture. Stop at the Qalawun complex of the 1280s, madrasa, mausoleum and hospital behind one façade, and at the facing buildings of al-Ghuri, from 1505.
*Sites:* Al-Muizz Street · Qalawun Complex · Al-Ghuri Complex

**Khan El Khalili**
The bazaar founded in 1382 is still a working market. The lanes near the El Hussein Mosque are the busiest with visitors; a few minutes’ walk in any direction brings you to workshops serving local customers. Bargaining is expected.
*Sites:* Khan El Khalili

## Is this day for you?

**A good fit if you:**
- are visiting for the first time and want the pyramids paired with the medieval city
- once rushed the pyramids on a group coach and want to do them properly
- want time with Cairo’s medieval Islamic architecture, not just a glance at it

- The plateau has no shade. If you’re sensitive to heat, tell your guide and the day can be sequenced around it.
- Traffic between Giza and the old city is unpredictable.
- The Grand Egyptian Museum isn’t included, though you’ll see it from the plateau.
