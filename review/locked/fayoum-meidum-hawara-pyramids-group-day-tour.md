## Opening

Egypt’s pyramids don’t end at Giza. South-west of Cairo stand two that show the tradition from the inside: Meidum, which collapsed, and Hawara, a mud-brick mound that once anchored the complex Herodotus called the Labyrinth. This shared day takes in both, then turns into the Fayoum itself, a farmed depression in the desert around the saltwater Lake Qarun. Neither pyramid is glamorous, and that’s the point.

---

## Why the day runs this way

You leave at 07:00, before Cairo’s traffic assembles, so Meidum comes in the morning light and Hawara before midday. Lunch is in the oasis, and the afternoon is the Fayoum’s lake and countryside, a slower, greener close to a day that starts in the desert. It’s physically moderate but long, with several hours in the vehicle.

## Your day

### Morning · Meidum and Hawara

**07:00 · Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you and the rest of the group in an air-conditioned vehicle for the drive south, about an hour and three-quarters.

**The Meidum Pyramid**
Begun under Huni at the end of the Third Dynasty, around 2600 BC, and finished or altered by Snefru. What stands is a three-stepped tower in a skirt of fallen casing: the result of an ancient structural failure. If you like, descend the low, sloping corridor to the burial chamber.
*Sites:* Meidum Pyramid

**The mastaba field**
Around the pyramid lie the mastaba tombs of the court, including that of Rahotep and Nofret. The tombs aren’t open to visitors. Their treasures are in Cairo: the painted statues of Rahotep and Nofret and the Meidum Geese are in the Egyptian Museum on Tahrir Square, and your guide explains where each piece came from.

**The Hawara Pyramid**
About forty-five minutes on. Built by Amenemhat III of the Twelfth Dynasty, around 1850 BC, in mud brick, which is why it now looks more like a hill than a monument. Its mortuary temple was so vast that Herodotus called it the Labyrinth and rated it above the pyramids. The Labyrinth is gone, quarried away over the centuries; your guide explains what the Greek and Roman accounts suggest about its scale.
*Sites:* Hawara Pyramid · the Labyrinth

### Afternoon · The Fayoum

**Lake Qarun and the Fayoum countryside**
Into the oasis: fields watered from the Nile by canal, villages, and Lake Qarun, a saltwater lake that is what remains of a far larger ancient one. About two hours, at an easier pace than the morning.
*Sites:* Fayoum Oasis · Lake Qarun

## Is this day for you?

**A good fit if you:**
- have done Cairo’s main sites and want the pyramid story away from the Giza crowds
- want to see how pyramid-building was learned, by trial and error
- like a day that ends in the countryside rather than in a city

**Worth knowing:**
- A date runs once four travellers have booked it.
- It’s a long day, about eleven hours, with several of them in the vehicle.
- The Meidum corridor is steep and low: not for anyone with claustrophobia or limited mobility. It’s optional.
- The mastaba tombs at Meidum are closed; their statues and paintings are in the Egyptian Museum in Cairo.
- This day doesn’t go to Wadi El Rayan or the Valley of the Whales.
