## Opening

This trip begins in the south. You fly straight into Luxor, ancient Thebes, and cruise south through the river temples to Aswan, where you leave the ship for a night at the Old Cataract, a Mandarin Oriental hotel, above the first cataract of the Nile. Then Abu Simbel, and the flight north to finish at Giza and the Grand Egyptian Museum.

---

## Why this route works

Starting in the south saves the long first leg from Cairo, and the river runs in one direction, so nothing is seen twice. Four nights on the cruise cover Luxor's two banks, Edfu, Kom Ombo and Aswan with someone else doing the travelling. The night at the Old Cataract gives Aswan a day of its own instead of a morning's disembarkation. Cairo comes last, when the museum can show you what the tombs and temples once held.

### Day 2 · Luxor
**Board the cruise, then Karnak and Luxor Temple**

You board your cruise ship for lunch. In the afternoon, Karnak, a temple city added to over fifteen centuries, with the Great Hypostyle Hall of 134 columns up to 21 metres. Then Luxor Temple, at the end of the Avenue of Sphinxes, which reopened in November 2021, as the light goes.

**The day:** Karnak · Great Hypostyle Hall · Luxor Temple · Avenue of Sphinxes
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Luxor
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers

### Day 3 · Luxor · the West Bank
**The Valley of the Kings, Hatshepsut and the Colossi**

The Valley of the Kings, more than sixty royal tombs cut into the cliffs, their corridors painted with scenes from the Book of the Dead. Hatshepsut's colonnaded temple at Deir el-Bahari, rising in terraces against the cliff, and the Colossi of Memnon, two seated figures of Amenhotep III about 18 metres tall. Then the cruise moves south.

**The day:** Valley of the Kings · Temple of Hatshepsut · Colossi of Memnon
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers · by cruise

**Optional on this day:**

### Day 4 · Edfu and Kom Ombo
**Two river temples**

Edfu's Temple of Horus, completed around 57 BC, its enclosure wall, roof, halls and sanctuary still standing: the clearest picture on the river of how a working temple was laid out. In the afternoon, Kom Ombo, its plan doubled for two gods, Sobek the crocodile and Horus the Elder, with two doorways, two halls and two sanctuaries, and mummified crocodiles in the small museum beside it.

**The day:** Temple of Horus at Edfu · Temple of Kom Ombo · Crocodile Museum
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By cruise

### Day 5 · Aswan
**The High Dam, Philae and a felucca**

The Aswan High Dam, completed in 1970, which created Lake Nasser, and the Unfinished Obelisk, about 42 metres long, cracked and abandoned in its granite quarry. By motorboat to Philae, the Temple of Isis, moved block by block to Agilkia Island in the 1970s. A late-afternoon felucca around Kitchener's Island.

**The day:** Aswan High Dam · Unfinished Obelisk · Temple of Philae · felucca
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Aswan
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers · motorboat to Philae · felucca

### Day 6 · Aswan
**A day at the Old Cataract**

Off the ship after breakfast and across to the Old Cataract, on a granite outcrop above the first cataract, where the Nile runs fast between the boulders. The day is yours. Your guide can take you to the Nubian Museum, which tells the story of the land Lake Nasser flooded, or the Botanical Garden on Kitchener's Island; or stay on the terrace and watch the feluccas.

**Overnight:** The Old Cataract, Aswan
**Meals:** Breakfast (on the cruise)
**Travel:** Private transfer to the hotel
**Pace:** Easy

### Day 7 · Aswan → Abu Simbel → Cairo
**The temples of Ramses II, then the flight north**

A short morning flight south to Abu Simbel: Ramses II's two cliff temples of the 13th century BC, the four seated figures of the Great Temple twenty metres high, and the smaller temple for his queen Nefertari and the goddess Hathor. Between 1964 and 1968 both were cut into blocks and rebuilt 65 metres higher, out of reach of Lake Nasser. In the afternoon, the flight to Cairo.

**The day:** Abu Simbel · Great Temple of Ramses II · Temple of Nefertari
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast
**Travel:** Domestic flights Aswan → Abu Simbel → Cairo (not included) · private transfers
**Pace:** A day of airports, worth it

### Day 8 · Giza
**The pyramids, the Grand Egyptian Museum and a Nile dinner cruise**

**Morning.** The Giza plateau: the pyramids of Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure, built around 2500 BC. You go inside the pyramid of Khafre or Menkaure, subject to site rules and availability on the day. Then the panorama viewpoint, the Great Sphinx and the Valley Temple.

**Afternoon.** The Grand Egyptian Museum, at the edge of the plateau, with the complete Tutankhamun collection. After a week of tombs and temples, you now see what they held.

**Evening.** A dinner cruise on the Nile.

**The day:** Giza pyramids · inside Khafre or Menkaure · panorama viewpoint · Great Sphinx · Valley Temple · Grand Egyptian Museum · Nile dinner cruise
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers
**Pace:** A full day

**Optional on this day:**
- Inside the Great Pyramid of Khufu: extra ticket, subject to availability and site rules on the day
- A camel ride on the plateau: extra cost. We work with handlers we know, whose animals are in good condition, and the ride is entirely your choice.
