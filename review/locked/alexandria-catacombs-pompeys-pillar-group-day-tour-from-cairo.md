## Opening

Alexandria was home to the Library, the Lighthouse and Alexander’s tomb, and almost none of it is visible now: the modern city was built over the ancient one. What survives is what lay underground, what was too heavy to move, and what has been collected since. This shared day from Cairo takes a small group to four of those survivals: a catacomb, a lone Roman column, a museum of the city’s Greek and Roman centuries and a marble theatre. It’s a lot of road, and we’ll say so plainly.

## Why the day runs this way

Alexandria is about 220 kilometres from Cairo, around three hours each way, so six of the day’s ten hours are on the road. The 07:00 start gets you there by mid-morning. The catacombs and Pompey’s Pillar come first, the museum puts what you’ve seen in order, and the amphitheatre closes the visit before the drive back.

## Your day

### Morning · The road north, the catacombs and the pillar

**07:00 · Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you and the rest of the group in an air-conditioned vehicle for the drive north to the Mediterranean.

**The Catacombs of Kom El Shuqafa**
Cut into the rock in the late 1st or early 2nd century AD, and found again in 1900 when a donkey fell through a shaft. Three levels down, past a rotunda and a hall where mourners held funeral banquets, to the main tomb: Anubis in a Roman soldier’s pose, Egyptian gods in Greek dress. About an hour, underground.
*Sites:* Catacombs of Kom El Shuqafa

**Pompey’s Pillar**
Nothing to do with Pompey: a single shaft of red Aswan granite, raised in 297 AD for the emperor Diocletian, at the heart of the Serapeum, the great temple of Serapis destroyed in 391 AD. The column survived, alone above the city, with two sphinx fragments at its foot.
*Sites:* Pompey’s Pillar · Serapeum

### Midday · The Greco-Roman Museum and lunch

**The Greco-Roman Museum**
Reopened in 2023 after a long restoration, with a rich collection from about 300 BC to the 7th century AD: portrait sculpture, terracottas, papyri, coins and carved sarcophagi. Your guide checks what’s on show before you arrive.
*Sites:* Greco-Roman Museum

**Lunch at a local restaurant (included)**

### Afternoon · The amphitheatre and the road back

**The Roman Amphitheatre at Kom El Dikka**
Built in Roman times and used until the 7th century: thirteen curved rows of white marble around a small stage, made for lectures, music and readings rather than gladiators. Around it, excavated streets, baths and lecture halls show the city’s everyday civic life. About an hour.
*Sites:* Kom El Dikka Roman Amphitheatre

**Around 17:15 · Back to your hotel**
About three hours’ drive, traffic permitting.
