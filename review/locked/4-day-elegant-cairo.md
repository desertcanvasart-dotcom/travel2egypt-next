## Opening

---

## Why this route works

Cairo’s layers press against each other: mosques over Coptic churches over pharaonic foundations. Four days is modest for a city this dense, so this tour makes a clear choice and stays in Greater Cairo, with no flights and no long drives. Each day keeps to one part of the city. The collection comes before the monuments, so what you see in the galleries in the morning is visible in stone on the plateau in the afternoon.

### Day 2 · Giza
**The Grand Egyptian Museum, then the Giza plateau**

**Morning.** The Grand Egyptian Museum, at the edge of the Giza plateau. The centrepiece is the Tutankhamun galleries: his burial equipment shown together, from the golden throne to the shrines. The visit is focused, not comprehensive, and sets up the afternoon.

**Midday.** Lunch at the Oberoi’s felucca-themed restaurant on the Nile.

**Afternoon.** Giza: the pyramids of Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure, built in the 25th century BC, with the courses of limestone at chest height and the plateau’s sheer extent. You go inside the pyramid of Khafre or Menkaure, subject to site rules and availability on the day; a narrow descending passage leads to the burial chamber. Then the panorama viewpoint, where the three pyramids line up in one frame, and the Great Sphinx and the Valley Temple below. The day ends with a camel ride across the plateau as the light drops. We work with handlers we know, whose animals are in good condition, and the ride is entirely your choice.

**The day:** Grand Egyptian Museum · Tutankhamun galleries · Giza pyramids · inside Khafre or Menkaure · panorama viewpoint · Great Sphinx · Valley Temple · camel ride
**Overnight:** Four Seasons Hotel Cairo at The First Residence
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch

### Day 3 · Old Cairo
**The royal mummies, Coptic Cairo, the Sultan Hassan and Al-Rifai mosques, and Khan El Khalili**

**Morning.** The National Museum of Egyptian Civilisation in Fustat, on the edge of Ain el-Sira lake, and its Royal Mummies Hall: twenty-two kings and queens, among them Ramses II, Seti I and Hatshepsut, shown with a care the old museum never managed. Then Cairo’s other history, a few minutes away, back through Byzantium to the early Christians. The Hanging Church, al-Muallaqa, built over two towers of a Roman fortress, its nave hung between them and dense with icons and carved screens. The Coptic Museum beside it. The Ben Ezra Synagogue, mostly 19th-century, where the Cairo Geniza, a cache of some 300,000 manuscript fragments, was found.

**Late morning.** Below the Citadel, two mosques face each other across a narrow street. The Mosque-Madrasa of Sultan Hassan, built by the Mamluk sultan an-Nasir Hasan in the 14th century, with its vast vaulted iwans around an open courtyard. And Al-Rifai, finished in the early 20th century in the same monumental spirit, where members of Egypt’s royal family are buried, as is the last Shah of Iran.

**Lunch at the Naguib Mahfouz Restaurant.** Lunch today is at the Naguib Mahfouz Restaurant in Khan El Khalili, the bazaar the Nobel laureate wrote about.

**Afternoon.** Free time in the bazaar, still selling spices, metalwork, textiles and jewellery after six centuries.

**The day:** National Museum of Egyptian Civilisation · Royal Mummies Hall · Hanging Church · Coptic Museum · Ben Ezra Synagogue · Mosque-Madrasa of Sultan Hassan · Al-Rifai Mosque · Khan El Khalili
**Overnight:** Four Seasons Hotel Cairo at The First Residence

## Is this journey for you?

**A good fit if you:**
- want Cairo on its own terms, not as a stopover
- would rather understand one city than skim three
- are drawn to the Coptic and Jewish layers, not only the pharaonic

**Worth knowing:**
- The pyramid interior means crouching in a narrow passage.
- Day 3 includes two mosques and a synagogue, as well as churches: dress modestly, with shoulders and knees covered. Women need a headscarf in the mosques (bring your own), and shoes come off at the door.
- On a Friday, the mosques close to visitors during the midday prayer, so that day the two mosques move to after lunch.
