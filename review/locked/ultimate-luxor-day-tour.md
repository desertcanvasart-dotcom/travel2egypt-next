## Opening

Luxor sits on top of Thebes, capital of the New Kingdom, and within ten kilometres you'll find more great monuments than most countries hold. This private day crosses both banks to see five of them: the Valley of the Kings, Hatshepsut's temple and the Colossi of Memnon on the West Bank, then Karnak and Luxor Temple on the East. The kings were buried on one bank and worshipped on the other; seeing both in a day makes the whole system make sense.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The early start isn't theatre. From May to September, Luxor passes 40°C by midday, and the tombs and Hatshepsut's open terraces are best before the heat. So the West Bank comes first, then lunch, then the East Bank temples in the afternoon, finishing at Luxor Temple as the light softens. It's a long day, about ten hours; if that gives you pause, the same monuments split neatly into two gentler days, along the line the river already draws.

## Your day

### Morning · The West Bank

**Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you early in a private air-conditioned car.

**The Valley of the Kings**
The burial ground of the New Kingdom pharaohs, about 1550–1070 BC, with more than sixty tombs in the Theban hills. Your ticket covers three, chosen with your guide from those open on the day; Ramses VI, Seti I and Merenptah carry some of the most complete funerary texts to survive.
*Sites:* Valley of the Kings

**The Temple of Hatshepsut**
At Deir el-Bahari, the temple Senenmut designed for the woman who ruled as pharaoh around 1473–1458 BC: three colonnaded terraces against the cliff, with reliefs of the voyage to Punt and of her divine birth.
*Sites:* Temple of Hatshepsut

**The Colossi of Memnon**
Two seated figures of Amenhotep III, about 18 metres tall, standing in the fields since around 1350 BC where his vast temple once was. A brief stop on the way back to the river, but one that sets you in the landscape.
*Sites:* Colossi of Memnon

**Optional extra, if you've chosen it**
- Tutankhamun's tomb in the Valley of the Kings: extra ticket. Its small painted burial chamber keeps its colour with unusual intensity.

### Midday · Lunch

**Lunch at a local restaurant (included)**
Between the two banks. Drinks aren't included.

### Afternoon · The East Bank

**Karnak**
Not one temple but a city of them, built over some 1,500 years. The Hypostyle Hall's 134 columns, up to 21 metres high, need the best part of an hour on their own; then the Sacred Lake, the ram-headed sphinxes and the obelisks of Hatshepsut and Thutmose I.
*Sites:* Karnak · Great Hypostyle Hall · Sacred Lake

**Luxor Temple**
A short drive south, and a fitting close. Built mainly under Amenhotep III and extended by Ramses II, whose colossi flank the pylon. Here Amun's statue arrived each year at the Opet festival. A Roman chapel occupies one court, and a still-active mosque sits high on the debris that once buried the columns to their capitals.
*Sites:* Luxor Temple
