## Opening

Two days in Luxor, five nights on the river in a traditional sailing dahabiya, and a final day and night in Aswan. You arrive to temples and the West Bank, step aboard on the third morning, and come off the boat in Aswan with time left for the island, the museum and the market before you fly home.

## Why this program works

A dahabiya is the best part of the trip, but it starts on a fixed morning and ends on a fixed morning, and a guest who flies in that day sees none of Luxor and meets the boat tired. So we put Luxor first. Two full days on both banks, with the balloon over the West Bank and a carriage ride through the town at night, then the river, then Aswan at a walking pace. The boat is the same one a guest could book alone. What we add is the week around it.

## Your nine days

> **Your arrival day depends on your boat.** Five boats (El Nil, Malouka, Adelaïde, Poppee and Najafa) sail on Mondays: you arrive in Luxor on Saturday, board on Monday, leave the boat on Saturday morning and fly home on Sunday. Four boats (Meroe, Agatha, Roman and Dendera) sail on Tuesdays, and the same sequence runs one day later.

### Day 1 · Luxor
**The temple of Hathor, the great precinct of Amun, and a lit evening**

You are met on arrival in Luxor and driven north to Dendera, about an hour by road. The Temple of Hathor, goddess of music, love and the sky, is one of the best preserved in Egypt, built mostly in the Ptolemaic and Roman periods. Inside you go down to the crypts, and up to the roof, where the Osiris chapel once held the famous round zodiac ceiling; the original has been in the Louvre since 1821. Lunch is included.

Back in Luxor you check in to the hotel. In the afternoon, Karnak: the precinct of Amun, built and added to over some two thousand years, with its Great Hypostyle Hall of 134 columns. In the evening, Luxor Temple, lit after dark, once the day's crowds have thinned. Dinner is at the hotel.

**The day:** Temple of Hathor at Dendera · Karnak · Luxor Temple by night
**Overnight:** Luxor hotel
**Meals:** Lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private vehicle


---

### Day 2 · Luxor
**The balloon, the Valley of the Kings, Hatshepsut and Medinet Habu**

The day starts before sunrise with a balloon flight over the West Bank, the green fields below you and the desert cliffs ahead, weather permitting; if the flight is cancelled, the balloon is refunded. After breakfast, the side of the river the Egyptians kept for the dead. The Valley of the Kings, more than sixty royal tombs cut from about 1550 BC, with Tutankhamun's tomb included in your visit: small beside the tombs of the great kings, but the one whose contents, found intact in 1922, shaped the world's picture of a royal burial. Then Hatshepsut's three terraces at Deir el-Bahari, and Medinet Habu, the great temple of Ramses III, and the Colossi of Memnon on the way back. Lunch is included, then you return to the hotel to rest.

After dinner at the hotel, a horse-drawn carriage takes you through the streets of Luxor.

**The day:** Balloon flight · Valley of the Kings · Tomb of Tutankhamun · Temple of Hatshepsut · Medinet Habu · Colossi of Memnon
**Overnight:** Luxor hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private vehicle · balloon · horse-drawn carriage


---

### Day 3 · Luxor → Esna
**Check out, and board your dahabiya**

You check out in the morning and drive south to Esna, a working market town on the west bank, where your dahabiya is waiting. The Temple of Khnum, built between about the 3rd century BC and the 3rd century AD, lies nine metres below the street, reached by going down into the excavation: ancient carved ceilings below, apartment blocks above. Esna itself is a busy, unpolished town, worth an afternoon. From here the boat's fixed program begins.

**The day:** Temple of Khnum · Esna market town
**Overnight:** Your dahabiya, at Esna
**Meals:** Lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By road Luxor → Esna


---

### Day 4 · El Kab and Edfu
**The temples the big cruise ships skip**

El Kab, on the east bank and rarely busy: the Temple of Nekhbet, the vulture goddess who was one of the two patrons of kingship, and decorated rock-cut tombs above the river that repay close attention. Then south to Edfu, by horse-drawn carriage from the river, for the Temple of Horus, built between 237 and 57 BC, with its outer walls, roof, inner sanctuary and most of its reliefs still in place.

**The day:** El Kab · Temple of Nekhbet · El Kab rock tombs · Temple of Horus at Edfu
**Overnight:** Your dahabiya
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By dahabiya · horse-drawn carriage


---

### Day 5 · On the Nile → Gebel el-Silsila
**A sailing day, a swim, and a valley of inscriptions**

A deliberate change of pace. The dahabiya sails under her own wind when the breeze allows, and the morning goes to watching the Nile move past. In the afternoon, a swim from an island, and a walk into the desert to a valley of rock inscriptions, some from before the pharaohs, modest beside the great temples but with a weight no museum cabinet can give them. The evening mooring is at the foot of the cliffs at Gebel el-Silsila.

**The day:** Sailing · island swim · desert valley inscriptions
**Overnight:** Your dahabiya, Gebel el-Silsila
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By dahabiya · on foot
**Pace:** Slow

---

### Day 6 · Gebel el-Silsila
**The quarry gorge and the temple of Horemheb**

The Nile narrows between sandstone cliffs here, and the stone cut from them built Karnak, Luxor, Edfu and Kom Ombo; the quarry faces still carry the workers' tool marks and graffiti. Large cruise ships cannot stop here. The main monument is the small rock temple of Horemheb, the general who became pharaoh after the turmoil of the Amarna period, carved into the cliff above the river. The afternoon is on the water: swimming from the bank and lunch on a shaded deck before the boat continues south.

**The day:** Gebel el-Silsila quarries · Speos of Horemheb · swim
**Overnight:** Your dahabiya
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By dahabiya · on foot
**Pace:** Slow


---

### Day 7 · Kom Ombo → Aswan
**The double temple**

Around the bend to Kom Ombo, one of the strangest buildings in Egypt: a Ptolemaic temple for Sobek, the crocodile god linked to fertility and the flood, and Horus the Elder, in perfect mirror image, with two entrances, two halls and two sanctuaries. The Crocodile Museum next door shows how important the animal once was here. Then on to Aswan, where the dahabiya moors near the bridge for the night, as regulations require.

**The day:** Temple of Kom Ombo · Crocodile Museum
**Overnight:** Your dahabiya, near the Aswan bridge
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By dahabiya


---

### Day 8 · Aswan
**Leave the boat, and spend your day in Aswan**

After breakfast you leave the dahabiya and go ashore. First a felucca, the traditional Nile sailing boat, across the water to the Aswan Botanical Garden on its island, planted in the 20th century with trees and flowers from many countries. Lunch is included. In the afternoon, the Nubian Museum, which tells the story of Nubia from prehistory to the present, including the villages lost under Lake Nasser. Then a walk through Aswan's local market, with its spices, hibiscus, cotton and handicrafts, before you settle into the hotel for dinner.

**The day:** Felucca ride · Aswan Botanical Garden · Nubian Museum · Aswan market
**Overnight:** Aswan hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private vehicle · felucca


---

### Day 9 · Aswan
**The temple of Isis, and the dam that moved it**

In the morning, Philae. The temple of Isis was moved to Agilkia Island between 1972 and 1980, stone by stone, when Lake Nasser rose behind the High Dam. A short boat crossing takes you to the island, with its pylons and the temple's inner rooms. Then the High Dam itself, completed in 1970: it controlled the flood, generated power and created Lake Nasser, and more than 100,000 people were displaced from their villages. You are then driven to the airport for your flight. Breakfast only; no lunch is included today.

**The day:** Temple of Philae (Isis) · Aswan High Dam
**Overnight:** —
**Meals:** Breakfast
**Travel:** Private vehicle · boat to Philae
