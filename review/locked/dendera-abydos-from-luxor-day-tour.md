## Opening

Two of the most rewarding temples in Upper Egypt lie north of Luxor, and most visitors never reach them. The temple of Seti I at Abydos, with some of the finest relief carving in Egypt, and Dendera's temple of Hathor, with its painted ceilings and rooftop chapels, are both largely intact and both quiet. This private day reaches both in one long arc, for travellers who love the texture of carved stone and the logic of myth.

---

## Why the day runs this way

Abydos is the furthest point, about two and a half hours north of Luxor, so you go there first, while the morning is fresh. Dendera lies on the way back, about an hour from Luxor, which breaks the return drive in two. The driving is the price of the solitude: far fewer people make it here than to Karnak or Abu Simbel. With a private guide, you spend that quiet as you like, lingering wherever holds you longest.

## Your day

### Morning · Abydos

**Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you early in a private air-conditioned car for the drive north.

**The Temple of Seti I**
Abydos was the burial ground of Egypt's first kings and the great centre of Osiris; to be buried here, or at least remembered here, was the hope of Egyptians for two thousand years. Seti I's temple of around 1280 BC has seven sanctuaries and painted reliefs that feel immediate rather than ancient. His son Ramses II finished it, and you can see where the hand changes.
*Sites:* Abydos · Temple of Seti I

**The King List and the Osireion**
In the Gallery of Lists, Seti names seventy-six kings from Menes to himself, leaving out Hatshepsut and the Amarna kings: memory as politics. Behind the temple, the Osireion, a half-flooded cenotaph of huge granite blocks built in deliberately ancient style. Access inside depends on conditions; the view from above is enough.
*Sites:* Abydos King List · Osireion

**The Temple of Ramses II**
A short walk north: smaller and less complete, with painted reliefs worth the detour.

### Afternoon · Dendera, on the way back

**The Temple of Hathor, Dendera**
Built mostly between the late 1st century BC and the 1st century AD, in one campaign, which gives it rare unity. Twenty-four columns crowned with Hathor's face fill the great hall, their faces damaged when early Christians used it as a church; deeper inside, the colour is startling. On the ceiling, a cast of the Dendera Zodiac (the original is in the Louvre); on the roof, up a steep stair, the chapels of Osiris's resurrection keep their ochres and blues.
*Sites:* Temple of Hathor, Dendera · Dendera Zodiac

**Around the temple**
A dry sacred lake, a Roman birth house for Hathor's son Ihy, and a Coptic basilica built into the precinct: one site, several faiths.

**The drive back to Luxor**
About an hour, arriving in the evening.
