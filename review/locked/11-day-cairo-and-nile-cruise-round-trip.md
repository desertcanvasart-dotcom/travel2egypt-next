## Opening

A round trip built around the river: the pyramids, then seven nights on a five-star Nile cruise ship from Luxor to Aswan and back, with a leisure day at the centre, and Cairo again to close with the Grand Egyptian Museum and a dinner cruise.

---

## Why this route works

Many Nile trips sail one way and hurry. This one treats the Nile as the way you travel, and seven nights is long enough for the river to stop being an experience and start being the way things are. The monuments arrive at the riverbank instead of asking you to drive to them. Sailing south and then north also lets you see each temple once, in the better light, and keeps Karnak for the end of the cruise rather than the start.

## Your eleven days

### Day 1 · Cairo
**Giza, Memphis and Saqqara**

You’re met at Cairo airport and go straight to the Giza plateau: the pyramids of Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure, built around 2500 BC, and the Great Sphinx below them. In the afternoon, Memphis, the first capital of a united Egypt, with the fallen colossus of Ramesses II and an alabaster sphinx, and Saqqara, where the Step Pyramid of Djoser, built around 2650 BC, is the oldest large stone building in the world. If you land late in the day, we move Memphis and Saqqara to the free morning of Day 10.

**The day:** Giza pyramids · Great Sphinx · Memphis · Saqqara
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Lunch
**Travel:** Meet and greet at the airport · private transfers

**Optional on this day:**
- Inside one of the pyramids: extra ticket

---

### Day 2 · Cairo → Luxor
**Flight south, and aboard**

An early flight to Luxor, where you board the Nile cruise ship that is your home for the next seven nights. Lunch is on board and the afternoon is for settling in. In the evening, a horse-drawn carriage through Luxor’s streets and coffee at a local ahwa, one of the open-fronted coffeehouses that anchor Egyptian street life.

**The day:** Luxor, in the evening
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Luxor
**Meals:** Lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Domestic flight Cairo → Luxor · private transfers · horse-drawn carriage
**Pace:** Easy

---

### Day 3 · Luxor
**The West Bank**

The heaviest day of the cruise. The Valley of the Kings, with more than sixty royal tombs cut into a desert valley, their corridors painted with scenes from the funerary texts, and Tutankhamun’s tomb. The three colonnaded terraces of the temple of Hatshepsut at Deir el-Bahari, built for a woman who ruled as pharaoh for about twenty years. The Colossi of Memnon, two seated quartzite figures about eighteen metres tall, to close the day. Tutankhamun’s treasures themselves are at the Grand Egyptian Museum, waiting for you at the end of the trip.

**The day:** Valley of the Kings · Temple of Hatshepsut · Colossi of Memnon
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers · by cruise

**Optional on this day:**
- The tombs of Tutankhamun, Seti I and Ramesses VI: extra tickets

---

### Day 4 · Luxor → Kom Ombo → Aswan
**A river day, and the crocodile temple**

The ship sails south, through the lock at Esna, and the morning is for the deck and the water. In the afternoon, Kom Ombo, a double temple with two of every doorway and sanctuary for Sobek, the crocodile god, and Horus the Elder, and a small museum of mummified crocodiles. The ship reaches Aswan in the evening.

**The day:** Temple of Kom Ombo · Crocodile Museum
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Aswan
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By cruise · private transfers
**Pace:** Easy

---

### Day 5 · Aswan
**The High Dam, the Unfinished Obelisk, Philae and a felucca**

Aswan is where the Nile narrows toward the first cataract. The High Dam, completed in 1970, which created Lake Nasser and is the reason the temples of Abu Simbel had to be moved. The Unfinished Obelisk, 42 metres long, abandoned in its granite quarry with a crack through it. Philae, the temple of Isis, rebuilt block by block on Agilkia Island between 1972 and 1980, by motorboat. As dusk falls, a felucca around Kitchener’s Island.

**The day:** Aswan High Dam · Unfinished Obelisk · Temple of Philae · felucca
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Aswan
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers · motorboat to Philae · felucca

---

### Day 6 · Aswan
**A leisure day on the water**

A day left open on purpose. Use the ship’s facilities, or go ashore for the covered market and the Nubian quarter. The ship begins sailing north during the day.

**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Pace:** As much or as little as you like

**Optional on this day:**
- Abu Simbel by road, a long day south to the temples of Ramesses II, leaving before dawn and back on board by midday: extra

---

### Day 7 · Edfu
**The temple of Horus, and the river north**

Edfu, where you go ashore by horse-drawn carriage to the Temple of Horus, built between 237 and 57 BC: not the oldest temple in Egypt, but the most complete, with its walls, roof, sanctuary and pylon intact. The ship then sails on toward Luxor, and the rest of the day is yours on the river.

**The day:** Temple of Horus at Edfu
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By cruise · horse-drawn carriage
**Pace:** Easy

---

### Day 8 · Luxor
**Karnak and Luxor Temple**

The East Bank, kept for the end of the cruise. Karnak, built and rebuilt over some 1,500 years, with a Great Hypostyle Hall of 134 columns up to 21 metres tall: less a temple than a temple city. In the afternoon, Luxor Temple, joined to Karnak by the Avenue of Sphinxes, which reopened in November 2021. A last night on board.

**The day:** Karnak · Great Hypostyle Hall · Luxor Temple · Avenue of Sphinxes
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Luxor
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers · by cruise
**Pace:** A full day

---

### Day 9 · Luxor → Cairo
**The Grand Egyptian Museum, the Citadel and Khan el-Khalili**

You leave the ship after breakfast and fly to Cairo. The Grand Egyptian Museum, fully open since 1 November 2025, with the complete Tutankhamun collection that the Valley of the Kings no longer holds. Then the Citadel, which has commanded the Cairo skyline since the 12th century, with the alabaster mosque of Muhammad Ali. Khan el-Khalili, trading since the 14th century, to finish.

**The day:** Grand Egyptian Museum · Citadel of Saladin · Mosque of Muhammad Ali · Khan el-Khalili
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast (on the ship) · lunch
**Travel:** Domestic flight Luxor → Cairo · private transfers
**Pace:** A full day

---

### Day 10 · Cairo
**Cairo’s own day, and a Nile dinner cruise**

The morning is free. If you would like it, we can take you to the older Egyptian Museum on Tahrir Square, or to Memphis and Saqqara if you moved them from Day 1. In the evening, a dinner cruise on the Nile closes the journey: the lit city on both banks, on the same water that carried your ship past Karnak.

**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers
**Pace:** Easy

---

**Departure**

Breakfast, and the transfer to Cairo airport.

**Overnight:** —
**Meals:** Breakfast
**Travel:** Private transfer to the airport
