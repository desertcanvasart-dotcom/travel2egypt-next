## Opening

At night, Cairo changes. The heat lets go, the edges soften and the Nile becomes something worth sitting beside. A dinner cruise isn’t the most original idea in Cairo, but it’s one of the better ones: a proper meal, belly dancing and Tanoura to a live band, and an unhurried drift past the lit-up city. It asks nothing of you, which is exactly why it works after a long sightseeing day.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The boat keeps its own clock: a fixed boarding window, then dinner, then band, dancer and Tanoura in their set order. So our part is around it: a pickup timed to the boarding window, and choosing the right night of your Cairo stay. We like it after your heaviest sightseeing day, as a reward. The one choice to make is the kind of dinner you’d like: a buffet on the *Nile Pharaoh*, or a set menu on the *Nile Maxim*.

## Your evening

### Evening · On the Nile

**Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver collects you in a private air-conditioned car and takes you to the dock of the boat you’ve chosen.

**Choose your boat and your dinner**
- **The *Nile Pharaoh*: a buffet.** Help yourself, at your own pace, from Egyptian favourites, grilled meats, rice, mezze and fresh bread, alongside international dishes.
- **The *Nile Maxim*: a set menu.** A served dinner, course by course, at your table.

Both boats moor along the Nile in central Cairo, with windows wide enough to see the river.

**The show**
Belly dancing first, to live Arabic music: footwork, isolations and a difficult costume, all on a moving boat. Then the Tanoura, the dancer turning without pause while the layered skirt fans into a disc of colour. Each runs fifteen to twenty minutes, with the band playing between.

**Cairo by night**
Through the windows: the lit hotels along the Corniche, the Cairo Tower on Gezira Island, the bridges across the river, all at quarter-throttle.
*Sites:* the Nile · Cairo Tower (from the water)

**Back to your hotel**
The evening ends when you’re home.
