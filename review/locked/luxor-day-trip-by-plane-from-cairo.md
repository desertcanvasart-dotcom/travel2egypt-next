## Opening

Luxor stands on ancient Thebes, capital of the New Kingdom. The East Bank was for the living, with temples where pharaohs stood before their gods; the West Bank was for the dead, with tombs cut deep into the hills. This private day flies you south from Cairo and crosses the river between the two: Karnak and Luxor Temple in the morning, Hatshepsut’s temple, the Valley of the Kings and the Colossi of Memnon in the afternoon. A day isn’t enough to understand Luxor, but it’s enough to be changed by it.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The train from Cairo takes nine to twelve hours; the flight takes about one. For a day trip, the plane is the only way to leave useful time on the ground. You land in the morning and start on the East Bank, then cross to the West Bank after lunch, and finish at the Colossi, with a local market if time allows, before the evening flight. It moves at a steady march, with Karnak alone able to fill a morning, so the day is about choosing where to linger.

## Your day

### Morning · Flight to Luxor and the East Bank

**Pickup and the flight south**
Your driver takes you to Cairo airport for the morning flight to Luxor, usually leaving between 06:00 and 08:00. Your Egyptologist meets you on arrival.

**Karnak**
About 1,500 years of temple-building, from the Middle Kingdom to the Ptolemies, dedicated to Amun, Mut and Khonsu. The Great Hypostyle Hall holds 134 sandstone columns, the tallest 21 metres high, a scale meant to shrink the human and enlarge the divine. It works.
*Sites:* Karnak · Great Hypostyle Hall

**Luxor Temple**
Begun by Amenhotep III around 1390 BC and extended by Ramses II, at the end of the Avenue of Sphinxes, reopened in 2021, along which Amun’s statue was carried each year from Karnak. One of the two entrance obelisks still stands; the other is in Paris. The mosque of Abu el-Haggag sits within the ruins.
*Sites:* Luxor Temple · Avenue of Sphinxes

### Midday · Lunch

**Lunch at a local restaurant (included)**
Before crossing the river. Drinks aren’t included.

### Afternoon · The West Bank

**The Temple of Hatshepsut**
Set into a bay of the cliffs at Deir el-Bahari: three colonnaded terraces of clean horizontal lines against the vertical rock, built for one of the few women to rule as pharaoh. Inside, reliefs of the expedition to Punt and of her divine birth keep much of their paint.
*Sites:* Temple of Hatshepsut

**The Valley of the Kings**
The burial place of New Kingdom pharaohs from Thutmose I to Ramses XI, in a hidden valley behind the hills. The standard ticket covers the tombs open on the day, which rotate for conservation; your guide picks the most rewarding. Their painted walls keep colour that astonishes.
*Sites:* Valley of the Kings

**The Colossi of Memnon**
Two seated figures of Amenhotep III, about 18 metres tall, that once guarded his vast mortuary temple, now almost entirely gone. Worn, massive and slightly surreal on the flat farmland: a good last image.
*Sites:* Colossi of Memnon

**A Luxor market, if time allows**
Before the airport, a walk through a local market: a lively cultural close to the day.

**The flight back to Cairo**
Usually around 21:00–22:00. Your driver meets you in Cairo and takes you to your hotel.
