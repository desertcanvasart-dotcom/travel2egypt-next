## Opening

A private Cairo and Alexandria route paced for families: a felucca on the Nile on the first evening, the pyramids and a children’s museum built around models and making things, a day by the Mediterranean, and Old Cairo before you fly home, with enough room between sites for the children to keep up.

---

## Why this route works

A standard four-day Cairo plan crams in sites and falls apart with children by the second afternoon. This one keeps the headline monuments and builds in the moments a six-year-old will actually remember: a felucca on the first evening, a camel ride at Giza, an afternoon at the Children’s Museum, a seafood lunch by the harbour in Alexandria. The Children’s Museum is the heart of it. Ancient Egypt is explained there through models, replicas and things to build, so the pyramids the children saw in the morning become something they understand in the afternoon. Your Egyptologist is briefed for the children’s ages and works at your pace.

### Day 2 · Cairo
**The pyramids, the Children’s Museum and Khan El Khalili**

**Morning.** The pyramids of Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure, built around 2500 BC, and the Sphinx, whose face holds a child’s attention longer than any photo. You go inside the pyramid of Khafre or Menkaure, subject to site rules and availability on the day; a narrow descending passage leads to the burial chamber. Then the panorama viewpoint, where the three pyramids line up in one frame, the Great Sphinx and the Valley Temple, sand games at the back of the plateau, where the crowds thin, and a camel ride. We work with handlers we know, whose animals are in good condition, and the ride is entirely your choice.

**Midday.** Lunch at a local restaurant.

**Afternoon · The Children’s Museum.** In six basement halls of the Egyptian Museum in Tahrir Square, entered from the building’s western side, ancient Egypt is told through LEGO constructions, replicas and educational models: how the pyramids were built, the Sphinx, writing and religion, death and the afterlife, a replica of Tutankhamun’s mask and a statue of Ramses II. After a morning at the real pyramids, this is where the children see how they were made.

Your guide shapes the visit around the youngest child:
- **Ages 4–5, “Become a pyramid builder”:** a short story, the construction model, then building something of their own, and telling you what they made.
- **Ages 6–8, “Young Egyptian explorer”:** how people built, wrote and dressed, with questions to solve, such as how would you move this stone?
- **Ages 9–12, “Museum detective”:** clues that link the models to a few chosen original objects in the main museum upstairs, comparing shapes, materials and purposes.

A LEGO or art workshop is included, arranged in advance as part of the afternoon wherever the museum can confirm one for your date. With the workshop, allow two to three hours in all.

**Evening.** Depending on energy, Khan El Khalili, the old bazaar, with lanterns, spices and somewhere to try feteer or kunafa.

**The day:** Giza pyramids · inside Khafre or Menkaure · panorama viewpoint · Great Sphinx · Valley Temple · camel ride · the Children’s Museum · Khan El Khalili

### Day 3 · Alexandria
**The Mediterranean day**

About three hours north by the desert road, to the city Alexander the Great founded: Hellenistic and Roman rather than pharaonic, a good contrast with Cairo. The Citadel of Qaitbay, on the foundations of the Pharos lighthouse, with walls to climb and fishing boats below. A seafood lunch by the harbour. In the afternoon, the Bibliotheca Alexandrina, opened in 2002, with a vast reading room and a real children’s section. Then, depending on energy, Montaza Gardens or the Roman theatre at Kom El Dikka, and back to Cairo by evening.

**The day:** Citadel of Qaitbay · Bibliotheca Alexandrina · Montaza Gardens or Kom El Dikka
**Overnight:** Barceló Cairo Pyramids or Pyramids Park Resort
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch
**Travel:** Private car, Cairo → Alexandria → Cairo, about three hours each way

### Day 4 · Cairo
**Coptic and Islamic Cairo, then the airport**

**Morning.** The Coptic quarter, with things for children to look for rather than a wall of dates: the Hanging Church above the Roman gate, and the Ben Ezra Synagogue, where the Cairo Geniza was found. Then Islamic Cairo: the Citadel of Saladin and the Mosque of Muhammad Ali, with its huge courtyard and the view over the city from the ramparts.

**Midday.** Lunch at a local restaurant, a last souvenir stop, then the transfer to the airport.

## Is this journey for you?

**A good fit if you:**
- are a family with children of about 4 to 12
- are bringing children to Egypt for the first time
- are several generations travelling together and want Cairo without exhaustion

**Worth knowing:**
- The Alexandria day means about six hours in the car, three each way.
- The Children’s Museum workshop is booked in advance through the museum, and depends on its confirmation for your date. Tell us the children’s ages when you book.
- For children who need additional support, the museum offers adapted workshops and multisensory visits. These usually need at least four weeks’ notice.
- The pyramid interior means crouching in a narrow passage.
- Day 4 suits an evening flight. With an earlier one, we shorten the morning.
- Dress modestly for the churches, the synagogue and the mosque: shoulders and knees covered. Shoes come off in the mosque.
