## Opening

Three days can’t cover Egypt, and this trip doesn’t try. It treats Cairo as a city in its own right: the pyramids of Giza on the day you land, Saqqara, where the pyramid began, on the second, and on the third the medieval city, from the Citadel on its ridge to the bazaar of Khan El Khalili.

---

## Why this route works

The pyramids come first, because they’re what most people come for. Saqqara the next morning goes further back, to the Step Pyramid that made Giza possible, so the second day explains the first. The third day leaves the pharaohs for the Cairo of the last thousand years and ends at the airport. Each day keeps to one part of the city, so the time goes to the sites rather than to traffic.

## Your three days

### Day 1 · Giza
**Arrival, and the pyramids**

You’re met at Cairo airport and driven to the Giza plateau. The pyramids of Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure, built around 2500 BC. You go inside the pyramid of Khafre or Menkaure, subject to site rules and availability on the day; a narrow passage leads down to the burial chamber. Then the panorama viewpoint, where the three pyramids line up in one frame, and the Great Sphinx and the Valley Temple below. The plateau is large enough for the crowds to thin out. Then to your hotel.

**The day:** Giza pyramids · inside Khafre or Menkaure · panorama viewpoint · Great Sphinx · Valley Temple
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Lunch
**Travel:** Meet and greet at Cairo airport · private air-conditioned transfers
**Pace:** Straight from the airport to the plateau

**Optional on this day:**

### Day 2 · Saqqara
**Where the pyramid was invented**

Saqqara was the burial ground of Memphis, Egypt’s first capital. At its centre stands the Step Pyramid of Djoser, built around 2650 BC and credited to the architect Imhotep: six stepped tiers, about 62 metres high, inside a walled funerary complex that became the model for what followed at Giza. The Imhotep Museum at the entrance holds finds from the necropolis, and a nobles’ tomb shows what Giza no longer can: carved scenes of daily life, offering chambers and false doors. The afternoon is yours.

**The day:** Saqqara · Step Pyramid of Djoser · Imhotep Museum · a nobles’ tomb
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch
**Travel:** Private air-conditioned transfers
**Pace:** A morning at the site, a free afternoon

**Optional on this day:**
- Inside the Step Pyramid: extra ticket

### Day 3 · Islamic Cairo → departure
**The Citadel, the alabaster mosque and Khan El Khalili**

The Citadel of Saladin, begun in the 12th century on a spur of the Mokattam hills, was the seat of Egypt’s rulers for some seven centuries. Inside its walls, the Mosque of Muhammad Ali, completed in the 1840s in an Ottoman style that looks to Istanbul; from the ramparts, on a clear day, you can see Giza. Then Khan El Khalili, trading since the 14th century, with jewellers, spice sellers, coppersmiths and coffee houses in the same lanes. The transfer to Cairo airport follows.

**The day:** Citadel of Saladin · Mosque of Muhammad Ali · Khan El Khalili
**Overnight:** —
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch
**Travel:** Private air-conditioned transfers · private transfer to the airport
**Pace:** A full day, ending at the airport
