## Opening

There’s no gentle way to put it: Cairo and back from Alexandria in one day means about 220 kilometres of desert road at each end, a start before breakfast and a return in the evening. What it buys is the Giza Plateau, Tutankhamun’s treasure at the Grand Egyptian Museum and the old bazaar of Khan El Khalili, with your own car, driver and Egyptologist, and no night away from your Alexandria hotel.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The road decides the shape. Leaving around 06:00 gets you through the desert road and onto the plateau in the morning, while it’s cooler and your energy is highest. Lunch comes next, then the museum’s air-conditioned halls fill the afternoon, and the bazaar comes last, as the light softens, before the long drive back west. Because the day is private, it can be trimmed: tell us how hard your party wants to push, and we’ll set the shape before you leave Alexandria.

## Your day

### Morning · The road south and the Giza Plateau

**Around 06:00 · Pickup from your hotel or the port**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you from your hotel, or meet you at the port entrance, in a private air-conditioned car. The desert road to Giza takes about two and a half to three hours.

**The three pyramids**
Khufu’s Great Pyramid, begun around 2560 BC: 146 metres high when finished, an estimated 2.3 million blocks averaging about 2.5 tonnes each. Khafre’s looks taller because it stands on higher ground, and near its top its original Tura limestone casing still shows how all three once shone. Menkaure’s is much smaller, and the contrast is its own history lesson.
*Sites:* Great Pyramid of Khufu · Pyramid of Khafre · Pyramid of Menkaure

**Inside a pyramid (included).** You go inside the pyramid of Khafre or Menkaure, subject to site rules and availability on the day. A narrow passage leads down to the burial chamber; it needs some crouching.

**The panorama viewpoint**
A rise in the desert where all three pyramids line up in one frame. This is the photography stop.

**The Great Sphinx and the Valley Temple**
Cut from the bedrock, 73 metres long and 20 high, most probably under Khafre, with his Valley Temple beside it. Who built the Sphinx, and when, is still debated, and your guide’s answer is more interesting than a confident one would be.
*Sites:* Great Sphinx · Valley Temple of Khafre

**Optional extras, if you’ve chosen them**
- Inside the Great Pyramid of Khufu: extra ticket, subject to availability and site rules on the day
- A camel ride at the panorama area: extra cost. We work with handlers we know, whose animals are in good condition, and the ride is entirely your choice.

### Afternoon · The Grand Egyptian Museum

**The Grand Egyptian Museum**
Fully open since 1 November 2025, at the north-east edge of the plateau. It’s vast, so the visit is focused, not comprehensive. The centrepiece is the Tutankhamun galleries: his complete burial equipment, reassembled for the first time, including the golden throne, the shrines, the canopic equipment and the chariots. After that, your guide follows your interests, from Old Kingdom statuary to the grand staircase lined with royal statues. The royal mummies aren’t here; they’re at the National Museum of Egyptian Civilisation.
*Sites:* Grand Egyptian Museum · Tutankhamun galleries

### Evening · Khan El Khalili and the road home

**Khan El Khalili**
Across the city in medieval Cairo. Founded in 1382 and trading ever since: covered lanes of spices, textiles, copper, silver, perfume and papyrus, of very mixed quality, and old coffee houses to sit in. Your guide gives you a short primer on bargaining before you go in. It’s crowded and sometimes pushy, and it’s the real thing.
*Sites:* Khan El Khalili

**The drive back to Alexandria**
Back at your hotel, or the port entrance, in the evening, traffic permitting.
