## Opening

This is not a Nile cruise. It is the other river journey in southern Egypt: three nights on Lake Nasser, the reservoir behind the Aswan High Dam, sailing north from Abu Simbel to Aswan. The temples of Lower Nubia were cut from their sites in the 1960s and rebuilt on higher ground before the water rose, and on this trip you reach them from the water.

---

## Why this route works

Starting at Abu Simbel puts Ramses II’s cliff temples first, then lets the lake settle into its own quiet. Sailing north, you meet the temples in the order a traveller coming down the river from Nubia would once have met them, and you finish at Aswan, where Philae, the Nubian Museum or a Nile cruise to Luxor can follow on.

## Your four days

### Day 1 · Abu Simbel
**The temples of Ramses II, and the ship turns north**

You’re met at Abu Simbel airport and taken to the ship for lunch. In the afternoon, the Great Temple, cut into the sandstone cliff for Ramses II in the 13th century BC, its four seated figures of the king about twenty metres high, and the smaller temple of his queen Nefertari. Around 22 February and 22 October the sun reaches the sanctuary. Between 1964 and 1968 both temples were cut into blocks and rebuilt 65 metres higher. Dinner as the ship sets off north.

**The day:** Great Temple of Abu Simbel · Temple of Nefertari
**Overnight:** Lake Nasser cruise ship
**Meals:** Lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Meet and assist at Abu Simbel airport · private transfer to the ship
**Pace:** An arrival, then the temples in the afternoon

### Day 2 · Qasr Ibrim and Amada
**A fortress from the deck, and the painted temples**

In the morning, Qasr Ibrim from the deck. It is the one site on the lake that was never moved: its hill stood high enough to stay above the water, and it now looks like an island. Landing is not allowed. In the afternoon, Amada, among the oldest temples in Nubia, begun in the 18th Dynasty, its reliefs still holding their colour. Beside it, Derr, a rock-cut temple of Ramses II, and the painted tomb of Pennut, an official of Lower Nubia under Ramses VI.

**The day:** Qasr Ibrim (from the deck) · Temple of Amada · Temple of Derr · Tomb of Pennut
**Overnight:** Lake Nasser cruise ship, sailing towards Wadi es-Sebua
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By cruise
**Pace:** A morning on deck, the visits in the afternoon

### Day 3 · Wadi es-Sebua, Dakka and Maharraqa
**The Valley of the Lions, and the sail to Aswan**

Wadi es-Sebua, the Valley of the Lions, named for the avenue of sphinxes that leads up to the temple of Ramses II. It was later used as a church, and in the sanctuary a painted figure of Saint Peter stands where the king’s cult image was. Nearby, rebuilt on the same site, Dakka, a Ptolemaic temple to Thoth finished under the Romans, and the small Roman kiosk of Maharraqa. The afternoon is on deck as the ship sails north towards Aswan.

**The day:** Temple of Wadi es-Sebua · Temple of Dakka · Maharraqa
**Overnight:** Lake Nasser cruise ship, near the High Dam
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By cruise
**Pace:** A morning ashore, an afternoon on the water

### Day 4 · Kalabsha → Aswan
**Three rescued temples on an island, then Aswan**

Breakfast on board, and you leave the ship near the High Dam. A short motorboat crossing takes you to New Kalabsha island, where three monuments were rebuilt: the Temple of Kalabsha, built in the Roman period for the Nubian god Mandulis; Beit el-Wali, a rock-cut temple of Ramses II whose coloured reliefs show his campaigns; and the Kiosk of Kertassi, a small Roman structure with two Hathor-headed columns. Then the transfer to Aswan.

**The day:** Temple of Kalabsha · Beit el-Wali · Kiosk of Kertassi
