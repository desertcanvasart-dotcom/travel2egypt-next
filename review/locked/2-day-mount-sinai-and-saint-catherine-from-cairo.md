## Opening

The Sinai is a different country from the Nile Valley: granite mountains, Bedouin towns and a sky that goes black at night. This private two-day trip drives you from Cairo to Saint Catherine, climbs Mount Sinai in the dark for sunrise, and comes down to the walls of the monastery.

---

## Why this route works

The pilgrim route to Mount Sinai has been walked, on and off, for sixteen centuries, and the sunrise is the reason to climb at night. Starting at 01:00 puts you on the summit for first light and back down at the monastery for its morning opening, so the mountain and the monastery fit into one morning before the drive home.

## Your two days

### Day 1 · Cairo → Saint Catherine
**The southern Sinai road**

Out of Cairo, under the Suez Canal through the Ahmed Hamdi tunnel and down the eastern shore of the Gulf of Suez: about 450 kilometres, most of the day. A short stop at the Springs of Moses, the brackish springs linked to the "twelve wells of water" of Exodus; then Hammam Pharaon, a coastal hot spring. Lunch at a restaurant on the road. Then inland into the mountains, through wadis of tamarisk and doum palm, climbing to Saint Catherine at about 1,600 metres. Check in, and an early dinner at your hotel: the climb starts in the night.

### Day 2 · Mount Sinai → Saint Catherine’s Monastery → Cairo
**The night climb, sunrise, and the monastery**

**01:00 · The climb.** With your Bedouin guide, up the Camel Path, the long, gradual route of about three hours, with camels available for most of it; the last stretch is steps. At 2,285 metres, the summit, where Christian, Jewish and Islamic traditions place the giving of the Ten Commandments, has a small chapel and a smaller mosque, and at first light the ranges turn from pink to red. Down by the Steps of Repentance, 3,750 stone steps, in the morning light.

**08:45 · Saint Catherine’s Monastery.** At the monastery for its morning opening. Built under Justinian in the mid-6th century, Saint Catherine’s has been held by its Greek Orthodox community ever since. Inside the walls: the Church of the Transfiguration and its 6th-century apse mosaic, the Chapel of the Burning Bush, and the bush itself in the courtyard.

**Breakfast and the road back.** Breakfast at your hotel, then the same road back to Cairo, with lunch at a restaurant on the way, arriving in the evening.
