## Opening

Aswan’s three great landmarks are all about stone and water: a granite obelisk that cracked in its quarry and was left where it lay, a dam that ended the Nile’s annual flood, and a temple taken apart and rebuilt on another island to escape the lake that dam created. This private day takes them in that order, with your own Egyptologist, and has you back by mid-afternoon with the river still to enjoy.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The Unfinished Obelisk comes first, while the quarry rock is still cool underfoot. The High Dam and Philae lie further south in the same direction, so the drives are short, and the dam explains why Philae stands where it does today. Lunch follows the temple, and the day ends by mid-afternoon on purpose: Aswan’s light and its river are at their best when you’re free to meet them. The three sites are fixed; the pace at each is yours.

## Your day

### Morning · The quarry and the dam

**Pickup from your hotel or ship**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you in a private air-conditioned car.

**The Unfinished Obelisk**
In the northern granite quarries, cut straight from the bedrock, probably under Hatshepsut, until a crack appeared and the work was abandoned. It lies where it was left: 42 metres long, an estimated 1,200 tonnes had it been finished, still joined to the rock, with the quarrymen’s marks all over it. It answers the question no finished obelisk can: how did they do it?
*Sites:* Unfinished Obelisk

**The High Dam**
Completed in 1970 and 3,830 metres long, built with Soviet help after Western funding was withdrawn. It created Lake Nasser, one of the largest artificial reservoirs on earth, ended the annual flood that had shaped Egyptian farming for millennia, and displaced the Nubians from their villages. It’s a lookout rather than a museum; your guide’s account of what it changed is the reason to stop.
*Sites:* Aswan High Dam

### Midday · Philae and lunch

**Philae Temple**
A short motorboat crossing brings the pylons up out of the water. This was the temple of Isis, where the old religion held out into the 6th century AD; the last known hieroglyphic inscription in Egypt was carved here in 394 AD. Flooded for part of each year after the first Aswan dam, then threatened for good by the High Dam, it was taken apart block by block between 1972 and 1980 and rebuilt on Agilkia Island, reshaped to match its old home. Walk through the pylons to the sanctuary, and out to Trajan’s Kiosk on the water’s edge.
*Sites:* Philae Temple · Trajan’s Kiosk

**Worth knowing:**
- Aswan’s dry heat is real. The quarry is open rock with little shade, so bring a hat and wear comfortable shoes.
- Getting into and out of the boat at Philae means a step down from the landing.
- The High Dam is a short stop with not much to look at. Its story is the point.
