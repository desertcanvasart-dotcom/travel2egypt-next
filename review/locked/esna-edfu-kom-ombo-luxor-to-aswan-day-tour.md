## Opening

Between Luxor and Aswan, the Ptolemies and the Romans after them built temples that still have their roofs, their painted ceilings and, in places, their original reds and blues. This private day connects three of them, Esna, Edfu and Kom Ombo, and ends in Aswan rather than looping back. It moves you as well as shows you: a long, one-way drive south with three serious stops.

---

## Why the day runs this way

You leave Luxor early and head south, stopping in the order the road gives you: Esna after about 55 kilometres, Edfu 60 kilometres on, then Kom Ombo, 45 kilometres short of Aswan, in the warm late light. The driving adds up to most of three hours. The three temples needn’t get equal time; most people find one holds them longest, so tell us which way you lean.

## Your day

### Morning · Esna

**Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you early from your hotel or cruise ship, in a private air-conditioned car.

**The Temple of Khnum, Esna**
Sunk some nine metres below the modern town, which has simply grown up around it. The hall is Roman, from the 1st and 2nd centuries AD, built for Khnum, the ram-headed god who shaped people on a potter’s wheel. Its 24 columns carry some of the latest hieroglyphs ever carved, as late as the 3rd century AD, under a ceiling that still keeps its colour.
*Sites:* Temple of Khnum, Esna

### Midday · Edfu and lunch

**The Temple of Horus, Edfu**
Begun under Ptolemy III in 237 BC and finished in 57 BC, then buried under sand and houses until Auguste Mariette uncovered it nearly intact in the 1860s. Pass the 36-metre pylon and its granite falcon into ever-darker halls: no site shows more clearly how an Egyptian temple worked. The walls tell the myth of Horus and his battles with Set. This is the stop that justifies the day.
*Sites:* Temple of Horus, Edfu

**Lunch at a local restaurant (included)**
Drinks aren’t included.

### Afternoon · Kom Ombo and Aswan

**The Temple of Kom Ombo**
A temple built double from the start: two sets of halls and sanctuaries side by side, for Horus the Elder and for Sobek the crocodile god, at a bend of the river where crocodiles once gathered. Next door, a small museum of mummified crocodiles makes the gods startlingly physical. The stones turn warm gold in the late afternoon.
*Sites:* Temple of Kom Ombo · Crocodile Museum

**Arrival in Aswan**
Your driver takes you to your hotel or ship in Aswan by early evening.
