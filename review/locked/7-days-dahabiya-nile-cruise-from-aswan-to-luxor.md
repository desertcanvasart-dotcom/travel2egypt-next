## Opening

A dahabiya is a traditional wooden sailing boat with two masts and a handful of cabins, the kind of boat nineteenth-century travellers took up and down this river. For six nights one carries you north from Aswan to Luxor, downstream with the current, mooring at islands and riverbanks the large cruise ships pass by, and ending with the tombs and temples of Thebes.

---

## Why this route works

North is the way the Nile runs, so the boat moves with the river rather than against it, under sail when the wind allows. You start at the First Cataract, where the landscape is still half Nubian, and the temples come in order along the way. Luxor’s two banks come last, on the final two days, with the boat as your base.

## Your seven days

> **The river sets the exact timing.** A dahabiya sails when the wind allows, and the passage through the lock at Esna runs to the lock’s own schedule. The sites are the same, but the hour of each visit can move, and your final itinerary confirms the order for your boat.

### Day 1 · Aswan
**Board the dahabiya: Philae and the High Dam**

You’re met on arrival in Aswan and taken to the dahabiya for lunch. In the afternoon, by motorboat to Philae, the Temple of Isis, where the old religion was still practised into the 6th century AD. It was taken apart and rebuilt on Agilkia Island when Lake Nasser rose, in the same UNESCO campaign that saved Abu Simbel. Then the Aswan High Dam, completed in 1970, which made the rescue necessary. The boat stays moored in Aswan for the night.

**The day:** Temple of Philae · Aswan High Dam
**Overnight:** Your dahabiya, Aswan
**Meals:** Lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Meet and assist at Aswan airport or station · private transfers · motorboat to Philae
**Pace:** An arrival, then two visits in the afternoon

### Day 2 · Aswan → Kom Ombo
**The first morning under sail, and the double temple**

Breakfast on deck as the boat leaves Aswan and the granite of the cataract drops astern. Kom Ombo stands on the riverbank at a wide bend: a Ptolemaic temple with its plan doubled along the axis, one half for Sobek the crocodile god and one for Horus the Elder, each with his own entrance and sanctuary. The museum beside it holds mummified crocodiles from Sobek’s cult. The boat moors off an island for the night, away from the cruise-ship berths.

**The day:** Sailing from Aswan · Temple of Kom Ombo · Crocodile Museum
**Overnight:** Your dahabiya, moored at an island
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By dahabiya
**Pace:** A morning on the water, a visit in the afternoon

### Day 3 · Edfu
**The Temple of Horus**

North to Edfu, and from the riverbank by horse-drawn carriage to the Temple of Horus, built by the Ptolemies between 237 and 57 BC on the site of an older shrine. Its roof, its sanctuary and its sequence of pylon, courts and halls are still in place, so the plan reads clearly from the gate to the innermost room. Back aboard, the afternoon is for the deck and the river.

**The day:** Temple of Horus at Edfu
**Overnight:** Your dahabiya
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By dahabiya · horse-drawn carriage
**Pace:** One visit, the rest of the day on the water

### Day 4 · El Kab
**The city of the vulture goddess**

El Kab was Nekheb, cult centre of the vulture goddess Nekhbet, one of the two patrons of the kingship of Upper and Lower Egypt. In the cliffs above the floodplain, rock-cut tombs of the early 18th Dynasty, around 1550 BC, carry painted scenes of daily life, still clear on the walls. The large ships can’t stop here, and few visitors come. The afternoon goes back to the river.

**The day:** El Kab · rock-cut tombs · Temple of Nekhbet
**Overnight:** Your dahabiya
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By dahabiya · on foot
**Pace:** A morning ashore, a slow afternoon

### Day 5 · On the Nile → Esna
**A river day, and the Esna lock**

A day for the river itself. The boat stops at a riverside village for a walk and a visit to a farm, among fields of sugarcane and clover that run down to the water and mud-brick houses on the levees. Later, the passage through the lock at Esna, a slow transit with the walls close on either side and other boats alongside. Then the last stretch towards Luxor.

**The day:** Riverside village and farm · Esna lock
**Overnight:** Your dahabiya
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By dahabiya · on foot
**Pace:** Slow

### Day 6 · Luxor · the West Bank
**The Valley of the Kings, Tutankhamun, Hatshepsut and the Colossi**

The Valley of the Kings, more than sixty tombs cut into the Theban hills between about 1550 and 1070 BC, their corridors painted with texts for the soul’s passage through the night. Your visit includes Tutankhamun’s tomb, small because the king died young, and famous for what Howard Carter found in it in 1922. Then Hatshepsut’s three terraces at Deir el-Bahari, and the Colossi of Memnon, two seated figures of Amenhotep III about 18 metres high.

**The day:** Valley of the Kings · Tomb of Tutankhamun · Temple of Hatshepsut · Colossi of Memnon
**Overnight:** Your dahabiya, Luxor
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers
**Pace:** An early start, to beat the heat

### Day 7 · Luxor · the East Bank
**Karnak and Luxor Temple, then departure**

Breakfast and farewell to the boat. Karnak, a precinct built and rebuilt over some 1,500 years, with the Great Hypostyle Hall of 134 columns, the tallest 21 metres high; it takes time, and the morning allows for it. Then along the Avenue of Sphinxes, reopened in November 2021, to Luxor Temple, the other end of the ceremonial axis of Thebes. Afterwards, the transfer to Luxor airport or station.

**The day:** Karnak · Great Hypostyle Hall · Avenue of Sphinxes · Luxor Temple
