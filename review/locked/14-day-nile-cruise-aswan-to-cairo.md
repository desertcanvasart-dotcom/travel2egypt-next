## Opening

The Nile the other way: a flight to Aswan, then twelve nights downriver with the current, through Kom Ombo, Edfu, Luxor, Dendera and Abydos to Amarna and Beni Hassan, ending in Cairo with the pyramids and the Egyptian Museum.

---

## Why this route works

Sailing north, you start where the river is at its most beautiful and finish in the capital. The ship is both transport and hotel, and the temples come in the order the river delivers them. The cruise sails on fixed dates, with a licensed Egyptologist for every excursion.

### Day 3 · Aswan
**The High Dam, Philae and the Nubian Museum**

The High Dam, completed in 1970, which created Lake Nasser and drowned ancient Nubia, and Philae, the temple of Isis, worshipped here into the 6th century AD, rebuilt block by block on Agilkia Island. In the afternoon, the Nubian Museum, for the culture the lake submerged. In the evening, a Nubian party on board.

**The day:** Aswan High Dam · Temple of Philae · Nubian Museum

### Day 6 · Luxor
**The West Bank and the East Bank**

The city of the dead. The Valley of the Kings, more than sixty royal tombs cut between about 1550 and 1070 BC, many still vivid; Tutankhamun’s the most famous. Then Hatshepsut’s temple against the cliffs of Deir el-Bahari. In the afternoon, karnak, a temple city expanded over fifteen centuries, its Great Hypostyle Hall a forest of 134 columns, the central ones 21 metres high, then Luxor Temple, where a Roman chapel, a church and the mosque of Abu el-Haggag have shared one sacred space. In the evening, a belly dancer show on board.

**The day:** Valley of the Kings · Temple of Hatshepsut · Karnak · Luxor Temple
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Luxor
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner

### Day 7 · Luxor → Naga Hammadi
**The Temple of Hathor**

The ship sails to Qena, and a short drive leads to dendera’s Temple of Hathor, built mainly in the 1st centuries BC and AD on much older foundations: its hypostyle hall, astronomical ceiling and roof chapels are all open, with a replica of the famous zodiac in place of the original, now in the Louvre. The ship then sails on to Naga Hammadi.

### Day 8 · Naga Hammadi → Sohag
**The Temple of Seti I**

The ship sails before dawn to El Balyana. Abydos, one of the most important sites in Egypt and one of the least visited on standard tours: the temple of Seti I, finished by his son Ramses II in the 13th century BC, for Osiris, its painted reliefs New Kingdom temple decoration at its best, and the Abydos King List, the Egyptians’ own record of their kings, from Menes to Seti I. The ship continues to Sohag, with a galabiya party on board.

**The day:** Temple of Seti I at Abydos · Abydos King List

### Day 10 · Tell el-Amarna → Minya
**Akhenaten’s city, Ashmunein and Tuna el-Gebel**

Tell el-Amarna, the capital Akhenaten built around 1346 BC for the sole worship of the Aten, abandoned within a generation and never rebuilt; its northern rock tombs, including the royal tomb of Akhenaten, the northern palace and the small temples of the Aten, record a brief break in three thousand years of religion. Then tuna el-Gebel, the necropolis of Hermopolis: catacombs of mummified ibises and baboons offered to Thoth, and the painted tomb of the priest Petosiris, blending Egyptian and Greek art with ease. Then Ashmunein, ancient Hermopolis, with the columns of a Christian basilica built from reused pharaonic blocks. The ship sails to Minya, with a folklore show on board.

**The day:** Tell el-Amarna rock tombs · Tuna el-Gebel · Tomb of Petosiris · Ashmunein
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Minya
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner

**Beni Hassan**

Beni Hassan, the Middle Kingdom tombs of regional governors, painted around 2000 BC with wrestling, hunting and everyday life: the tombs of Baqet III, Khety, Amenemhet and Khnumhotep II. The ship sails on to Beni Suef, where a quiz night closes the day.

**The day:** Beni Hassan rock tombs

The ship sails north to Cairo. Coptic Cairo, the older layers of the city: the Hanging Church above the gatehouse of a Roman fortress, the Coptic Museum, tracing Egypt’s Christian centuries, and the Ben Ezra Synagogue. In the evening, a tanoura show on board.
