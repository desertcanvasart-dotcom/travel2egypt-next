## Opening

Eight days in one hotel in Aswan, where the Nile narrows toward the first cataract: four guided days for Philae, the High Dam, the river, the west bank and a Nubian village, an open day in the middle, and Abu Simbel by air if you want it.

---

## Why this route works

Many trips give Aswan a day or two before a cruise carries them north. Aswan is quieter than Cairo and Luxor, and the light is softer, and it rewards the slower pace that a base gives. Staying in one hotel with no overnight transfers and no dawn flights to catch means you can take the sites as they come, and let the river and the city fill the time between them.

### Day 2 · Aswan
**Philae, the High Dam and the Unfinished Obelisk**

Philae, the temple of Isis, a sanctuary of the old Egyptian religion that stayed active into the 6th century AD, long after the rest of Egypt had converted. When Lake Nasser rose it was taken apart and rebuilt on Agilkia Island, a job that took until 1980, and you reach it by motorboat. The High Dam, completed in 1970, which ended the annual flood and created Lake Nasser. The Unfinished Obelisk, 42 metres long, still lying in its granite quarry, abandoned when a crack appeared in the stone. No finished obelisk shows as much about ancient stonecutting.

**The day:** Temple of Philae · Aswan High Dam · Unfinished Obelisk
**Overnight:** Aswan hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers · motorboat to Philae

### Day 3 · Aswan
**A felucca on the Nile**

Aswan’s geography makes sense on the water, in a felucca, the wooden sailing boat that has worked this stretch of the river for centuries. From the river you see the width of the Nile at the cataract, the low green of Elephantine Island, the desert escarpment on the west bank, and the Aga Khan Mausoleum on its hilltop, where the Aga Khan III was buried in 1957. The sail takes in Kitchener’s Island, a botanical garden planted in the 1890s with species from across Africa and Asia. The afternoon is free.

**The day:** felucca · Kitchener’s Island · Aga Khan Mausoleum
**Overnight:** Aswan hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers · felucca

### Day 5 · Aswan
**The west bank, Elephantine and the Nubian Museum**

The Tombs of the Nobles, cut into the hillside above the river, where Aswan’s governors and senior officials were buried from the Old and Middle Kingdoms, roughly 2300 to 1650 BC. The decoration is less showy than the royal tombs at Luxor, but the view back across the river is among the best in Aswan, and the site is rarely crowded. Then the ruins of the ancient settlement on Elephantine Island, excavated since the 19th century, with remains from every major period of Egyptian history. In the afternoon, the Nubian Museum, which follows Nubia from prehistory to the present, including the communities displaced by the High Dam.

**The day:** Tombs of the Nobles · Elephantine Island · Nubian Museum
**Overnight:** Aswan hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers · motorboat

### Day 6 · Aswan
**A Nubian village**

By motorboat to one of the Nubian villages on the west bank or on Elephantine Island, their houses painted in bright colours and their streets narrow, a community and a culture that go back long before the Arab settlement of Egypt. A guide who knows the village is the difference between a visit and a tour. The afternoon and evening are free: the Corniche at sunset, a drink on a terrace, the last feluccas crossing the pink water.

**The day:** Nubian village
**Overnight:** Aswan hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers · motorboat

---

### Day 7 · Aswan
**Abu Simbel by air, or a day in Aswan**

If you have not been to Abu Simbel, this is the day for it, by air, about 45 minutes south of Aswan, to the two temples Ramesses II had cut into the sandstone cliff at the Sudanese border in the 13th century BC. Four seated figures of the king, about twenty metres high, guard the entrance to the Great Temple, and the smaller temple was cut for his queen Nefertari. Between 1964 and 1968 both temples were cut into blocks and raised 65 metres above their original place, to save them from Lake Nasser. If you would rather stay, the day is yours in Aswan.

**Overnight:** Aswan hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · dinner
**Pace:** As much or as little as you like

**Optional on this day:**
