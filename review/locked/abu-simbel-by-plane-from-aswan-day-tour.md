## Opening

Arriving at Abu Simbel by air has its own quality: the valley narrows and disappears below you, the land turns the colour of pale bone, and then the four seated colossi of Ramses II come into view above the lake that nearly swallowed them. This private morning flies you there and back from Aswan, with an Egyptologist for both temples, and you’re back before the heat takes hold. It’s the gentlest way to reach Ramses, especially with a family.

## Why the day runs this way

The road is about 280 kilometres of open desert, three hours or more each way. The flight takes forty minutes, so the case for flying is simple. The early start is set by the flight timetable, not by us, and so is lunch: with an early flight you’re back in Aswan in time for lunch there; with a later one, lunch is in Abu Simbel before the flight home. About two hours at the temples, then the flight back, and the afternoon is free.

## Your day

### Morning · Abu Simbel

**Pickup and the flight south**
A private transfer to Aswan airport for the short flight. In Abu Simbel, your guide meets you at the airport.

**The Great Temple of Ramses II**
Carved into the sandstone cliff around 1264 BC to project Egyptian power deep into Nubia. Four seated figures of Ramses, each about 20 metres tall, front a temple that runs 63 metres into the rock: a hall of eight pillars bearing the king’s face as Osiris, and the inner chambers where the cult statues remain. Around 22 February and 22 October, the sun reaches the sanctuary and lights every figure but Ptah, god of the underworld.
*Sites:* Great Temple of Ramses II

**The Temple of Hathor and Nefertari**
A short walk north: smaller and quieter, with colossal figures of Nefertari standing as tall as those of Ramses beside her, a rare honour. From the terrace between the temples, the view opens over Lake Nasser.
*Sites:* Temple of Hathor and Nefertari

**The rescue**
Between 1964 and 1968, a UNESCO campaign cut both temples into blocks of up to 30 tonnes and rebuilt them 65 metres higher and 200 metres back, keeping their orientation. To any reasonable eye, they sit exactly as they always did.
