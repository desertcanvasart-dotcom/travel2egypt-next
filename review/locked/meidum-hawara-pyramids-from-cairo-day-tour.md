## Opening

Egypt's pyramids don't end at Giza. South of Cairo lie two of the least-visited and most candid: Meidum, which collapsed, and Hawara, a mud-brick mound that once anchored the complex Herodotus called the Labyrinth. This private day is given entirely to them, with a quiet stop at a small village monastery in between. Neither pyramid is glamorous, and that's the point: they show the tradition from the inside, experiments, failures and all.

---

## Why the day runs this way

You leave early, before Cairo's traffic assembles, and drive south. Meidum comes first, in the morning light; then lunch, a short stop at a village monastery, and Hawara in the afternoon. With the day given only to the two pyramids, there's time to walk each site properly rather than glance at it from the car. It's a physically moderate day, but a long one on the road.

## Your day

### Morning · Meidum

**Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you early in a private air-conditioned car for the drive south.

**The Meidum Pyramid**
Begun under Huni at the end of the Third Dynasty, around 2600 BC, and finished or altered by Snefru. What stands is a three-stepped tower in a skirt of fallen casing: the result of an ancient structural failure. If you like, descend the low, sloping corridor inside.
*Sites:* Meidum Pyramid

**The mastabas of Meidum**
Around the pyramid lie the mastaba tombs of the court, including that of Rahotep and Nofret. The tombs aren't open to visitors. Their treasures are in Cairo: the painted statues of Rahotep and Nofret and the Meidum Geese are in the Egyptian Museum on Tahrir Square, and your guide explains where each piece came from.

### Midday · Lunch and a village monastery

**Lunch at a local restaurant (included)**
Drinks aren't included.

**A village monastery**
A short stop at one of the small monasteries in the villages along the way. They aren't on the tourist map, but they are places of real devotion, and we've known them for years. The visit is brief and quiet.

### Afternoon · Hawara

**The Hawara Pyramid**
Built by Amenemhat III of the Twelfth Dynasty, around 1850 BC, in mud brick, which is why it now looks more like a hill than a monument. Its mortuary temple was so vast that Herodotus called it the Labyrinth and rated it above the pyramids. The Labyrinth is gone, quarried away over the centuries; your guide explains what the Greek and Roman accounts suggest about its scale.
*Sites:* Hawara Pyramid · the Labyrinth

**The drive back to Cairo**
Back at your hotel in the early evening, depending on traffic.

## Is this day for you?

**A good fit if you:**
- have done Cairo's main sites and want the pyramid story outside the Giza crowds
- want to see how pyramid-building was learned, by trial and error

**Worth knowing:**
- Several hours in the car.
- The Meidum corridor is steep and low: not for anyone with claustrophobia or limited mobility. It's optional.
- The mastaba tombs at Meidum are closed; their statues and paintings are in the Egyptian Museum in Cairo.
