## Opening

The pyramid wasn’t invented at Giza. It was worked out in the desert south of Cairo, over several generations, and this shared day follows that working-out in order: Memphis, the first capital; Saqqara, where Djoser’s Step Pyramid shows Egyptians first building big in stone; and Dahshur, where Snefru’s two pyramids found the shape Khufu later took to Giza. Three sites within about thirty kilometres of each other, with a small group and one Egyptologist.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The order is the story. Memphis sets the frame, Saqqara shows the first stone monument, and Dahshur shows the pyramid as we know it, so by mid-afternoon the logic behind Giza is clear. The sites are open, largely undeveloped and short on shade. Lunch falls between Saqqara and Dahshur, and you’re back at your hotel in the late afternoon.

## Your day

### Morning · Memphis and Saqqara

**08:00 · Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you and the rest of the group in an air-conditioned vehicle. It’s about an hour south to Memphis.

**Memphis**
Founded, by tradition, by Menes around 3100 BC as the capital of a newly unified Egypt. The ancient city lies mostly under the village of Mit Rahina; what you visit is an open-air museum. Its centrepiece is a colossal limestone Ramses II, lying in its own hall, the scale clear only when you walk its length. In the courtyard sits an alabaster sphinx from the New Kingdom.
*Sites:* Memphis open-air museum

**The Step Pyramid of Djoser**
Built around 2650 BC by the architect Imhotep for the Third Dynasty king Djoser: six stepped platforms of cut stone, the moment Egyptian builders turned from mud-brick to stone and began to build upwards. Around it lies a deliberately complex funerary precinct, once enclosed by a wall more than a kilometre long. About two hours at Saqqara.
*Sites:* Saqqara · Step Pyramid of Djoser

**The Imhotep Museum**
The site museum at the entrance to Saqqara, named after Djoser’s architect. Included in your visit.
*Sites:* Imhotep Museum

**A nobles’ tomb**
One of the Old Kingdom nobles’ tombs, painted with daily life, hunting and farming, chosen by your guide from those open on the day: which tombs are open changes as conservation work rotates.
*Sites:* Saqqara nobles’ tombs

### Midday · Lunch

**Lunch at a local restaurant (included)**
A barbecue lunch at a local restaurant on the way to Dahshur.

### Afternoon · Dahshur

**The Bent Pyramid**
Built by Snefru, Khufu’s father, around 2600 BC. It rises at about 54 degrees, then changes to 43 degrees partway up. The leading theory: the builders saw signs of stress and lowered the angle to cut the weight.

**The Red Pyramid**
Snefru’s second attempt, built at the gentler angle from the base, and it worked: the first true smooth-sided pyramid. On a quiet afternoon there are few other visitors here.
*Sites:* Dahshur · Bent Pyramid · Red Pyramid

**Optional extras, if you’ve chosen them**
- Inside the Step Pyramid of Djoser: extra ticket
- Inside the Red Pyramid: extra ticket. The passage is long, low and narrow, and the chambers are undecorated.
