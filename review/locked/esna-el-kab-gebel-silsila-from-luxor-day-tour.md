## Opening

South of Luxor lie three sites most visitors never reach: a Roman-era temple at Esna, the tombs of the soldiers who helped found Egypt's empire at El Kab, and the sandstone cliffs of Gebel Silsila, where the stone for Karnak and Luxor was cut. There are no crowds here and no gift shops. It's a private day that turns the Nile Valley from a string of famous monuments into one long, layered story.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The day heads south along the river. Esna comes first, about 54 kilometres from Luxor, then El Kab on the east bank, and finally Gebel Silsila, the furthest point, where the Nile narrows between the cliffs. Each stop explains the next: the temple, the people who served its kings, and the quarry that supplied the stone. It means a long day on the road, but the car and guide are yours alone, so you set the pace at each site.

## Your day

### Morning · Esna

**Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you in a private air-conditioned car for the drive south along the west bank.

**The Temple of Khnum, Esna**
The temple sits in a pit some nine metres below the busy market town: two thousand years of street level piled on top of it. Walk down into the 1st century AD, into a hall whose columns are covered floor to ceiling in hieroglyphs, among the last great religious texts in the script. Roman emperors appear in the reliefs as pharaohs, honouring the ram-headed Khnum, who shaped humans on a potter's wheel.
*Sites:* Temple of Khnum, Esna

### Midday · El Kab and lunch

**The tombs of El Kab**
Ancient Nekheb, city of the vulture goddess Nekhbet, on the east bank. In his tomb, the officer Ahmose, son of Ibana, tells in the first person how he served three kings, fought to drive out the Hyksos and campaigned in Nubia and Syria: history carved in his own words. Nearby, the tombs of Paheri and Renni are painted with farming and home life.
*Sites:* El Kab · Tomb of Ahmose son of Ibana · Tomb of Paheri

**Lunch at a local restaurant (included)**
Drinks aren't included.

### Afternoon · Gebel Silsila

**Gebel Silsila**
Where the Nile squeezes between sandstone cliffs and successive dynasties quarried stone for Karnak, Luxor and the great Theban monuments. A small rock-cut chapel of Horemheb and stelae line the cliff. After a day of temples and tombs, this is where you see where the stone came from.
*Sites:* Gebel Silsila · Speos of Horemheb

**The drive back to Luxor**
Back at your hotel in the early evening.
