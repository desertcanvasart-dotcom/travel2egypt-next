## Why the day runs this way

There's no fixed route; that's the point. The one rule is geography: you visit each bank once. Start on the East Bank or the West Bank, whichever suits you and wherever you're staying, cross the river once, and at the end of the day we take you back to your hotel or ship, on whichever side it is. Many people like the West Bank early, while the tombs are cool and before the crowds, and the East Bank later, when the light is kind to Luxor Temple; the choice is yours. Two or three sites done properly beat five done in a rush, and your guide helps you sequence them around the heat, your interests and the season.

### Morning · Pickup and the first stops

### Choose your stops

**The Valley of the Kings**
The burial ground of the New Kingdom pharaohs, about 1550–1070 BC, with more than sixty tombs in the cliffs. A standard ticket opens three of them; Tutankhamun's, small and famous for what was found in it, is a separate ticket.
*Sites:* Valley of the Kings

**The Temple of Hatshepsut**
Three colonnaded terraces against the cliff at Deir el-Bahari, built by her architect Senenmut for the woman who ruled as pharaoh for some twenty years. Look for the painted voyage to Punt.
*Sites:* Temple of Hatshepsut

**The Colossi of Memnon**
Two seated figures of Amenhotep III, about 18 metres tall, at the edge of the farmland: battered, and entirely impressive.
*Sites:* Colossi of Memnon

**Karnak**
Less a building than a city of temples, built over some 1,500 years. Its Hypostyle Hall, 134 columns up to 21 metres high, is one of the most overwhelming spaces of the ancient world; a guide turns its forest of stone into something you can read.
*Sites:* Karnak · Great Hypostyle Hall

**Luxor Temple**
More intimate, and best read as one composition, built mainly under Amenhotep III and Ramses II, with a mosque still inside and traces of a Roman camp. Lovely at dusk, when the stone warms and the crowds thin.
*Sites:* Luxor Temple

**Back to your hotel or ship**
At the end of your eight hours.
