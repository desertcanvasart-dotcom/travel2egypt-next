## Opening

This isn’t a show staged for tourists. It’s a performance tradition, presented for decades in the same Mamluk caravanserai off Al-Muizz Street, that visitors are welcome to watch. Dancers in layered, brightly coloured skirts spin for longer than seems possible, to drums, pipes and chanting, inside a courtyard built in 1505. It’s one evening in Cairo that’s genuinely different from a Nile dinner cruise.

The show runs on Monday, Wednesday and Saturday evenings, so the evening is planned around those nights. ## Your day

### Evening · Islamic Cairo

**Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and assistant collect you in a private air-conditioned car and drop you near the Khan El Khalili district.

**The walk to the wikala**
Your assistant walks with you: a few minutes on foot along Al-Muizz Street, past the mausoleum and mosque that Sultan al-Ghouri also built. Look up at the façades as you go.

**Wikala El Ghoury**
Built in 1505 by Sultan Qansuh al-Ghouri as a merchants’ inn: goods stored on the ground floor, rooms above the arcaded courtyard. Restored in the late 20th century, it’s now a performance and arts venue, and the Mamluk stonework and arches are the backdrop no theatre could copy.
*Sites:* Wikala El Ghoury · Al-Muizz Street

**The Tanoura show**
About ninety minutes. The spinning is the centrepiece, and it isn’t decoration: in the Sufi tradition, whirling is a form of active meditation that the audience witnesses rather than a show put on for it. Around it, the troupe plays tabla and mizmar and leads call-and-response chanting.

**Back to your hotel**
Your assistant meets you after the show and walks you back to the car.
