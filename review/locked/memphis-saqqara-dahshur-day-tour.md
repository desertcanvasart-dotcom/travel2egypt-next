## Opening

South of Cairo there’s a stretch of desert where you can walk the early history of the pyramid in a single day: from the Step Pyramid at Saqqara, where Egyptians first built big in stone, to the two pyramids at Dahshur that worked out the shape Khufu later perfected at Giza. Memphis, Saqqara and Dahshur sit within about thirty kilometres of each other. By late afternoon, the logic connecting them is clear in a way no book quite manages.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The day follows the story in order. Memphis, the first capital, sets the frame; Saqqara shows the first stone monument; Dahshur shows the pyramid as we know it. The sites are old, open and largely undeveloped, with no shade to speak of and, at Dahshur especially, few other visitors. Because the tour is private, your Egyptologist can tilt the hours towards Saqqara’s tombs or Dahshur’s experiments as your interest shows itself.

## Your day

### Morning · Memphis and Saqqara

**Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you in a private air-conditioned car. The city gives way to desert faster than you’d expect.

**Memphis**
Founded, by tradition, by Menes around 3100 BC as the capital of a newly unified Egypt. The ancient city lies mostly under the village of Mit Rahina; what you visit is an open-air museum. Its centrepiece is a colossal limestone Ramses II, lying in its own hall, the scale clear only when you walk its length. In the courtyard, an alabaster sphinx from the New Kingdom sits worn smooth.
*Sites:* Memphis open-air museum

**The Step Pyramid of Djoser**
Built around 2650 BC by the architect Imhotep for the Third Dynasty king Djoser: six stepped platforms of cut stone, the moment Egyptian builders turned from mud-brick to stone and began to build upwards. Up close, it isn’t a rough prototype but a deliberately complex funerary complex, once enclosed by a wall more than a kilometre long. Going inside the pyramid is an extra ticket.
*Sites:* Saqqara · Step Pyramid of Djoser

**The Imhotep Museum**
The site museum at the entrance to Saqqara, named after Djoser’s architect. Included in your visit.
*Sites:* Imhotep Museum

**A nobles’ tomb**
One of the Old Kingdom nobles’ tombs, painted with daily life, hunting and farming, in remarkable condition, chosen with your guide from those open on the day: which tombs are open changes as conservation work rotates.
*Sites:* Saqqara nobles’ tombs

### Afternoon · Dahshur

**The Bent Pyramid**
Built by Snefru, Khufu’s father, around 2600 BC. It rises at about 54 degrees, then changes to 43 degrees halfway up. The leading theory: the builders saw signs of stress and lowered the angle to cut the weight.

**The Red Pyramid**
Snefru’s second attempt, built at the gentler angle from the base, and it worked: 104 metres high, the first true smooth-sided pyramid. Going inside, down the long, low passage to the corbelled chambers, is an extra ticket; there’s no decoration, so the engineering is the whole point. On a quiet day, you may have it nearly to yourself.
*Sites:* Dahshur · Bent Pyramid · Red Pyramid
