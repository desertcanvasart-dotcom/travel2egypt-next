## Opening

Abu Simbel is a long way south of Luxor, and as a day trip it means more hours in the car than at the temples. This private route splits the journey. Drive to Aswan and see its monuments on day one, sleep there, and reach Abu Simbel the next morning rested, with time to take the temples in.

---

## Why this route works

The night in Aswan is the whole point. It cuts the Abu Simbel drive to about three hours, gives Aswan and the dam their own unhurried day, and ends the trip in Aswan, where most travellers continue south by air or north by train or cruise.

## Your two days

### Day 1 · Luxor → Aswan
**Philae, the Unfinished Obelisk and the High Dam**

Your driver and guide collect you from your hotel in Luxor. The drive south reaches Aswan in time for its three signature stops, in whatever order the light and the crowds suggest. Philae, the temple of Isis, reached by motor launch: it was moved to Agilkia Island between 1972 and 1980, and its reliefs of Isis nursing Horus are among the clearest in Egypt. The Unfinished Obelisk, which would have stood 42 metres had it ever been raised, abandoned where the granite cracked. And the High Dam, completed in 1970, which created Lake Nasser and displaced some 100,000 Nubians; your guide explains what was lost as well as gained. Lunch in Aswan, then an unhurried afternoon, and dinner at your hotel.

**The day:** Temple of Philae · Unfinished Obelisk · Aswan High Dam

**Meals:** Lunch · dinner

**Pace:** Sightseeing in the morning and early afternoon, then time to yourself

### Day 2 · Aswan → Abu Simbel → Aswan
**The temples of Ramses II**

Breakfast at your hotel, and departure around 06:00 for the three-hour drive south. The Great Temple, carved for Ramses II in the 13th century BC, is fronted by four seated figures over 20 metres tall, with the Battle of Kadesh across its great hall. The smaller temple honours Nefertari and Hathor, the queen shown at the same scale as the king. The whole mountain around them is artificial: between 1964 and 1968 the temples were cut into blocks, lifted 65 metres and rebuilt inside a hollow dome, and the sun still lights the sanctuary around 22 February and 22 October. Lunch in Abu Simbel, then the drive back to Aswan, where the trip ends at your hotel, the railway station or the airport.
