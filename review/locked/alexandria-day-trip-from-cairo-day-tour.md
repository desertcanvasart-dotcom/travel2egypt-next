## Opening

Alexandria isn’t a beach resort or a footnote to ancient Egypt. It was one of the ancient world’s great intellectual capitals, home of the Library and the Lighthouse, and it still wears its history in layers. In one long private day from Cairo, you see four of its most substantial sites, chosen to show the city’s range: an Egyptian-Greek-Roman catacomb, a lone Roman column, a museum of the city’s whole story and a marble theatre. It’s a lot of road, and we’ll say so plainly.

---

## Why the day runs this way

Alexandria is about 220 kilometres from Cairo, around three hours each way: a six-hour round trip around a four-site day. So you leave early, before Cairo’s traffic takes hold, and arrive by mid-morning. The sites run from underground to open air, from the oldest to the most civic, and a private car means the pace follows your interest at each one, not a group’s.

## Your day

### Morning · The road north and the catacombs

**Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you early in a private air-conditioned car, across the edge of the Delta to the Mediterranean.

**The Catacombs of Kom El Shuqafa**
Cut into the rock in the late 1st or early 2nd century AD, and found again in 1900 when a donkey fell through a shaft. Three levels down, past a rotunda and a hall where mourners held funeral banquets, to the main tomb: Anubis in a Roman soldier’s pose, Egyptian gods in Greek dress. At least forty-five minutes.
*Sites:* Catacombs of Kom El Shuqafa

**Pompey’s Pillar**
Nothing to do with Pompey: a single shaft of red Aswan granite, raised in 297 AD for the emperor Diocletian, at the heart of the Serapeum, the great temple of Serapis destroyed in 391 AD. The column survived, alone above the city, with two sphinx fragments at its foot. Twenty or thirty minutes.
*Sites:* Pompey’s Pillar · Serapeum

### Afternoon · The museum and the amphitheatre

**The National Museum of Alexandria**
A restored Italianate palace, its collection running from Pharaonic through Greek, Roman, Coptic and Islamic times. Look for the statues, anchors and architectural pieces raised from the sunken royal quarter of the Eastern Harbour. Thorough without being exhausting.
*Sites:* Alexandria National Museum

**The Roman Amphitheatre at Kom El Dikka**
Built in Roman times and used until the 7th century: thirteen curved rows of white marble around a small stage, made for lectures, music and readings rather than gladiators. Around it, excavated streets, baths and lecture halls show the city’s everyday civic life.
*Sites:* Kom El Dikka Roman Amphitheatre

Back at your hotel in the early evening, traffic permitting.

## Is this day for you?

**A good fit if you:**
- are based in Cairo with limited time, and want one serious encounter with Greco-Roman Egypt

**Worth knowing:**
- About six of the twelve hours are on the road.
- It isn’t a leisurely day, and there’s no time for the Corniche or the beach.
- Alexandria’s greatest monuments, the Library, the Lighthouse and Alexander’s tomb, are gone. What you see is the city that grew up around them.
