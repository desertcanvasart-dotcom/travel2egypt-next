## Opening

Most tours of Egypt fly between its famous places. This one sails between them: fifteen days up the whole inhabited length of the Nile, from the pyramids to the first cataract, stopping at Memphis, Beni Hassan, Tell el-Amarna and Abydos, the sites that fly-in itineraries rarely reach.

---

## Why this route works

The ship is both transport and hotel, moving while you sleep and arriving each morning somewhere new, so by the time you reach Luxor you haven’t just ticked off the famous temples: you’ve followed the river through the towns and landscapes that kept Egypt running while the pharaohs built in stone. The cruise sails on fixed dates, with a licensed Egyptologist for every excursion.

**Giza and the Egyptian Museum**

The Giza plateau: the pyramids of Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure and the Great Sphinx, built around 2500 BC. In the afternoon, the Egyptian Museum on Tahrir Square, opened in 1902, from the Narmer Palette to the gold of Tanis.

### Day 3 · Cairo
**Memphis, Saqqara and Coptic Cairo**

Memphis, first capital of a united Egypt, with the fallen colossus of Ramses II and an alabaster sphinx, and Saqqara, where the Step Pyramid of Djoser, built by Imhotep around 2650 BC, is where the pyramid was invented. Coptic Cairo, the older layers of the city: the Hanging Church above the gatehouse of a Roman fortress, the Coptic Museum, tracing Egypt’s Christian centuries, and the Ben Ezra Synagogue. In the evening, a tanoura show on board.

**The day:** Memphis · Saqqara · Step Pyramid of Djoser · Hanging Church · Coptic Museum · Ben Ezra Synagogue
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Cairo
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner

### Day 6 · Minya
**Tuna el-Gebel, Ashmunein and Beni Hassan**

Tuna el-Gebel, the necropolis of Hermopolis: catacombs of mummified ibises and baboons offered to Thoth, and the painted tomb of the priest Petosiris, blending Egyptian and Greek art with ease. Then Ashmunein, ancient Hermopolis, with the columns of a Christian basilica built from reused pharaonic blocks. In the afternoon, beni Hassan, the Middle Kingdom tombs of regional governors, painted around 2000 BC with wrestling, hunting and everyday life: the tombs of Baqet III, Khety, Amenemhet and Khnumhotep II.

**The day:** Tuna el-Gebel · Tomb of Petosiris · Ashmunein · Beni Hassan rock tombs
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Minya
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner

### Day 7 · Minya → Asyut
**Akhenaten’s city**

One of the most intellectually dense days. Tell el-Amarna, the capital Akhenaten built around 1346 BC for the sole worship of the Aten, abandoned within a generation and never rebuilt; its northern rock tombs, including the royal tomb of Akhenaten, the northern palace and the small temples of the Aten, record a brief break in three thousand years of religion. In the afternoon the ship sails to Asyut, passing the Monastery of Prince Tawadros, seen from the river with your guide’s commentary on its place in Coptic Egypt. A galabiya party on board in the evening.

**The day:** Tell el-Amarna rock tombs · Prince Tawadros Monastery from the river
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Asyut
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers · by cruise

### Day 9 · Sohag → Naga Hammadi
**The Temple of Seti I**

The ship sails before dawn to El Balyana. Abydos, one of the most important sites in Egypt and one of the least visited on standard tours: the temple of Seti I, finished by his son Ramses II in the 13th century BC, for Osiris, its painted reliefs New Kingdom temple decoration at its best, and the Abydos King List, the Egyptians’ own record of their kings, from Menes to Seti I. In the afternoon the ship continues to Naga Hammadi.

**The day:** Temple of Seti I at Abydos · Abydos King List
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Naga Hammadi

### Day 10 · Naga Hammadi → Luxor
**The Temple of Hathor**

The ship sails to Qena, and a short drive leads to dendera’s Temple of Hathor, built mainly in the 1st centuries BC and AD on much older foundations: its hypostyle hall, astronomical ceiling and roof chapels are all open, with a replica of the famous zodiac in place of the original, now in the Louvre. The ship sails on to Luxor in the afternoon.

**The day:** Temple of Hathor at Dendera
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Luxor

### Day 11 · Luxor · the West Bank
**The Valley of the Kings and Hatshepsut**

The city of the dead. The Valley of the Kings, more than sixty royal tombs cut between about 1550 and 1070 BC, many still vivid; Tutankhamun’s the most famous. Then Hatshepsut’s temple against the cliffs of Deir el-Bahari. In the evening, a belly dancer show on board.

**The day:** Valley of the Kings · Temple of Hatshepsut
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Luxor
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner

### Day 12 · Luxor → Edfu
**Karnak, Luxor Temple, and the ship sails**

Karnak, a temple city expanded over fifteen centuries, its Great Hypostyle Hall a forest of 134 columns, the central ones 21 metres high, then Luxor Temple, where a Roman chapel, a church and the mosque of Abu el-Haggag have shared one sacred space. After lunch the ship sails south through the Esna lock towards Edfu.

**The day:** Karnak · Great Hypostyle Hall · Luxor Temple

### Day 13 · Edfu → Aswan
**The Ptolemaic temples**

The Temple of Horus at Edfu, built between 237 and 57 BC, its walls, roof and inner sanctuary intact. The ship sails to Kom Ombo for kom Ombo, the double temple of Sobek and Horus the Elder, its plan doubled in exact mirror image. It moors at Aswan in the evening.

**The day:** Temple of Horus at Edfu · Temple of Kom Ombo
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Aswan
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By cruise

### Day 14 · Aswan
**The High Dam, Philae and the Nubian Museum**

The High Dam, completed in 1970, which created Lake Nasser and drowned ancient Nubia, and Philae, the temple of Isis, worshipped here into the 6th century AD, rebuilt block by block on Agilkia Island. In the afternoon, the Nubian Museum, for the culture the lake submerged. In the evening, a Nubian party on board.

**The day:** Aswan High Dam · Temple of Philae · Nubian Museum
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Aswan
