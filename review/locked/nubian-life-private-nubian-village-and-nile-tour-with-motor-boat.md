## Opening

The Nubian villages around Aswan aren’t a heritage display. They’re where people live, speak their own language and paint their houses in colours you won’t see anywhere else in Egypt. This is a short private trip by motorboat to Gharb Soheil on the west bank: a walk through the village, tea in a family’s home, and back, in about two hours.

---

## Why the day runs this way

Two hours is the honest length. This is a visit to a living village, not a monument, and a longer one would mean either shop time or getting in the way of village life. Going by boat gives you the river too: the granite islands of the cataract and the city behind you. Because it’s short, it fits where you need it: a free afternoon, a morning before your cruise boards, or between temple days.

## Your day

### Afternoon · Across the river to the village

**The Corniche**
You meet your boatman and assistant at the landing on the Corniche. The boat is yours alone.

**Upstream by motorboat**
South along the river, past Elephantine and the dark granite islands where the First Cataract begins, with the desert rising on the west bank.

**Gharb Soheil**
The assistant walks you up from the landing into the village: domed and vaulted houses painted in strong colours, often with crocodiles and protective symbols around the doorways. The people here are Nubian, with a language of their own, not a dialect of Arabic. Further south, the old Nubian homeland disappeared under Lake Nasser in the 1960s and its people were resettled; villages like this one keep the language, the building and the colour of that world. Your assistant explains what you’re seeing and translates.
*Sites:* Gharb Soheil

**Tea in a Nubian home**
A stop at a family’s house for tea, served the traditional way.

**Back to Aswan**
The same way by motorboat, with the river opening north towards the city.

## Is this day for you?

**A good fit if you:**
- have two free hours in Aswan and want to see how Nubians live today
- are travelling with children: it’s short, and the boat is part of the fun

**Worth knowing:**
- It’s a village where people live. Ask before you photograph anyone.
- Families in the village may offer handicrafts; there’s no obligation to buy.
- Your assistant speaks English and isn’t an Egyptologist. For Nubia’s history in depth, add the Nubian Museum.
