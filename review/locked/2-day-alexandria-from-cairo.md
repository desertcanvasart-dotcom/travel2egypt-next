## Opening

Alexandria is the answer for travellers who suspect Egypt is more than pyramids and pharaohs. Done in a day from Cairo, it’s a lot of road for a few stops. With a night by the sea, it opens up: the Roman-era catacombs and the Greco-Roman Museum on the first day, Qaitbey Citadel lit up for its Sound and Light Show in the evening, and the Bibliotheca, the Roman theatre and the gardens of El Montaza on the second.

---

## Why this route works

Alexandria is about 220 kilometres from Cairo, around three hours each way. Splitting the visit over two days means the road takes a morning and an afternoon rather than the middle of a single day, and the city gets the time it needs. Day 1 goes from underground to the museum, with a rest at the hotel before the evening at Qaitbey. Day 2 moves from the modern library to the ancient theatre, then to the sea air of El Montaza before the drive home.

## Your two days

### Day 1 · Cairo → Alexandria
**The catacombs, Pompey’s Pillar, the Greco-Roman Museum and Qaitbey by night**

**Morning.** An early pickup from your hotel in Cairo, and about three hours north across the edge of the Delta to the Mediterranean. Then the Catacombs of Kom El Shuqafa, found in 1900 when a donkey fell through a collapsed passage, and used from about the late 1st to the 4th century AD. The tombs follow Egyptian layouts in Greek and Roman dress: in the main chamber, Anubis appears in Roman armour. Three levels go down a spiral stair; the lowest is often flooded and closed. Then Pompey’s Pillar, which has nothing to do with Pompey: a single shaft of red Aswan granite, about 27 metres tall, raised in 297 AD for the emperor Diocletian on the site of the Serapeum, the great temple destroyed in 391 AD.

**Midday.** Lunch at a local restaurant.

**Afternoon.** The Greco-Roman Museum, reopened in 2023 after a long restoration, with a rich collection from about 300 BC to the 7th century AD: portrait sculpture, terracottas, papyri, coins and carved sarcophagi. Then check in at your hotel and rest.

**Evening.** The Sound and Light Show at Qaitbey Citadel, the Mamluk fort built in 1477 at the mouth of the Eastern Harbour, on the site of the ancient lighthouse and partly from its stone.

**The day:** Catacombs of Kom El Shuqafa · Pompey’s Pillar · Serapeum · Greco-Roman Museum · Sound and Light Show at Qaitbey Citadel
**Overnight:** Paradise Inn Le Metropole Hotel
**Meals:** Lunch
**Travel:** Pickup from your Cairo hotel · private air-conditioned car, Cairo → Alexandria
**Pace:** A full day, with a rest before the evening

### Day 2 · Alexandria → Cairo
**The Bibliotheca, the Roman theatre, El Montaza and the drive home**

**Morning.** Breakfast at your hotel. The Bibliotheca Alexandrina, the modern library opened in 2002 in homage to the ancient Library of Alexandria: a vast tilted disc facing the sea, its walls carved with letters from the world’s scripts, and inside, a reading room that steps down in terraces beneath the sloping roof. Then the Roman Theatre at Kom El Dikka: thirteen tiers of white marble around a small stage, made for lectures and music rather than spectacle, with excavated streets and baths around it.

**Midday.** Lunch at a local restaurant.

**Afternoon.** A walk in the gardens of El Montaza, on the bay at the eastern end of the city. Then into your car for the drive back to Cairo, about three hours, arriving at your hotel around 19:00 to 20:00.

**The day:** Bibliotheca Alexandrina · Kom El Dikka Roman Theatre · El Montaza gardens

## Is this journey for you?

**A good fit if you:**
- are based in Cairo and want Egypt’s Greco-Roman layers without rushing them into one day
- want a night by the Mediterranean in a Cairo trip

**Worth knowing:**
- About three hours on the road each way, with an early start on Day 1.
- The Sound and Light Show is in English, with headphones for other languages.
- The catacombs have a spiral stair and the stone can be wet. Wear shoes with grip.
- Most of ancient Alexandria lies under the modern city. What you see is what survived underground, what was too big to move, or what was rebuilt from older stone.
