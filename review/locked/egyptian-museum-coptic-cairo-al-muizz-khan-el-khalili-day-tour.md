## Opening

Cairo doesn’t ease you in. It gives you Pharaonic, Coptic and Islamic history all at once and leaves you to find the thread. This private day finds it for you, in order: the Egyptian Museum on Tahrir Square in the morning, the churches and synagogue of Coptic Cairo, then the medieval avenue of Al-Muizz Street and the Khan El Khalili bazaar. It’s a full day on foot, and a structured first look at the city before you explore on your own.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The museum comes first, while you’re fresh and before the heat. In summer, the hours between noon and three are serious, and the museum keeps you indoors for the worst of the morning. Coptic Cairo follows, with lunch after it, and the afternoon moves to the medieval city, ending in the bazaar. Because the car and guide are yours alone, the order can flex: a longer museum morning, a shorter bazaar.

## Your day

### Morning · The Egyptian Museum

**Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you in a private air-conditioned car.

**The Egyptian Museum, Tahrir Square**
The red-domed museum has stood on Tahrir Square since 1902. Tutankhamun’s treasure has moved to the Grand Egyptian Museum and the royal mummies to the NMEC, but more than 100,000 objects remain. Your guide picks a route through them: the Narmer Palette, from around 3100 BC and the unification of Egypt; the painted Old Kingdom statues of Rahotep and Nofret; and the funerary treasure of Yuya and Thuya. Allow about two hours.
*Sites:* Egyptian Museum

### Midday · Coptic Cairo and lunch

**The Hanging Church**
Built over the southern gatehouse of the Roman fortress of Babylon, which is why it seems to hang. Glass panels in the floor show the Roman stonework below the nave. Inside it’s dim and cool, with ivory-inlaid screens and old icons.

**Abu Serga and the Ben Ezra Synagogue**
A few minutes’ walk away, the church of Saints Sergius and Bacchus keeps a crypt that Coptic tradition holds as a shelter of the Holy Family on their flight into Egypt. Next to the church enclosure stands the Ben Ezra Synagogue, whose storeroom for worn-out texts produced the Cairo Geniza: some 400,000 manuscript fragments that changed how scholars read medieval Mediterranean life.
*Sites:* Hanging Church · Abu Serga · Ben Ezra Synagogue

**Lunch at a local restaurant (included)**
Drinks aren’t included.

### Afternoon · Al-Muizz Street and the bazaar

**Al-Muizz Street**
The spine of the medieval city, laid out by the Fatimids after 969 AD and now pedestrianised. Walking north, you pass the Qalawun Complex of 1284–85, with its mausoleum, madrasa and public hospital, and the Sultan Al-Ghuri Complex of 1505, built just before the Ottoman conquest. Look up at the striped stonework, the carved stalactite ceilings and the wooden balconies.
*Sites:* Al-Muizz Street · Qalawun Complex · Al-Ghuri Complex

**Khan El Khalili**
The bazaar has traded here since the Mamluk period: loud, crowded and deliberately confusing, selling silver, brass, spices in paper cones, textiles and souvenirs of every quality. Bargaining is expected. Find Al-Fishawy, the old coffeehouse in an alley near the centre, for tea even if you buy nothing.
*Sites:* Khan El Khalili · Al-Fishawy

## Is this day for you?

**A good fit if you:**
- are new to Cairo and want its three great layers, Pharaonic, Coptic and Islamic, in one day
- want the reasons behind what you see, not just the sights

**Worth knowing:**
- This is the Tahrir museum, not the Grand Egyptian Museum. Tutankhamun’s treasure is at the GEM, and the royal mummies are at the NMEC: both separate visits.
- It’s a full day with six stops and a lot of walking on uneven stone. Wear closed, comfortable shoes.
- In summer, the heat between noon and three is serious.
