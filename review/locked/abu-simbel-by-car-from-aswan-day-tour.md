## Opening

Abu Simbel is a long way from anywhere: about three and a half hours south of Aswan, on the desert road towards Sudan. Then the road drops towards Lake Nasser and the two temples appear, cut into the sandstone cliff, with four seated colossi of Ramses II looking out across the water. It’s an early start and seven hours in the car. With your own car and Egyptologist, the time at the temples is entirely yours.

## Why the day runs this way

Leaving at 06:00 puts you at Abu Simbel in the morning, before the sun on the open site turns harsh. The road itself is a smooth desert highway along the western side of Lake Nasser, not a scenic drive. After the temples, lunch looks out over the lake, and you’re back in Aswan around 16:00 with the evening free. Because the tour is private, your guide paces the visit around you.

## Your day

### Morning · The road south and the temples

**06:00 · Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you in a private air-conditioned car. The drive south through open desert takes about three and a half hours.

**The Great Temple of Ramses II**
Built around 1264 BC. Four seated statues of Ramses, each about twenty metres tall, flank the entrance: a statement of Egyptian power to anyone arriving from Nubia. Inside, eight pillars show the king as Osiris, and the walls carry his Battle of Kadesh against the Hittites, told as a clean Egyptian victory. In the sanctuary at the back, Ramses sits beside Ptah, Amun-Ra and Ra-Horakhty.
*Sites:* Great Temple of Ramses II

**The Temple of Hathor and Nefertari**
About a hundred metres to the north, built for Nefertari, Ramses’s principal wife, and the goddess Hathor. On the façade, the queen’s statues stand at the same scale as the king’s, a rare sign of her standing. Inside, the temple is more intimate, and its painted reliefs keep much of their colour.
*Sites:* Temple of Hathor and Nefertari

**The rescue of the temples**
When the High Dam’s reservoir was about to flood the valley, a UNESCO-led campaign between 1964 and 1968 cut both temples into blocks and rebuilt them sixty-five metres higher, keeping their orientation. Look closely and you can still see the seams. Allow two to two and a half hours at the site.
