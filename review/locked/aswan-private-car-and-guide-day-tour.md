## Your day: ideas for your eight hours

These are suggestions, not a fixed route. Pick the ones that interest you.

**The Unfinished Obelisk**
In the northern granite quarries, cut straight from the bedrock until a crack appeared, probably under Hatshepsut, and abandoned where it lay: 42 metres long, still joined to the rock on one side, with the quarrymen’s marks all over it. It answers the question no finished monument can: how did they do it?
*Sites:* Unfinished Obelisk

**Philae Temple**
A short motorboat ride to the temple of Isis, where the old religion held out until the 6th century AD. Flooded after the dams were built, it was taken apart block by block between 1972 and 1980 and rebuilt on higher ground on Agilkia Island.
*Sites:* Philae Temple

**The High Dam**
Finished in 1970, it created Lake Nasser, ended the annual flood that had shaped Egyptian farming for millennia, and displaced the Nubians from their villages. There isn’t much to look at; your guide’s account of what it changed is the reason to stop.
*Sites:* Aswan High Dam

**The Nubian Museum**
Opened in 1997 and too often skipped: Nubian history from flint tools to Christian frescoes, Meroitic inscriptions, gold, reconstructed houses and the story of the 20th-century displacement. Nubian kings ruled all of Egypt as the 25th Dynasty. Allow at least an hour and a half.
*Sites:* Nubian Museum

**The local market**
Aswan’s market, with its spices, baskets and the everyday life of the city: a good way to end the day.
*Sites:* Aswan market

### A suggested shape

- **Morning:** the Unfinished Obelisk, then the High Dam and Philae, which lie south of the city in the same direction
- **Afternoon:** the Nubian Museum, out of the midday sun
- **Late afternoon:** the local market

**Back to your hotel or ship**, at the end of your eight hours.
