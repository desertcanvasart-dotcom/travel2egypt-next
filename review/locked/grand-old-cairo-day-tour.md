## Opening

Cairo reveals itself at street level: under a gate that has stood since 1092, inside a church built over a Roman fortress wall, in a bazaar lane where a craftsman does what his grandfather did. This private day crosses three of the city’s oldest quarters, Islamic Cairo, Coptic Cairo and the Mokattam Hills, and ends in Al-Azhar Park as the light softens over the minarets. It’s for travellers who have done Giza and want the rest of Cairo.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The day moves through about twelve centuries of the city, one layer at a time: the medieval Islamic city first, then south to the Coptic and Jewish quarter that is older than the Arab conquest, up into the hills for the Cave Church, back to the bazaar, and the park last, for the view and a calm end to a dense day. Eight stops sounds like a lot, but none of them takes long, so the day covers them all comfortably. It’s a day in Cairo’s working streets rather than behind ticket gates. The pace is deliberate; with a private guide, you stay as long as you like where something holds you.

## Your day

### Morning · Islamic Cairo

**Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you in a private air-conditioned car.

**Bab Zuweila**
Built in 1092 by the Fatimid vizier Badr al-Gamali, one of three surviving gates of the medieval city, topped by the twin minarets of the Mosque of al-Mu’ayyad. Climb up, if you like, for the view down the Al-Muizz corridor.
*Sites:* Bab Zuweila · Mosque of al-Mu’ayyad

**The Mosque of Ibn Tulun**
Completed in 879 AD by Ahmad ibn Tulun, the Abbasid governor who briefly made Egypt his own, and one of the oldest mosques in Cairo to survive largely in its original form. A vast open courtyard ringed by arcades, and a spiral minaret modelled on Samarra, which you can climb. Its austerity isn’t emptiness; it’s confidence.
*Sites:* Mosque of Ibn Tulun

### Midday · Coptic Cairo and lunch

**The Hanging Church, Abu Serga and Ben Ezra**
The Hanging Church sits over the gatehouse of the Roman fortress of Babylon, so its nave seems to float, dense with carved ivory and icons. Nearby, Abu Serga keeps the crypt where tradition says the Holy Family rested, its columns borrowed from older buildings. The Ben Ezra Synagogue completes the quarter’s three faiths; here the Cairo Geniza was found in 1896.
*Sites:* Hanging Church · Abu Serga · Ben Ezra Synagogue

**Lunch at a local restaurant (included)**
Drinks aren’t included.

### Afternoon · Mokattam, the bazaar and the park

**The Cave Church of Saint Simon**
Carved into the limestone cliffs of Mokattam, with an amphitheatre that holds thousands. The Zabbaleen, Cairo’s Coptic community of rubbish collectors, have worshipped here since the 1970s, under monumental rock-cut reliefs of biblical scenes. Few international visitors find it; it’s the surprise of the day.
*Sites:* Cave Church of Saint Simon · Mokattam

**Khan El Khalili**
The point here is the craftsmen more than the shops. Your guide can take you to the coppersmiths, the tentmakers’ lanes of Khayamiyya and the makers of mashrabiya woodwork. What you buy is your business.
*Sites:* Khan El Khalili · Khayamiyya

**Al-Azhar Park**
Terraced gardens and fountains over what was for centuries a rubbish heap, opened in 2005 by the Aga Khan Trust for Culture, with a restored Ayyubid wall along its edge. From the upper terrace, the Citadel and the minarets of the old city fill the skyline.
*Sites:* Al-Azhar Park
