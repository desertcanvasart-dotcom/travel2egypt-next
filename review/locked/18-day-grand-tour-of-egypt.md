## Opening

Four Egypts in one journey, with enough days in each that none feels merely visited: Cairo’s older layers and the pyramids, the Nile from Abu Simbel and Aswan to Luxor, the Red Sea reefs at Hurghada and Sharm El Sheikh, and the granite mountains of southern Sinai.

---

## Why this route works

Many trips pick one Egypt and stay in it. This one moves through four that rarely share a programme, and puts them in an order that builds: Cairo’s older layers first, the south and the river next, then the sea, then the mountains, and Giza last, when the whole sequence stands behind you. By the time you reach the pyramids on Day 17 you have seen what came before them and what came after.

### Day 2 · Cairo
**Old Cairo, the Citadel and Khan el-Khalili**

The older layers first. Old Cairo, the ancient quarter of Fustat: the Ben Ezra Synagogue, where tradition holds the infant Moses was drawn from the Nile, the Hanging Church, built in the 4th or 5th century AD above a Roman fortress gatehouse, the Coptic Museum next door, and Abu Serga Church, built over a crypt said to have sheltered the Holy Family. In the afternoon, the Citadel of Saladin, from which Egypt was governed for seven centuries, and the alabaster mosque of Muhammad Ali inside it. The day ends in Khan el-Khalili, trading since the 14th century.

**The day:** Old Cairo · Ben Ezra Synagogue · Hanging Church · Coptic Museum · Abu Serga Church · Citadel of Saladin · Mosque of Muhammad Ali · Khan el-Khalili
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch
**Travel:** Private transfers

---

### Day 3 · Cairo
**Memphis, Saqqara and Dahshur**

The pyramid-building sequence from its start. Memphis, the first capital of a united Egypt, founded around 3100 BC, with the fallen limestone colossus of Ramesses II and a great alabaster sphinx. Saqqara, where the Step Pyramid of Djoser, built around 2650 BC, is the oldest large stone building in the world. Dahshur, where the builders refined the form a century later: the Bent Pyramid, with its abrupt change of angle halfway up, and the Red Pyramid, smooth from the start.

**The day:** Memphis · Saqqara · Step Pyramid · Dahshur · Bent Pyramid · Red Pyramid
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch
**Travel:** Private transfers

---

### Day 4 · Cairo → Abu Simbel → Aswan
**The temples of Ramesses II, and a felucca**

The longest day of the trip. An early flight south to Abu Simbel, near the Sudanese border, where Ramesses II had two temples cut into a sandstone cliff in the 13th century BC, the larger one guarded by four seated figures of the king, each about twenty metres high. Between 1964 and 1968 both temples were cut into blocks and raised 65 metres, to save them from Lake Nasser. The flight continues to Aswan, where the Nile narrows toward the first cataract. In the afternoon, a felucca past Kitchener’s Island, now a botanical garden, with a Nubian village and the Aga Khan Mausoleum on the west bank.

**The day:** Abu Simbel · felucca at Aswan
**Overnight:** Aswan hotel
**Meals:** Lunch
**Travel:** Domestic flights Cairo → Abu Simbel and Abu Simbel → Aswan · private transfers · felucca
**Pace:** The longest day

---

### Day 5 · Aswan
**The High Dam, the Unfinished Obelisk, Philae and boarding the ship**

The High Dam, completed in 1970, which created Lake Nasser. The Unfinished Obelisk, 42 metres long, abandoned in its granite quarry with a crack running through it. In the afternoon, Philae, the temple of Isis, taken apart and rebuilt on Agilkia Island between 1972 and 1980, reached by motorboat. You board your Nile cruise ship at the end of the day.

**The day:** Aswan High Dam · Unfinished Obelisk · Temple of Philae
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Aswan
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers · motorboat to Philae

---

### Day 6 · Aswan → Kom Ombo → Edfu
**Two river temples**

The ship sails north. Kom Ombo, a double temple divided between Sobek, the crocodile god, and Horus the Elder, with a small museum of mummified crocodiles. Then Edfu, and the Temple of Horus, built between 237 and 57 BC and the most complete temple in Egypt, with a colossal granite falcon at its entrance.

**The day:** Temple of Kom Ombo · Crocodile Museum · Temple of Horus at Edfu
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By cruise · horse-drawn carriage at Edfu

---

### Day 7 · Luxor
**The West Bank**

The ship reaches Luxor. The Valley of the Kings, with more than sixty royal tombs cut into the limestone, their walls still bright with scenes of the afterlife, and Tutankhamun’s tomb. The three colonnaded terraces of the temple of Hatshepsut at Deir el-Bahari, built for a woman who ruled as pharaoh for about twenty years. The Colossi of Memnon, two seated figures of Amenhotep III, close the day.

**The day:** Valley of the Kings · Temple of Hatshepsut · Colossi of Memnon
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Luxor
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers · by cruise

**Optional on this day:**
- The tombs of Tutankhamun, Seti I and Ramesses VI: extra tickets

---

### Day 8 · Luxor
**Karnak and Luxor Temple**

The living side of Thebes. Karnak, expanded by nearly every pharaoh over some 1,500 years, with a Great Hypostyle Hall of 134 columns up to 21 metres tall. Luxor Temple, joined to Karnak by the Avenue of Sphinxes, which reopened in November 2021. A horse-drawn carriage through the markets closes the afternoon. You leave the ship and spend the night in a Luxor hotel.

**The day:** Karnak · Great Hypostyle Hall · Luxor Temple · Avenue of Sphinxes
**Overnight:** Luxor hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch
**Travel:** Private transfers · horse-drawn carriage

---

**East across the desert to the sea**

The drive from Luxor to Hurghada takes three to four hours across the Eastern Desert, limestone plateau and then the granite Red Sea Hills. You check in to your all-inclusive resort. After eight days of early starts, the rest of the day is yours.

**Overnight:** Red Sea resort, Hurghada, all-inclusive
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private car, Luxor → Hurghada
**Pace:** Easy

### Day 10 · Hurghada
**The reef**

Snorkelling over coral gardens at the Giftun Islands, a protected marine archipelago an hour offshore. A glass-bottom boat for anyone who would rather stay dry, windsurfing, and diving for certified divers. Nothing is compulsory.

**The day:** your choice
**Overnight:** Red Sea resort, Hurghada, all-inclusive

**Across the Red Sea by air**

A flight to Sharm El Sheikh, at the tip of the Sinai Peninsula. It is more developed and more international than Hurghada, and Ras Mohammed, a protected marine park just to the south, is known to divers around the world. The rest of the day is free.

### Day 15 · Sharm El Sheikh → Saint Catherine
**Into the granite mountains**

The quietest day of the trip. The drive takes you through the granite mountains of southern Sinai, a landscape severe enough to explain why Christians and Jews came here seeking God in the desert. Saint Catherine’s Monastery, founded in the 6th century AD by the Emperor Justinian around the site traditionally identified as the Burning Bush, is the oldest continuously inhabited Christian monastery in the world, with a library of early manuscripts. Access inside changes, and your guide confirms what is open on the day.

**The day:** Saint Catherine’s Monastery
**Overnight:** Saint Catherine hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private car, Sharm El Sheikh → Saint Catherine

**Optional on this day:**
- The pre-dawn climb of Mount Sinai, to be at the summit for sunrise on the morning of Day 16, as pilgrims have done for fifteen centuries: arranged on request

### Day 16 · Saint Catherine → Cairo
**Back to the city, and Sound and Light at Giza**

The drive back to Cairo is timed for the Sound and Light show at Giza: the plateau flood-lit, the Sphinx telling its own history. After two weeks in Egypt, the pyramids at night carry a different weight than they would have on the first day.

**The day:** Sound and Light show at Giza
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch
**Travel:** Private car, Saint Catherine → Cairo
**Pace:** Easy

### Day 17 · Cairo
**The pyramids of Giza and the Grand Egyptian Museum**

The day many travellers have been building toward since Saqqara. The pyramids of Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure, built across roughly half a century around 2500 BC, and the Great Sphinx. They are not the oldest monuments you have seen, but they organise everything else, and the sequence behind you gives the plateau a context a first-day visit cannot have. In the afternoon, the Grand Egyptian Museum beside the plateau, fully open since 1 November 2025, with the complete Tutankhamun collection.

**The day:** Giza pyramids · Great Sphinx · Grand Egyptian Museum
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch
**Travel:** Private transfers

**Optional on this day:**
- Inside one of the pyramids: extra ticket
