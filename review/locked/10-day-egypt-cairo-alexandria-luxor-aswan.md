## Opening

The classic spine of Egypt for a first visit, at a pace that lets each place register: Giza, Alexandria, the pyramids south of Cairo, two days in Luxor, the road to Aswan, Abu Simbel, and both of Cairo’s great museums to close.

---

## Why this route works

The obvious sites are obvious for a reason; the question is pace. Alexandria comes on Day 2, while you’re fresh. Memphis, Saqqara and Dahshur get their own day. Luxor gets two, one for each bank. And Cairo’s museums come last, after the sites that produced their objects.

## Your ten days

### Day 1 · Cairo
**Arrival and Giza**

After the airport transfer, the first afternoon goes to Giza: the pyramids of Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure, built around 2500 BC, and the Sphinx below. The scale, the distances between them, and the silence once you move off the edges are what photographs miss. Cairo gives you the headline first.

**The day:** Giza pyramids · Great Sphinx
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Travel:** Meet and greet at the airport · private transfers

**Optional on this day:**
- Inside one of the pyramids: extra ticket

### Day 2 · Alexandria
**The Mediterranean detour**

North by the desert road to Alexander’s city, founded in 331 BC. The Bibliotheca Alexandrina, its tilted granite disc carved with the world’s scripts; Qaitbay Fort, on the foundations of the Pharos lighthouse; the Catacombs of Kom El Shuqafa, where Egyptian religion absorbed Greek and Roman forms; Pompey’s Pillar, raised for Diocletian in AD 297; and the Alexandria National Museum. Seafood on the waterfront, then back to Cairo in the evening.

**The day:** Bibliotheca Alexandrina · Qaitbay Fort · Catacombs of Kom El Shuqafa · Pompey’s Pillar · Alexandria National Museum
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Lunch
**Travel:** Private car, Cairo → Alexandria → Cairo

### Day 3 · Memphis, Saqqara and Dahshur
**The pyramid sequence**

Memphis, first capital of a united Egypt, with the fallen colossus of Ramses II and the alabaster sphinx. Saqqara and the Step Pyramid of Djoser, built by Imhotep around 2650 BC, where the pyramid form was invented. Dahshur: the Bent Pyramid, corrected halfway up, and Sneferu’s Red Pyramid, the first successful true pyramid. Having seen Giza already, you now see the learning curve behind it. Lunch is a traditional mixed grill.

**The day:** Memphis · Saqqara · Step Pyramid of Djoser · Bent Pyramid · Red Pyramid
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Lunch
**Travel:** Private transfers

### Day 4 · Cairo → Luxor
**The East Bank**

A morning flight to Luxor, ancient Thebes. Karnak, built over about 1,500 years, with the Great Hypostyle Hall of 134 columns over more than 5,000 square metres, the tallest 21 metres high, seen without a tight schedule. Then Luxor Temple and the Luxor Museum, with its New Kingdom sculpture. A free evening.

**The day:** Karnak · Great Hypostyle Hall · Luxor Temple · Luxor Museum
**Overnight:** Luxor hotel
**Travel:** Domestic flight Cairo → Luxor · private transfers

### Day 5 · Luxor · the West Bank
**The Valley of the Kings, Hatshepsut and the Colossi**

The land of the dead. The Valley of the Kings, more than sixty royal tombs still vivid after three thousand years: Tutankhamun’s, small beside Ramses VI’s or Seti I’s. Hatshepsut’s three terraces at Deir el-Bahari, built for the woman who ruled as pharaoh for some twenty years; and the Colossi of Memnon. A free afternoon.

**The day:** Valley of the Kings · Temple of Hatshepsut · Colossi of Memnon
**Overnight:** Luxor hotel
**Travel:** Private transfers
**Pace:** Free afternoon

**Optional on this day:**
- The tombs of Tutankhamun, Seti I and Ramses VI: extra tickets

### Day 6 · Luxor → Aswan
**Edfu, Kom Ombo and a felucca**

South along the Nile, stopping twice. Edfu’s Temple of Horus, built between 237 and 57 BC, with walls, roof and sanctuary intact. Kom Ombo, the double temple of Sobek and Horus the Elder, with its mummified crocodiles. Aswan in the afternoon, and a felucca past Kitchener’s Island and the Nubian village on the west bank as the light drops.

**The day:** Temple of Horus at Edfu · Temple of Kom Ombo · felucca
**Overnight:** Aswan hotel
**Travel:** Private car, Luxor → Aswan · felucca

### Day 7 · Abu Simbel and Aswan
**The temples of Ramses II, the High Dam and the obelisk**

The longest day. An early, short flight to Abu Simbel: the Great Temple, its four seated figures of Ramses II twenty metres high, and the Temple of Nefertari, cut into 807 numbered blocks between 1964 and 1968 and rebuilt 65 metres higher and 200 metres back. In the afternoon, Aswan: the High Dam, completed in 1970, and the Unfinished Obelisk, cracked in its quarry. Dinner in Aswan.

**The day:** Abu Simbel · Aswan High Dam · Unfinished Obelisk
**Overnight:** Aswan hotel
**Meals:** Dinner
**Travel:** Domestic flights Aswan ↔ Abu Simbel · private transfers
**Pace:** The longest day

### Day 8 · Aswan → Cairo
**Old Cairo, Khan El Khalili and a Nile dinner cruise**

A flight north and the city’s older layers: the Ben Ezra Synagogue; the Hanging Church above the gatehouse of a Roman fortress; and the Coptic Museum, tracing Egypt’s Christian centuries. Then Khan El Khalili, trading since 1382, and a dinner cruise on the Nile.

**The day:** Ben Ezra Synagogue · Hanging Church · Coptic Museum · Khan El Khalili · Nile dinner cruise
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Dinner (on the dinner cruise)
**Travel:** Domestic flight Aswan → Cairo · private transfers

> More about this stop → *(expands the essay’s Old Cairo passage)*

### Day 9 · Cairo
**The two museums**

The Egyptian Museum on Tahrir Square, opened in 1902, from the Narmer Palette to the gold of Tanis. Then the Grand Egyptian Museum at the foot of the plateau, with the complete Tutankhamun collection, some five thousand objects, the gold mask among them. A heavy day, and the right ending: the objects carry a different weight after the tombs and temples.

**The day:** Egyptian Museum (Tahrir) · Grand Egyptian Museum
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Travel:** Private transfers
**Pace:** A full day

**Optional on this day:**
- The royal mummies at the National Museum of Egyptian Civilisation
