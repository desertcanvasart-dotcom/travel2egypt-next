## Opening

Fourteen days that let Egypt build up rather than overwhelm, for families who want the pyramids, the river and the sea without a rush: Giza and the Grand Egyptian Museum, a three-night Nile cruise from Aswan to Luxor with two full days in Luxor, four nights by the Red Sea at Hurghada, and a last stretch in Cairo.

---

## Why this route works

Families remember the days that had room in them. The monument days come first and are long, so the rest days that follow are earned, and the rest days in turn make the next monument day possible. The ship is the easiest stretch for younger children, and Hurghada is the other. Cairo comes back at the end, when the children have the energy for Coptic and Islamic Cairo, and the Nile closes the trip as it opened it.

## Your fourteen days

### Day 1 · Cairo
**Arrival and a welcome dinner**

You’re met at Cairo airport and taken to your hotel. In the evening, a welcome dinner and an introduction to dishes worth knowing: koshari, the layered staple of lentils, rice and pasta, and molokhia, the dark green herb soup that has been on Egyptian tables since the pharaohs. There is no monument today, on purpose.

**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Dinner
**Travel:** Meet and greet at the airport · private transfer
**Pace:** Easy

---

### Day 2 · Cairo
**Giza and the Grand Egyptian Museum**

The morning is the one most families come to Egypt for: the Giza plateau, with the Great Pyramid of Khufu and the pyramids of Khafre and Menkaure, built around 2500 BC, and the Great Sphinx below them. In the afternoon, the Grand Egyptian Museum at the edge of the plateau, fully open since 1 November 2025, with the complete Tutankhamun collection: the gold mask, the innermost coffin, the canopic jars and the throne.

**The day:** Giza pyramids · Great Sphinx · Grand Egyptian Museum
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch
**Travel:** Private transfers

**Optional on this day:**
- A camel ride on the plateau: extra
- Inside one of the pyramids: extra ticket

---

### Day 3 · Cairo → Aswan
**The High Dam, Philae and boarding the ship**

A morning flight south to Aswan, quieter and greener than Cairo, where the Nile narrows at the first cataract. The High Dam, completed in 1970, which created Lake Nasser and drowned much of ancient Nubia. Then Philae, the temple of Isis, taken apart and rebuilt stone by stone on Agilkia Island between 1972 and 1980, reached by motorboat. You board your Nile cruise ship in Aswan, and the evening is free for a walk on the Corniche.

**The day:** Aswan High Dam · Temple of Philae
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Aswan
**Meals:** Lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Domestic flight Cairo → Aswan · private transfers · motorboat to Philae

---

### Day 4 · Aswan → Kom Ombo → Edfu → Esna
**The crocodile temple and the temple of Horus**

Kom Ombo, a double temple divided between Sobek, the crocodile god, and Horus the Elder, with a small museum of mummified crocodiles that younger travellers rarely forget. The ship sails to Edfu and the Temple of Horus, built between 237 and 57 BC and the most complete temple in Egypt, so the way an Egyptian temple worked is easy to read. The rest of the day is yours on the water.

**The day:** Temple of Kom Ombo · Crocodile Museum · Temple of Horus at Edfu
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Esna
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** By cruise · horse-drawn carriage at Edfu

---

### Day 5 · Luxor
**The West Bank**

The ship reaches Luxor in the morning. The Valley of the Kings, with more than sixty royal tombs cut into a desert valley, their corridors painted with scenes from the funerary texts, and Tutankhamun’s tomb. The three colonnaded terraces of the temple of Hatshepsut at Deir el-Bahari, built for a woman who ruled as pharaoh for about twenty years. The Colossi of Memnon, two seated statues of Amenhotep III, to close the day.

**The day:** Valley of the Kings · Temple of Hatshepsut · Colossi of Memnon
**Overnight:** Nile cruise ship, Luxor
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers · by cruise

**Optional on this day:**
- The tombs of Tutankhamun, Seti I and Ramesses VI: extra tickets

---

### Day 6 · Luxor
**Karnak and Luxor Temple**

The East Bank. Karnak, enlarged over about fifteen centuries, with a Great Hypostyle Hall of 134 columns up to 21 metres tall. In the afternoon, Luxor Temple, joined to Karnak by the Avenue of Sphinxes, reopened in November 2021, begun under Amenhotep III around 1380 BC, with a 13th-century mosque still standing inside it. You leave the ship and spend the night in a Luxor hotel.

**The day:** Karnak · Great Hypostyle Hall · Luxor Temple · Avenue of Sphinxes
**Overnight:** Luxor hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch
**Travel:** Private transfers

---

### Day 7 · Luxor → Hurghada
**East across the desert to the sea**

The drive from Luxor to Hurghada takes three to four hours across the Eastern Desert, limestone plateau and then the granite Red Sea Hills. The drive is plain on purpose: a decompression between six days of dense history and four of open water. You check in to your all-inclusive resort.

**Overnight:** Red Sea resort, Hurghada, all-inclusive
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private car, Luxor → Hurghada
**Pace:** Easy

---

### Day 8 · Hurghada
**The reef**

Snorkelling over coral gardens, with parrotfish, angelfish and clownfish, at the Giftun Islands, a protected marine archipelago an hour offshore. A glass-bottom boat for anyone who would rather stay dry, kayaking and windsurfing, and diving for certified divers. Nothing is compulsory.

**The day:** your choice
**Overnight:** Red Sea resort, Hurghada, all-inclusive
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Pace:** As much or as little as you like

---

### Day 9 · Hurghada
**Your day by the Red Sea**

A day left open on purpose, to absorb the energy that six days of sightseeing leave in children in particular. Use the resort, go back to the reef, or walk Hurghada’s seafront.

**Overnight:** Red Sea resort, Hurghada, all-inclusive
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Pace:** As much or as little as you like

---

### Day 10 · Hurghada
**Your day by the Red Sea**

Another open day. Choose from Day 8’s options, or take a boat to one of the nearby islands for a quieter stretch of beach.

**Overnight:** Red Sea resort, Hurghada, all-inclusive
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Pace:** As much or as little as you like

---

### Day 11 · Hurghada → Cairo
**Back to the city**

The last Hurghada morning is free until the afternoon flight north. After four nights at resort pace, Cairo announces itself again. The evening is free.

**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast
**Travel:** Domestic flight Hurghada → Cairo · private transfers
**Pace:** Easy

---

### Day 12 · Cairo
**Coptic Cairo, Islamic Cairo and a Nile dinner cruise**

Cairo’s other face. Coptic Cairo, in the old Roman fortress quarter of Babylon, one of the oldest continuously inhabited Christian communities in the world, with the Hanging Church, built in the 4th or 5th century AD, and the Coptic Museum. In the afternoon, Islamic Cairo: Khan el-Khalili, trading since the 14th century, and the Mosque of al-Hussein at the heart of the quarter. The day closes with a farewell dinner cruise on the Nile.

**The day:** Coptic Cairo · Hanging Church · Coptic Museum · Khan el-Khalili · Mosque of al-Hussein · Nile dinner cruise
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner
**Travel:** Private transfers

---

### Day 13 · Cairo
**A lighter day**

No fixed programme. A last morning at the Grand Egyptian Museum can be arranged, or coffee shops, markets and the Nile-side gardens, as you prefer.

**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast
**Pace:** Easy

---

### Day 14 · Cairo
**Departure**

Breakfast, and the transfer to Cairo airport.

**Overnight:** —
**Meals:** Breakfast
**Travel:** Private transfer to the airport

- want the pyramids, the Nile and the sea in one trip
- would rather see fewer places and see them properly

**Worth knowing:**
