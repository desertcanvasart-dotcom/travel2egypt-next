## Opening

A felucca is not a dahabiya. It is a small, open wooden sailing boat with no cabins: you sleep on cushions on deck under an awning or the open sky, the crew cooks for you, and the toilet is ashore. This private journey puts you on one for five days, chartered for your party alone, sailing north from Aswan between quiet islands and three temples to Edfu, with Cairo and a sleeper train before and Luxor and the train after.

---

## Why this route works

North from Aswan is the reach where feluccas still sail overnight, between islands and banks that have changed little, with Kom Ombo, Gebel el-Silsila and Edfu along the way. The boat goes with the current but usually against the wind, so progress is slow and set by the weather; five days allow for that. The sleeper train links Cairo with Aswan and Luxor overnight, so no daylight is spent travelling.

### Day 2 · Giza → the sleeper train
**The pyramids, the Grand Egyptian Museum and the night train south**

Giza in the morning: the pyramids of Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure, built around 2500 BC, with a walk inside Khafre’s or Menkaure’s, the panorama, and the Sphinx with the Valley Temple below it. Then the Grand Egyptian Museum on the edge of the plateau, where Tutankhamun’s complete burial collection is shown together: the gold mask, the nested coffins, the canopic shrine. It deserves at least two unhurried hours. In the evening you board the sleeper train to Aswan, with dinner aboard.

**The day:** Giza pyramids · inside Khafre’s or Menkaure’s pyramid · Great Sphinx · Valley Temple · Grand Egyptian Museum
**Overnight:** Sleeper train, Cairo → Aswan
**Meals:** Breakfast · dinner (on the train)
**Travel:** Private transfers · sleeper train, Cairo → Aswan

### Day 3 · Aswan
**The High Dam, the Unfinished Obelisk and a first felucca**

By morning you are in Upper Egypt. The Aswan High Dam, completed in 1970, which ended the annual flood and created Lake Nasser; then the Unfinished Obelisk, still lying in its granite quarry where it cracked, the quarrymen’s tool marks plain on the stone. In the afternoon, a first sail: around Elephantine Island, past the gardens of Kitchener’s Island and below the Aga Khan Mausoleum on the west bank. A last night in a hotel before the river.

**The day:** Aswan High Dam · Unfinished Obelisk · felucca around Elephantine Island
**Overnight:** Aswan hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast (on the train)

### Day 5 · The river → Kom Ombo
**Harbeyab and Baloly, and the double temple**

A day of sailing past Harbeyab and Baloly, banks of palms and fields that look much as nineteenth-century travellers drew them, with time to swim where the crew says it’s safe, or simply to watch the river. In the afternoon, Kom Ombo on its bend: a Ptolemaic temple built for two gods at once, every hall and sanctuary doubled for Sobek and Horus the Elder, with mummified crocodiles in the museum beside it. Your Egyptologist meets you at the temple.

### Day 7 · Gebel el-Silsila
**The quarry that built the temples**

Gebel el-Silsila, where the river narrows between sandstone cliffs, was the great sandstone quarry of the New Kingdom, the source of the stone for much of Karnak and Luxor. Along the west bank, rock-cut shrines and stelae left by the officials and workmen, and the Speos of Horemheb, a chapel cut into the cliff. Few visitors stop here, and your Egyptologist has the site largely to your party. Then on downstream for the night.

**The day:** Gebel el-Silsila · Speos of Horemheb · rock-cut shrines
**Overnight:** On the felucca, moored at the bank

### Day 8 · Fawaza → Edfu
**The last sail, and the Temple of Horus**

The last morning on the water, past Fawaza Island, to Edfu. The Temple of Horus, begun under Ptolemy III in 237 BC and finished some 180 years later, still has its enclosure wall, halls, sanctuary and roof, and two black granite falcons at the entrance. At the sites upstream you were reading ruins; at Edfu you are reading a building. A last night on deck, moored near the town.

**The day:** Fawaza Island · Temple of Horus at Edfu
**Overnight:** On the felucca, moored near Edfu

### Day 9 · Edfu → Luxor → the sleeper train
**The West Bank, Karnak and Luxor Temple, then the night train north**

Breakfast on board and goodbye to the crew, then the drive north to Luxor. The West Bank first: the Valley of the Kings, more than sixty royal tombs, three of them on your ticket; Hatshepsut’s terraces at Deir el-Bahari; and the Colossi of Memnon. In the afternoon Karnak, its Great Hypostyle Hall of 134 columns up to 21 metres, and Luxor Temple at the end of the Avenue of Sphinxes. In the evening, the sleeper train to Cairo, with dinner aboard.

### Day 10 · Cairo
**Memphis, Saqqara, Dahshur and Khan el-Khalili, then departure**

The train reaches Cairo in the morning. Memphis, first capital of a united Egypt around 3100 BC, with the fallen colossus of Ramses II; Saqqara, where the Step Pyramid of Djoser, around 2650 BC, shows the experiment that made Giza possible, with the Imhotep Museum and a nobles’ tomb; and Dahshur, where Sneferu’s Bent and Red Pyramids show the builders correcting their angles. After lunch, Khan el-Khalili, and the transfer to the airport for an evening flight.

## Is this journey for you?

**A good fit if you:**
- would rather sleep on deck by a river island than in a cruise cabin
- know the standard route already, or want a slower one
- see the rustic side of a felucca as the point, not a compromise

**Worth knowing:**
- The felucca is simple: no cabins, no shower, and the toilet is ashore. If that isn’t for you, a Nile cruise or a dahabiya is the right trip.
- A felucca depends on the wind, and the wind can drop at any time, anywhere in Egypt. When it does, the crew may arrange a tow by motorboat to keep to the plan.
- October to April is the best sailing season on this stretch. Nights on the river are cold in winter, so bring a warm layer.
- Day 9 and Day 10 are full days after the slow ones, and Day 10 ends at the airport.
