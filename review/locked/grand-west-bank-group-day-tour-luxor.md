## Opening

Within a few kilometres on Luxor’s West Bank lie the tombs of pharaohs and queens, two of Egypt’s best-preserved mortuary temples and the village of the craftsmen who made all of it. This shared day stays on the West Bank and covers six sites, from the royal valleys to the craftsmen’s village, with a small group of four to eight and one licensed Egyptologist. Taken together, the sites read as what they were: a planned royal city of the dead.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The 07:00 start is how you reach the tombs before the heat and the crowds do. The Valley of the Kings comes first, then the queens, then the temples, and the day ends at Deir el-Medina, which answers the question the royal tombs leave hanging: who actually made all this? The order is fixed for the group, and timings can shift a little with traffic and the queues at each site.

## Your day

### Morning · The royal valleys

**07:00 · Pickup from your hotel**
Your guide meets the group, and you cross the river to the West Bank in an air-conditioned vehicle.

**The Valley of the Kings**
The burial ground of the New Kingdom pharaohs, about 1550–1070 BC: more than sixty tombs under a natural pyramid-shaped peak. Your ticket covers three tombs, from those open on the day. Tutankhamun’s tomb is a special tomb, with its own ticket bought at the site: it’s small and his treasure has left, but its painted burial chamber is among the most intimate in the valley.
*Sites:* Valley of the Kings

**The Valley of the Queens**
Two kilometres south, the burial ground of the New Kingdom’s queens and royal children. Your ticket covers the tombs open on the day. The tomb of Nefertari, Ramses II’s principal wife, is closed to visitors at present.
*Sites:* Valley of the Queens

**The Colossi of Memnon**
Two seated quartzite figures of Amenhotep III, eighteen metres tall, all that stands of his great mortuary temple. The Greeks heard them "sing" at dawn, until a Roman repair silenced the cracked statue.
*Sites:* Colossi of Memnon

### Midday · Hatshepsut and lunch

**The Temple of Hatshepsut**
Built for the woman who ruled as pharaoh in her own right, around 1473–1458 BC, by her architect Senenmut: three colonnaded terraces cut into the cliff, with reliefs of the voyage to Punt. Some of her images were defaced after her death, most likely under Thutmose III. About an hour here.
*Sites:* Temple of Hatshepsut

**Lunch at a local restaurant (included)**
A break out of the sun before the afternoon’s two sites.

### Afternoon · Medinet Habu and Deir el-Medina

**Medinet Habu**
Ramses III’s mortuary temple, around 1180 BC, and for us the most underrated site on the West Bank: exceptionally well preserved, with outer walls recording his wars against the Sea Peoples, one of the few contemporary accounts of them.
*Sites:* Medinet Habu

**Deir el-Medina**
The walled village of the royal tomb builders: houses, wells, a temple and their own small, steep tombs, painted for themselves by the same craftsmen who decorated the royal tombs. Their papers record strikes, petitions, debts and love poems.
*Sites:* Deir el-Medina

**Around 16:15 · Back to your hotel**
