## Opening

Cairo doesn’t present itself in order. On this private day you go from the Giza Plateau, where three Fourth Dynasty kings built their pyramids, among them the only ancient wonder still standing, to the great mosques of Sultan Hassan and al-Rifa’i below the Citadel, and then into the medieval market of Khan El Khalili: some 4,500 years in one day. It’s the essential Cairo combination for a short stay, with a guide to explain what you’re actually looking at.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The plateau comes first, in the morning, before the open, shadeless site heats up; it deserves the whole morning. After lunch you cross the city, about 25 kilometres, to the mosques and the market, which need the afternoon. Because the day is private, the order is yours to set: a longer, slower Giza morning, or the bazaar kept for last.

## Your day

### Morning · The Giza Plateau

**Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you in a private air-conditioned car.

**The three pyramids**
Khufu’s Great Pyramid stood 146.5 metres high when it was finished. Khafre’s looks taller from most angles because it stands on higher ground, and it keeps some of its original casing near the top: a detail most visitors miss. Menkaure’s is the smallest, cased in granite at its base and finished in apparent haste after the king’s death.
*Sites:* Great Pyramid of Khufu · Pyramid of Khafre · Pyramid of Menkaure

**Inside a pyramid (included).** You go inside the pyramid of Khafre or Menkaure, subject to site rules and availability on the day.

**The Great Sphinx**
Carved under Khafre from a knot of rock the quarrymen left behind: about 73 metres long and 20 high. And no, Napoleon’s gunners didn’t take the nose; a medieval Arab writer recorded its deliberate removal centuries earlier. Your guide separates the history from the mythology.
*Sites:* Great Sphinx

**Optional extras, if you’ve chosen them**
- Inside the Great Pyramid of Khufu: extra ticket, subject to availability and site rules on the day
- A camel ride: extra cost. We work with handlers we know, whose animals are in good condition, and the ride is entirely your choice.

### Midday · Lunch

**Lunch at a local restaurant (included)**
Unfussy Egyptian food: kushari, grilled kofta or slow-cooked ful. Drinks aren’t included.

### Afternoon · Below the Citadel and the bazaar

**Sultan Hassan and al-Rifa’i**
Sultan Hassan, begun in 1356, is one of the great mosques of the Islamic world, its four vaulted halls for the four Sunni schools of law set around a vast courtyard. Opposite, al-Rifa’i mirrors its Mamluk style but was built between 1869 and 1912, as a royal mausoleum; King Farouk and the last Shah of Iran are buried here. Five and a half centuries apart, across one street.
*Sites:* Sultan Hassan Mosque-Madrasa · Al-Rifa’i Mosque

**Khan El Khalili**
A working market since the 14th century, now led by the tourist trade: gold and silver, blown glass, spices, papyrus, brass and plenty of mass-produced souvenirs, which you’ll soon learn to tell apart. Al-Fishawy coffeehouse is worth a short detour.
*Sites:* Khan El Khalili · Al-Fishawy

## Is this day for you?

**A good fit if you:**
- are in Cairo for a short time and want the essential landmarks done properly
- want context, not a coach-tour gallop

**Worth knowing:**
- Six to seven hours on your feet. The plateau is open and shadeless; in late spring the heat is serious.
- The pyramid passages are narrow, steep and warm: exciting for some, claustrophobic for others.
- The mosques ask for modest dress.
- This day doesn’t include the Grand Egyptian Museum.
- The Citadel itself isn’t part of this day: the two mosques stand in the square below it.
