## Opening

At night, Philae seems to float: pylons and colonnades lit over dark water on the island where the temple was rebuilt, stone by stone, after Lake Nasser threatened to drown it. The Sound and Light Show here isn’t the most sophisticated in Egypt, but it may be the most atmospheric. The setting does most of the work.

---

## Why the day runs this way

You arrive as the last daylight leaves the sky, with just enough dusk to read the reliefs before the lights take over. A short drive and a short boat crossing get you there; the show itself is shared with the evening’s audience. It sits gently at the end of a day, and it changes register if you’ve walked the island by daylight first.

## Your day

### Evening · Agilkia Island

**Pickup and the boat**
Your driver collects you as the afternoon light fades. From the landing, a small private motor boat crosses calm water to the island, its lit outline ahead.

**The temple at dusk**
Through the first pylon, 18 metres high, carved with Ptolemy XII striking his enemies, into the temple of Isis: the birth house to one side, the sanctuary ahead and, on the water’s edge, the roofless colonnade of Trajan’s Kiosk. Pick up a translation headset in your language before the show begins.
*Sites:* Philae Temple · Trajan’s Kiosk

**The show**
About 45 to 55 minutes: the story of Isis and Osiris, his murder by Set, her search, his resurrection and the birth of Horus, told with narration, music and light moving across the stone.

**Back to your hotel**
By boat and car.

## Is this day for you?

**A good fit if you:**
- have seen Philae by day and want it in another light
- have one free evening in Aswan, or a cruise night there

**Worth knowing:**
- **In winter, it’s cold.** From October to March, evenings on the water and on the open island are genuinely cold, colder than most visitors expect in Egypt. Bring a warm jacket, and a scarf if you feel the cold.
- The show is dramatised myth rather than archaeology, and a little operatic.
- It isn’t recommended for children.
- There’s no demanding walking beyond the temple’s uneven paving.
- There’s no meal on this evening: it’s the boat, the temple and the show.
