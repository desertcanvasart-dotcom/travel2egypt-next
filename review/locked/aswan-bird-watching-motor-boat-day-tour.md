## Opening

At Aswan the Nile is wide, dotted with islands and broken by the granite of the First Cataract, and it sits on one of Africa’s great bird flyways. This private dawn trip takes you out by boat with a specialist birder, while the water is still and the city asleep, on one of two very different waters: the river channels between the islands, or the sheltered lake around Philae. You don’t need to be an ornithologist, only willing to get up before dawn.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The hour between first light and full dawn is when birds are most active, and before the feluccas and cargo boats get moving, the water has a stillness it soon loses. So the boat leaves before sunrise and comes back as the heat builds. The two waters suit different birds. The Nile among the islands means reedbeds, sandy shores and quiet backwaters, with the river’s residents close at hand. The lake around Philae is open, calm water, with more ducks, grebes and the larger waterbirds. Season changes everything: the migrations, peaking in October and in March and April, bring the biggest numbers.

## Your morning

### Before sunrise · To the water

**Pickup from your hotel**
Before sunrise, a private car takes you to your boat: the Nile waterfront in Aswan, or the landing for the lake at Philae.

### Early morning · Choose your water

**The Nile among the islands**
Through the channels around Elephantine, Kitchener’s Island and the small, empty outcrops further south, past reedbeds, sandy shores and quiet lagoons. Kitchener’s Island, planted with trees and flowering shrubs in the late 19th century, is a reliable resting place for tired migrants; your guide knows which trees are busy and how to approach without disturbing them. Here the river’s residents are the point: pied and white-throated kingfishers over the shallows, Nile Valley sunbirds, African green bee-eaters, the African swamphen and the purple heron in the reeds, the little heron at the water’s edge, Senegal thick-knees and spur-winged lapwings on the sandbanks, and the clamorous reed warbler, more often heard than seen. On calm backwaters, look for the ferruginous duck, a near-threatened species. In season, white-winged terns, wagtails, warblers and waders.
*Sites:* Elephantine Island · Kitchener’s Island (Botanical Garden), from the water

**Or the lake around Philae**
The calm water surrounding Philae Island, open and sheltered, with the temple of Isis on its island as your backdrop. A different set of birds: Egyptian geese and tufted ducks, black-necked grebes, Eurasian spoonbills and, depending on the season, great white pelicans; overhead, ospreys, black kites and brown-necked ravens; and white-throated kingfishers along the edges.
*Sites:* the lake around Philae Island, from the water

**With your guide**
Your guide is a specialist birder who knows these waters: where each species feeds and roosts, and how to approach without disturbing them. Binoculars are provided. Your guide also explains why the birds follow the Nile, and where birds like the ibis and the hoopoe sit in Egyptian belief.

### Mid-morning · Back to your hotel

**Back to the landing**
As the sun rises fully, the boat returns and a private car takes you to your hotel.

## Is this day for you?

**A good fit if you:**
- are a birder chasing the migration peaks, in October or March–April
- want a quiet dawn on the water before the heat
- want to see Aswan’s resident birds, whatever the season

**Worth knowing:**
- The start is before dawn.
- What you see depends on the season and on the water you choose: the Nile among the islands for the river’s residents, the lake around Philae for ducks, grebes and the larger waterbirds. Midsummer is quieter.
- The lake around Philae is at its best in winter: most of its pelicans, grebes, ducks and spoonbills are winter visitors.
- Your guide speaks English. If you’d like translation into another language, ask when you book.
- No walking beyond stepping into the boat. The patient pace suits older children better than restless young ones.
- Wear muted colours.
