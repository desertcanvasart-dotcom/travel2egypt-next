## Opening

Egypt is not a country most travellers think of as a birdwatching destination. Yet the same geography that produced the pyramids, a narrow river valley between two deserts on the flyway between Africa and Eurasia, brings hundreds of species through each year. This private journey puts the birds first, with dawn sessions at Lake Qarun in the Fayoum, the salt pools of Wadi El Natrun and the First Cataract at Aswan, and the monuments of the same valley in the hours between.

---

## Why this route works

Birds are best at first light and the monuments hold up well later in the day, so the two share the hours without competing. A naturalist travels with you throughout and an Egyptologist takes over at the temples. The route goes west into the Fayoum and north to Wadi El Natrun first, then flies south and works back north along the Nile, from Abu Simbel to Luxor, and ends in Cairo with the pyramids that came before Giza.

### Day 2 · Giza and Cairo
**The pyramids, the Grand Egyptian Museum and Khan el-Khalili**

The Giza plateau in the morning: the pyramids of Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure, built around 2500 BC, with a walk inside Khafre’s or Menkaure’s, the panorama, and the Sphinx with the Valley Temple below it. The afternoon at the Grand Egyptian Museum on the edge of the plateau, where Tutankhamun’s complete burial collection is shown together. The day ends in Khan el-Khalili, the bazaar trading since the late 14th century, before you return to your hotel.

**The day:** Giza pyramids · inside Khafre’s or Menkaure’s pyramid · Great Sphinx · Valley Temple · Grand Egyptian Museum · Khan el-Khalili
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel

### Day 3 · Cairo → Meidum → Fayoum
**The collapsed pyramid, the potters of Tunis and Lake Qarun at dusk**

South to Meidum, a pyramid usually attributed to Sneferu, around 2600 BC, partly collapsed in antiquity and now rising from a mound of its own debris above the Fayoum plain. Then through the pottery villages of Nazla and Tunis, where hand-thrown ceramics are still a working trade. In the late afternoon, Lake Qarun, a salt lake left from the much larger lake that filled the Fayoum in pharaonic times. Your naturalist works the shore at dusk: herons, egrets, terns over the water and, in winter, greater flamingos.

**The day:** Meidum Pyramid · Nazla · Tunis pottery village · Lake Qarun at dusk
**Overnight:** Tzila Lodge, Fayoum
**Meals:** Breakfast · dinner
**Travel:** Private car, Cairo → Meidum → Fayoum

### Day 4 · Fayoum → Wadi El Natrun → Cairo
**Dawn at the lake, then the salt pools and the monastery**

The first dedicated session, at the lake edge at first light: grey, purple and squacco herons, little and great egrets, pied and common kingfishers along the channels, and greater painted-snipe in the marshy ground. After breakfast, north to Wadi El Natrun, the desert depression whose natron deposits the embalmers used for mummification. Its saline pools hold flamingos, avocets and black-winged stilts, with larks and wheatears between them. The Monastery of Saint Macarius, founded in the 4th century and still a living Coptic community. Lunch on the way, then Cairo.

**The day:** Lake Qarun at dawn · Wadi El Natrun salt pools · Monastery of Saint Macarius
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch
**Travel:** Private car, Fayoum → Wadi El Natrun → Cairo

### Day 5 · Cairo → Abu Simbel → Aswan
**The temples of Ramses II, Nubian lunch and a felucca at the cataract**

An early flight south to Abu Simbel: Ramses II’s two cliff temples of the 13th century BC, the four seated figures at the Great Temple about twenty metres high. Between 1964 and 1968 they were cut into blocks and rebuilt 65 metres higher, out of reach of Lake Nasser. On to Aswan for a Nubian lunch, the High Dam, completed in 1970, and the Unfinished Obelisk, cracked in its quarry. At the end of the day, a felucca among the granite islands, your naturalist watching the banks.

**The day:** Abu Simbel · Aswan High Dam · Unfinished Obelisk · felucca at the First Cataract
**Overnight:** Aswan hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch
**Travel:** Domestic flights Cairo → Abu Simbel → Aswan (not included) · private transfers · felucca

### Day 6 · Aswan → Kom Ombo → Edfu → Luxor
**Dawn at the cataract, then two river temples**

The trip’s main birdwatching morning, by boat at first light among the islands of the First Cataract. Pied and white-throated kingfishers over the shallows, Senegal thick-knees and spur-winged lapwings on the sandbanks, purple herons in the reeds, and ospreys, marsh harriers and black kites over the river. Then north by road: Kom Ombo, its plan doubled for Sobek and Horus the Elder, and Edfu’s Temple of Horus, roof, halls and sanctuary standing as finished in the 1st century BC. Luxor by evening.

**The day:** Dawn boat at the First Cataract · Temple of Kom Ombo · Crocodile Museum · Temple of Horus at Edfu
**Overnight:** Luxor hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast
**Travel:** Private boat · private car, Aswan → Kom Ombo → Edfu → Luxor

### Day 7 · Luxor
**Luxor Temple, the Valley of the Kings and Hatshepsut**

One full day in ancient Thebes, which is not enough but is what nine days allow. Luxor Temple first, begun by Amenhotep III and extended by Ramses II, whose pylon carries the Battle of Kadesh. Then the West Bank: the Valley of the Kings, more than sixty royal tombs, three of them on your ticket; Hatshepsut’s colonnaded terraces at Deir el-Bahari; and the Colossi of Memnon, two seated figures of Amenhotep III about 18 metres tall. Lunch on the West Bank, and the late afternoon free for the river.

**The day:** Luxor Temple · Valley of the Kings (three tombs) · Temple of Hatshepsut · Colossi of Memnon
**Overnight:** Luxor hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch
**Travel:** Private transfers

**Optional on this day:**

### Day 8 · Luxor → Cairo
**Memphis, Saqqara and Dahshur, and the Nile after dark**

An early flight north, and a day for the pyramids before Giza. Memphis, the first capital of a united Egypt around 3100 BC, with the fallen colossus of Ramses II and an alabaster sphinx. Saqqara, where the Step Pyramid of Djoser, built around 2650 BC, shows the experiment that made Giza possible, with the Imhotep Museum and a nobles’ tomb. Dahshur, where Sneferu’s Bent and Red Pyramids show the builders correcting their angles. The evening closes with a dinner cruise on the Nile.

**The day:** Memphis · Saqqara · Imhotep Museum · a nobles’ tomb · Step Pyramid of Djoser · Bent Pyramid · Red Pyramid · dinner cruise on the Nile
**Overnight:** Cairo hotel
**Meals:** Breakfast · lunch · dinner (on the dinner cruise)
**Travel:** Domestic flight Luxor → Cairo (not included) · private transfers
**Pace:** An early flight and a full day
