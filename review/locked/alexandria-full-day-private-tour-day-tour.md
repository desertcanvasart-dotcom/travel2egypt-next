## Opening

Alexandria isn’t Cairo. Built by Greeks, enlarged by Romans, sustained by Byzantine and Arab dynasties and remade by 19th-century cosmopolitans, it’s a Mediterranean port with an Egyptian accent. This private day moves through those layers of history, from the catacombs to the modern Bibliotheca, with a proper lunch by the sea and a walk along the Corniche. Its theme is synthesis: at every stop, cultures that should have clashed share the same stone.

---

## Why the day runs this way

The day starts underground, in the catacombs, and works its way up and out to the sea: the Roman theatre, the city’s great museum, lunch, Pompey’s Pillar, the Bibliotheca Alexandrina, Qaitbey on the harbour and, last, the Corniche, unstructured, to let the day settle. It’s a full day, and the city rewards lingering, so we can drop a stop to slow the others down, or stretch lunch by the sea. Whether you start from a hotel or a cruise ship, you’re brought back to it at the end.

## Your day

### Morning · Underground and the old city

**Pickup from your hotel or your ship**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you in a private air-conditioned car, from your hotel in Alexandria or at the port if you’re arriving on a Mediterranean cruise.

**The Catacombs of Kom El Shuqafa**
Cut into the bedrock in the 2nd century AD: Roman chambers decorated with Egyptian gods in Greek sculptural style, Anubis in a Roman cuirass, Sobek in Hellenistic robes. A deliberate visual language for a mixed community of the dead. The Hall of Caracalla adds a darker note. Forty-five minutes to an hour.
*Sites:* Catacombs of Kom El Shuqafa

**The Roman Theatre, Kom El Dikka**
Thirteen tiers of white marble, intimate in scale: an auditorium for lectures and music rather than spectacle. Around it, excavated Roman houses and baths, with mosaic floors, show everyday life in late-antique Alexandria.
*Sites:* Kom El Dikka Roman Theatre

**The Greco-Roman Museum**
Reopened in 2023 after a long restoration, with a rich collection from about 300 BC to the 7th century AD: portrait sculpture, terracottas, papyri, coins and carved sarcophagi. Your guide checks what’s on show before you arrive.
*Sites:* Greco-Roman Museum

### Midday · Lunch by the sea

**Lunch at a local seafood restaurant (included)**
Eat as Alexandrians do: fish and seafood, simply cooked, whatever the morning’s catch allowed. Drinks aren’t included.

### Afternoon · The Serapeum, the Bibliotheca, the harbour and the Corniche

**Pompey’s Pillar and the Serapeum**
The Serapeum was the great temple of Serapis, a god invented under Ptolemy I to bridge Egyptian and Greek belief; it was destroyed in 391 AD. From its platform rises Pompey’s Pillar, which has nothing to do with Pompey: a single shaft of red Aswan granite, about 27 metres tall, raised in 297 AD for Diocletian. The view across the rooftops is one of the city’s unsung ones.
*Sites:* Pompey’s Pillar · Serapeum

**The Bibliotheca Alexandrina**
The modern library, opened in 2002 in homage to the ancient Library of Alexandria: a vast tilted disc facing the sea, its walls carved with letters from the world’s scripts, and inside, a reading room that steps down in terraces beneath the sloping roof.
*Sites:* Bibliotheca Alexandrina

**Qaitbey Citadel**
Built in 1477 on the tip of Pharos, on the site of the ancient lighthouse, one of the Seven Wonders, felled by earthquakes; look for its blocks in the walls. You go inside the fortress, through its halls and up to the ramparts, with the Mediterranean on three sides.
*Sites:* Qaitbey Citadel

**The Corniche**
An unhurried walk along the Eastern Harbour, the curve of bay Alexander’s planners first laid out.
*Sites:* Alexandria Corniche · Eastern Harbour
