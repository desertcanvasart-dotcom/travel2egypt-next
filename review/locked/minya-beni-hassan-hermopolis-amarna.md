## Opening

About 250 kilometres south of Cairo lies a stretch of the Nile most itineraries skip, and that's a real loss. Minya holds the painted tombs of Beni Hassan; Amarna, the capital Akhenaten built for his one god and his successors abandoned; the city of Thoth at Hermopolis; and its necropolis at Tuna el-Gebel. None is as famous as Luxor. All are quieter, easier to read, and in some ways more surprising.

---

## Why the day runs this way

Three hours of driving before the first site means an early start, to reach the tombs by mid-morning, before the midday heat settles into the limestone. The river sets the order: Beni Hassan and Amarna are both on the east bank, so you see them first, then cross to the west bank after lunch for Hermopolis and Tuna el-Gebel, which lie close together. One crossing each way, and no time lost going back and forth. The road itself is part of it: sugar cane, clover, irrigation canals and villages where the minarets are the tallest things in sight.

## Your day

### Morning · The east bank: Beni Hassan and Amarna

**Pickup from your hotel**
Your driver and Egyptologist collect you early in a private air-conditioned car for the three-hour drive south.

**The tombs of Beni Hassan**
Cut into the cliff above the Nile's east bank: thirty-nine tombs of the Middle Kingdom's regional governors, from about 2000 BC, several of them open. Their walls show wrestlers, hunts, farm work and processions of foreigners, in colours kept unusually fresh by the dry cliff, and read less like religious texts than illustrated records of a provincial life.
*Sites:* Beni Hassan tombs

**Tell el-Amarna**
Around 1346 BC, Akhenaten left Thebes, set aside the old gods for the sun disc Aten alone and built a new capital, Akhetaten, on empty ground. He ruled here for about seventeen years. After his death, his successors went back to Thebes and tried to erase his name, and the city was never lived in again. In the hills, his officials' tombs show the royal family beneath the Aten's rays; in the plain lie the outlines of palaces and temples.
*Sites:* Tell el-Amarna · Tombs of the Nobles

### Afternoon · The west bank: Hermopolis and Tuna el-Gebel

**Hermopolis**
Ancient Khmun, the city of Thoth, god of writing and wisdom, whom the Greeks after 332 BC took as their Hermes. Little stands now; the clearest remains are two colossal baboons, Thoth's sacred animal, from the reign of Amenhotep III.
*Sites:* Hermopolis (el-Ashmunein)

**Tuna el-Gebel**
The necropolis of Hermopolis, with streets of tombs, catacombs of sacred ibises and baboons, and the tomb of the priest Petosiris, from around 300 BC, which blends Egyptian and Greek art.
*Sites:* Tuna el-Gebel · Tomb of Petosiris

## Is this day for you?

**A good fit if you:**
- have done Cairo, Luxor and Aswan and want the chapters the usual circuit skips
- are curious about Pharaonic history; a good guide turns these sites from old stones into a story

**Worth knowing:**
- About six of the twelve hours are on the road.
- You may pass routine security checkpoints along the way. They're there for travellers' safety and are nothing to worry about.
- The sites are open and the sun is strong. Comfortable shoes, sun protection and water are essentials.
- Much of what was found at Amarna is now in museums; the site itself is the landscape, the city plan and the tombs.
